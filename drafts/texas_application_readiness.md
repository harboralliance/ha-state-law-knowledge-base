# Texas Application Readiness

## Overview
The Office of the Attorney General (OAG) in Texas requires human trafficking training for employees in commercial lodging establishments as part of a broader effort to combat human trafficking. The Harbor Alliance must prepare to comply with this requirement by providing appropriate training and ensuring all necessary materials are available.

## Requirements for Training
### Materials Needed
1. **Training Videos**: Office of the Attorney General has approved several training videos that meet the requirements.
2. **Anti-Trafficking Signage**: Employees must be able to access anti-trafficking signage at their workplace.
3. **Training Program Documentation**: A comprehensive program outlining the curriculum, delivery method, and frequency of training.

### Standards to Meet
1. **Annual Training for Large Establishments**: Employees in commercial lodging establishments with 10 or more rooms must receive annual training.
2. **All Employees Training**: All employees at any size of lodging establishment are required to complete the training.
3. **Training Content**: The training must cover identifying and responding to human trafficking, including recognizing signs of trafficking, reporting suspected incidents, and understanding anti-trafficking signage requirements.

### Review Risks/Gaps
1. **Compliance Verification**: Ensure that all employees have completed the required annual training by maintaining a record of training completion.
2. **Signage Placement**: Verify that anti-trafficking signage is prominently displayed in all lodging establishments.
3. **Resource Availability**: Confirm that the necessary training materials and resources are readily accessible to all employees.

## Next Steps
1. **Access Training Materials**: Obtain the approved training videos from the Office of the Attorney General.
2. **Develop Training Program**: Create a comprehensive training program that includes the required curriculum, delivery method, and frequency of training.
3. **Signage Procurement**: Order and ensure proper placement of anti-trafficking signage in all lodging establishments.
4. **Employee Communication**: Inform all employees about the upcoming training requirements and provide them with necessary resources.

## Conclusion
The Harbor Alliance must take proactive steps to ensure compliance with the Texas OAG's human trafficking training requirement for commercial lodging establishments. By focusing on acquiring the necessary materials, developing a comprehensive training program, ensuring signage placement, and communicating clearly with employees, the Harbor Alliance can meet the requirements and demonstrate its commitment to combating human trafficking in Texas.
