### Summary of California's Response to Human Trafficking

California is committed to combating human trafficking through both criminal and civil law enforcement. Here’s a detailed breakdown:

#### Civil Law Enforcement
1. **Human Trafficking Awareness Training**: Businesses such as hotels, motels, and bed and breakfasts are required by law to provide training on human trafficking awareness to their employees who may interact with victims. This training must be completed by January 1, 2020, for current employees, and within six months for new hires.

#### Criminal Law Enforcement
1. **CRD (California Human Trafficking Task Force)**: CRD enforces California’s human trafficking civil rights law and investigates complaints of human trafficking.
  
2. **Support for Survivors**: Civil remedies may be available to survivors of human trafficking. For more detailed information, you can review the document linked on their website.

#### Hotlines and Resources
1. **National Human Trafficking Resource Center (NHTRC)**: Call 1-888-373-7888 or text "Be Free" or 233733.
2. **California Coalition to Abolish Slavery and Trafficking (CAST)**: Call 1-888-KEY-2FRE(EDOM) or 1-888-539-2373.

#### Criminal Law Enforcement Options for Survivors
For more information on criminal law enforcement options, you can review the webpages linked on the California Attorney General’s website and the U.S. Department of Justice Human Trafficking Prosecution Unit website.

### Additional Information

1. **Model Notice**: SB 970 requires hotels and motels to post a notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking about hotline numbers to seek help or report unlawful activity.
  
2. **Posting Mandates**: The mandated notices must be posted in English, Spanish, and an additional language if the business is located in certain counties covered by federal language assistance provisions.

### Conclusion
California takes human trafficking seriously and provides various resources for victims, including training for employees in the hospitality industry and hotlines for immediate assistance. For further details, you can visit the State of California’s Department of Justice website or contact their designated offices.
