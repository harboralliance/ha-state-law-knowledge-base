## Summary

The California Department of Justice and other relevant agencies have enacted laws requiring businesses, including motels and hotels, to post a notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking about hotline numbers for assistance. Specifically:

1. **Hotel and Motel Training Requirement**: Senate Bill 970 (SB 970) requires hotel and motel employers to provide at least 20 minutes of training to employees who are likely to interact with victims of human trafficking by January 1, 2020.

2. **Posting Requirement**: Section 52.6 of the Civil Code (Senate Bill 1193) mandates that specified businesses, including hotels and motels, post a notice informing the public about hotline numbers for assistance and support in case of human trafficking incidents. This includes posting in English and Spanish, as well as additional languages depending on the county.

## Key Points

### Training Requirement (SB 970)
- **Effective Date**: January 1, 2020
- **Employees Covered**: Employees likely to interact with victims of human trafficking
- **Training Content**: At least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training regarding human trafficking awareness
- **Existing Training**: Employers who provided training on or before January 1, 2019, are not required to provide additional training

### Posting Requirement (Senate Bill 1193)
- **Effective Date**: April 1, 2013
- **Businesses Covered**: Hotels and motels
- **Languages Required**:
  - English
  - Spanish
  - Additional languages if the business is located in counties covered by language assistance provisions of the federal Voting Rights Act

## Compliance Guidance

### Training
To comply with the training requirement, employers should:

1. Develop a training program that covers the basics of human trafficking.
2. Ensure the training is delivered within the required timeframe (at least 20 minutes).
3. Keep records of the training provided to employees.

### Posting
To meet the posting requirements, businesses must:

1. Obtain and post the appropriate model notice in English, Spanish, and any additional languages as required.
2. Verify that the notice complies with all language and formatting requirements.
3. Ensure the notice is prominently displayed in areas accessible to potential customers and employees.

### Resources
- **Model Notices**: Available on the California Department of Justice website (https://oag.ca.gov/human-trafficking/model-notice)
- **Hotline Numbers**: National Human Trafficking Resource Center: 1-888-373-7888 or TEXT: Be Free or 233733
- **Additional Support**: Information on criminal law enforcement options for trafficking survivors can be found on the State of California Attorney General website and United States Department of Justice Human Trafficking Prosecution Unit website.

By following these guidelines, businesses can ensure compliance with the requirements and provide necessary support to potential victims of human trafficking.
