This Illinois Act requires employers, lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide their employees with training on the recognition of human trafficking and protocols for reporting such incidents. The training must cover identifying individuals at risk, distinguishing labor from sex trafficking in these industries, and outlining employee roles in reporting and responding to human trafficking.

The training is mandatory for new hires within 6 months and must be completed every 2 years thereafter by continuing employees. It should last at least 20 minutes.

Employers can use either their own training program or a third-party one that meets the Act's requirements, which include definitions of human trafficking, identification guidelines, distinctions between labor and sex trafficking for lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops, and employee roles in reporting and responding to such incidents.

If an employer fails to comply within 30 days after receiving notice, a local government unit or law enforcement agency can impose a civil penalty not exceeding $1,500 per offense. The Attorney General or State's Attorney can also bring a civil action against the employer for non-compliance, which is considered a business offense.

This Act takes effect on January 1, 2026.
