The bill introduces a new law that requires employers, including lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops, to provide their employees with training in recognizing human trafficking and protocols for reporting it to the appropriate authority. The training must be completed within 6 months of employment and every 2 years thereafter. Employers may use their own human trafficking training program or a third-party program that includes a definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children, guidance on how to identify individuals most at risk for human trafficking, the difference between labor and sex trafficking as it relates to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops, and guidance on the role of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking. If an employer fails to comply with this law, they may be subject to a civil penalty of up to $1,500 for each offense.
