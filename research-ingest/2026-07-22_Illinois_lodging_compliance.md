The bill being discussed is designed to address human trafficking within the hospitality industry in Illinois. Specifically, it requires that all employees at lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops receive training on recognizing signs of human trafficking and how to report suspected cases.

### Key Points:

1. **Effective Date**: The legislation becomes effective January 1, 2026.
  
2. **Training Requirements**:
   - Employees must complete a training program within six months of starting their role and every two years thereafter.
   - The training must be at least 20 minutes in length and cover topics such as human trafficking definitions, identifying individuals at risk, the difference between labor and sex trafficking, and reporting procedures.

3. **Training Curricula**:
   - Employers can either use their own training program or adopt a third-party one that meets specific criteria.
   - The Department of Labor must develop an approved curriculum for establishments that do not provide their own program. This curriculum must include the same core topics as the employer-curated ones.

4. **Penalties**:
   - If an employee is found to have violated the law, they may face a civil penalty of up to $1,500 per offense.
   - Local government units or law enforcement agencies can monitor compliance and issue reasonable notices to employers who do not correct violations within 30 days.

### Purpose:

The primary goal of this legislation is to increase awareness among hospitality workers about the signs of human trafficking. By ensuring that these employees are well-equipped to recognize potential victims and report suspicious activities, the bill aims to reduce the incidence of human trafficking within the hospitality industry.

This measure reflects a proactive approach in combating human trafficking, leveraging the existing networks and presence of hospitality workers who interact with the public frequently.
