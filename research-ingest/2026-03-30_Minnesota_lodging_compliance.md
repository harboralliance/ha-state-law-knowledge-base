### Minnesota Sex Trafficking Prevention Training for Hotel and Motel Staff

#### Overview:
The Minnesota law requires all hotel, motel, and short-term rental staff to complete a sex trafficking prevention and response training program. This training is designed to help these staff recognize the signs of sex trafficking and understand how to respond appropriately.

#### Key Points:

1. **Who Needs Training:**
   - All owners, managers, and on-site employees must complete the training.
   - New employees must take this training within 90 days of hire.
   - Existing staff must retake the training annually.

2. **Displaying the Poster:**
   - A required back-of-house poster must be displayed in a location all staff will see it.

3. **Training Content:**
   - The training covers identifying indicators of sex trafficking, understanding the legal requirements, and knowing how to respond if trafficking is suspected or observed.
   - It includes a virtual presentation with guided exercises, a companion guide, a manager/owner toolkit, and a poster set.

4. **Exceptions:**
   - Employees under 16 (unless they clean rooms), employees who work exclusively in restaurants providing catering services, and those with no direct contact with guests or guest rooms may be exempt.
   - However, MDH encourages training for all employees.

5. **Training Resources:**
   - The training is available in multiple languages including English, Spanish, Somali, and Hmong.
   - Managers and owners can access the training through the Minnesota Department of Health (MDH) Learning Center.
   - Employees can access the training in their preferred language.

6. **Compliance Costs:**
   - MDH does not charge a fee for the mandatory training package.
   - However, licensees are responsible for paying any additional costs such as printing posters.

7. **Training Schedule:**
   - The virtual presentation and companion guide will be offered in Spanish, Hmong, and Somali starting late fall 2023/early 2024.

8. **Questions and Assistance:**
   - For questions or assistance accessing the training, contact `health.safeharbor@state.mn.us`.
   - Contact MDH at `health.foodlodging@state.mn.us` or 651-201-4500 if unsure about licensing.

#### Conclusion:
The Minnesota law requires comprehensive sex trafficking prevention and response training for all hotel, motel, and short-term rental staff. By complying with this requirement, these establishments can help prevent and respond to sex trafficking, thereby protecting the well-being of their guests and employees.
