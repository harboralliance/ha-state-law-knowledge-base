The Illinois Human Trafficking Prevention Act, commonly known as the "SB 1829," became law in 2019 and requires certain employers, such as lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops, to provide their employees with training on recognizing human trafficking and reporting it to the appropriate authorities. The purpose of this act is to increase awareness and prevent human trafficking.

Here are some key points about the Act:

1. **Definitions**: The Act defines "human trafficking" as the deprivation or violation of a person's liberty with the intent to obtain forced labor, commercial sex, or other exploitation. It also includes definitions for lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.

2. **Training Requirement**: Effective June 1, 2020, employers must provide their employees with training on recognizing human trafficking and reporting it. The training must cover:
   - A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
   - Guidance on how to identify individuals who are most at risk for human trafficking.
   - Differences between human trafficking for labor and sex purposes in the context of lodging establishments, restaurants, or truck stops.
   - Guidance on the role of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

3. **Training Curriculum**: The Department of Human Services is responsible for developing a curriculum for an approved human trafficking training program that must be used by employers who do not develop their own program. This curriculum must include all the topics covered in the employer-provided training.

4. **Penalties**: Employers who fail to comply with the training requirement or who fail to correct any violations within 30 days after receiving notice may face penalties of up to $1,500 for each offense.

The Act's goal is to help employers identify and respond appropriately to signs of human trafficking among their employees and customers, thereby preventing potential instances of trafficking.
