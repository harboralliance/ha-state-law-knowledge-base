### Overview

Human trafficking is a serious crime that involves the exploitation and control of individuals through force, coercion, or deception. California has enacted laws to combat human trafficking, and various organizations are involved in enforcing and supporting survivors.

### Key Points from the Sources:

1. **California's Anti-Trafficking Laws:**
   - California has comprehensive anti-trafficking laws, including CRD (California Respect for Democracy).
   - CRD enforces California’s human trafficking civil rights law.
   - Individuals can file complaints with CRD regarding human trafficking.

2. **Criminal and Civil Remedies:**
   - Survivors of human trafficking may be eligible for civil remedies.
   - For more information, you can review the "Human Trafficking FAQs" document provided by CRD.

3. **Support for Survivors:**
   - If in immediate danger, call 911 or contact a 24-hour, toll-free hotline.
   - National Human Trafficking Resource Center (1-888-373-7888) and California Coalition to Abolish Slavery and Trafficking (CAST) are available for assistance.

4. **Training Requirements:**
   - Under SB 970, hotel and motel employers must provide 20 minutes of training on human trafficking awareness to employees likely to interact with victims.
   - The training is required within six months for new employees as well.
   - Employers who have provided the training before January 1, 2019, are exempt from additional training.

5. **Model Notice:**
   - Businesses and establishments must post a model notice informing the public about human trafficking hotline numbers.
   - The mandated notice is available for download on various languages including English, Spanish, and other minority languages.

### Additional Resources:

- **National Human Trafficking Hotline:** Call (888) 373-7888, Text: Be Free
- **21st Century Policing Program:** For information on 21st century policing strategies and initiatives.

### Conclusion

Human trafficking is a pervasive issue that requires the involvement of various stakeholders. California's laws provide resources for survivors and enforcement agencies to combat this crime. Employees in specific industries, such as hotels and motels, are required to undergo training to recognize and respond appropriately to human trafficking incidents. For support, victims can contact designated hotlines or consult with legal organizations specializing in anti-trafficking efforts.
