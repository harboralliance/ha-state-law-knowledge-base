### Summary of Key Points from the Legislation (SB1422)

**Title**: Strengthening Human Trafficking Protections in Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops

**Effective Date**: January 1, 2026

**Key Provisions:**

1. **Definitions**:
   - "Department" refers to the Department of Human Services.
   - "Employee" includes individuals who have recurring interactions with the public in lodging establishments, restaurants, or truck stops.
   - "Employer" is defined as a person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
   - "Human trafficking" involves depriving or violating personal liberty for forced labor or services, commercial sex, or exploitation.

2. **Training Requirements**:
   - **Implementation**: Starting June 1, 2020, employers must provide training in human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols to their employees who have recurring interactions with the public.
   - **Frequency**: Employees must complete initial training within 6 months of employment and every 2 years thereafter if still employed by the same employer.
   - **Duration**: The training must be at least 20 minutes long.

3. **Training Curriculum**:
   - Employers may use their own program or a third-party program that includes:
     1. Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
     2. Guidance on identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
     3. Differences between labor and sex trafficking in the context of lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
     4. Roles of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

   - The Department will develop a standardized curriculum that includes the above requirements. This curriculum must be used by employers not administering their own program.

4. **Penalties**:
   - Local government units and law enforcement agencies can monitor and enforce compliance with this act.
   - Upon discovering a violation, they must provide a reasonable notice of noncompliance to the employer. If the issue is not resolved within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer.
   - Violation of this act is a business offense punishable by a fine not exceeding $1,500 per offense.

### Context and Intent

The legislation aims to enhance awareness and response mechanisms in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to human trafficking. It requires employers to provide regular training to employees who interact with the public, ensuring they are equipped to identify potential cases of human trafficking and report them appropriately. This proactive approach is intended to protect vulnerable individuals and hold employers accountable for failing to do so.

### Impact

- **Enhanced Awareness**: Increases knowledge among staff on recognizing signs of human trafficking.
- **Reporting Mechanisms**: Provides a structured way to report incidents, potentially leading to earlier interventions.
- **Legal Enforcement**: Establishes penalties for non-compliance, ensuring accountability and deterrence.
- **Community Protection**: Protects individuals who might be trafficked by ensuring businesses are vigilant.

### Next Steps

The legislation is effective starting in 2026. It will require the development and implementation of a standardized human trafficking training program by the Department of Human Services. Stakeholders, including employers, employees, and law enforcement, should prepare for the changes to ensure compliance and maximize effectiveness in combating human trafficking.
