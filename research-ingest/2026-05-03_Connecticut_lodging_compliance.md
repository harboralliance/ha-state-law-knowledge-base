# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has enacted a series of laws to combat human trafficking, including both sex and labor trafficking. The state provides various trainings for different sectors such as DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, youth, and the community. The training programs aim to educate participants on identifying victims, understanding laws, recognizing warning signs, and providing appropriate responses.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable Industry**: DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, youth, and the community.
- **Training Required**: Yes, mandatory for DCF staff annually, optional for others. Various training programs covering human trafficking definitions, legal requirements, warning signs, impacts, and victim support.
- **Signage Required**: Not explicitly mentioned in the sources.
- **Reporting Required**: Yes, state attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- **Enforcement Actions**: Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns, keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.
- **Victim Services**: DCF provides services to human trafficking victims classified as “uncared for” and offers specialized support.

## Enforcement and Legal Measures
- The state has enacted several laws aimed at expanding protections for victims of human trafficking and strengthening the crime of human trafficking.
- Laws require mandatory data reporting, statewide oversight, and monitoring body.
- Affirmative defense for minors in trafficking cases and specific allowances for crime victim compensation applications.

## Effective Date
The legislation was enacted starting from 2013 with ongoing updates to strengthen protections and response mechanisms against human trafficking.
