The text you provided discusses sex trafficking prevention and response training for hotel, motel, and short-term rental staff in Minnesota. The following points summarize the key requirements and details:

1. **Training Requirements:**
   - All owners, managers, and on-site employees must complete the required training.
   - New employees must take this training within 90 days of hire.
   - All required staff must retake this training every year.

2. **Displaying the Poster:**
   - The required back-of-house poster must be displayed in a location where all staff will see it.

3. **Ongoing Awareness Campaigns:**
   - An ongoing awareness campaign for employees is also required.

4. **Scope of Training:**
   - Most of the training package is required for all hotels, motels, and short-term rentals in Minnesota.
   - There are very few exceptions for only parts of the package.

5. **Training Content:**
   - The training covers signs of sex trafficking and how to respond effectively.
   - It is designed by the Minnesota Department of Health in partnership with Hospitality Minnesota.
   - The training includes a virtual presentation, a training companion guide, a manager/owner toolkit, and a poster set.

6. **Exceptions:**
   - Employees under 16 (unless they clean rooms) are exempt.
   - Employees working exclusively in restaurants or those with no direct contact with guests or guest rooms are also exempt.

7. **Language Availability:**
   - Training materials will be available in English, Spanish, Somali, and Hmong starting late fall 2023/early 2024.

8. **Costs:**
   - The Minnesota Department of Health does not charge a fee for the mandatory training package.
   - Licensees are required to pay the costs of compliance with poster printing, etc.

9. **Contact Information:**
   - For questions or assistance accessing the training, contact `health.safeharbor@state.mn.us`.

10. **Additional Resources:**
    - The text also mentions the Minnesota Human Trafficking Prevention and Response Network (MNHTPRN) for further resources and support.

This comprehensive approach aims to ensure that hotel, motel, and short-term rental staff are well-equipped to recognize and respond effectively to potential sex trafficking scenarios within their establishments.
