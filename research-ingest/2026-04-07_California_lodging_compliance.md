### Key Points from the Sources:

1. **Training Requirement for Hotel and Motel Employees**:
   - **SB 970** requires that, by January 1, 2020, hotel and motel employers provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training and education regarding human trafficking awareness to each employee who is likely to interact or come into contact with victims of human trafficking.
   - Employers must also provide such training to new employees within six months of their employment in that role. 
   - An employer who has provided this training before January 1, 2019, is not required to provide additional training.

2. **Model Notice for Human Trafficking Awareness**:
   - The California Attorney General's Office provides a model notice that businesses and establishments must post to inform the public and victims of human trafficking about hotline numbers they can use to seek help or report unlawful activity.
   - These notices must be posted in English and Spanish, and in additional languages in specified counties as required by federal law.

3. **Enforcement and Penalties**:
   - Failure to comply with the posting requirements can result in fines.
   - Each mandated business is required to post the notice in English and Spanish. For businesses located in specific counties that are subject to the language assistance provisions of the federal Voting Rights Act, posting in a third language may also be required.

4. **Resources for Victims**:
   - The National Human Trafficking Resource Center provides a hotline number (1-888-373-7888) and a text service (Be Free or 233733) where victims can seek help.
   - Victims are encouraged to call 911 if they are in immediate danger.

5. **Public Awareness Campaigns**:
   - Various government agencies, including the Department of Justice, continue to run public awareness campaigns about human trafficking to educate the public and raise awareness.

### Summary:

The State of California has enacted legislation (SB 970) mandating that hotel and motel employees receive training on human trafficking by a specific date. This training is essential for their role in recognizing and responding appropriately to victims of human trafficking. Accompanying this legislation, there are requirements for businesses to post notices informing the public about available resources for assistance in English and Spanish, with additional languages required in certain counties. Failure to comply can result in fines.
