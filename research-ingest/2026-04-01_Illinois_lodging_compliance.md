This Illinois law requires lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide their employees with training in recognizing human trafficking and protocols for reporting it. The training must be completed within 6 months of starting employment in the role and every two years thereafter if still employed by the employer. The training must include a definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children, guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking, differences between labor and sex trafficking as relates to lodging establishments, restaurants, or truck stops, and guidance on employees' roles in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

The Department of Human Services is responsible for developing a curriculum for an approved human trafficking training recognition program that must be used by lodging establishments, restaurants, or truck stops that do not administer their own program. The curriculum must include all of the required elements as outlined above.

If a unit of local government or law enforcement agency discovers a violation of this Act, they have the authority to provide reasonable notice of noncompliance and impose a civil penalty if the violation is not corrected within 30 days. If the violation persists after the cure period, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer.
