### Overview

This overview provides comprehensive information about human trafficking, including requirements for employee training and posting notices in specific establishments like motels and hotels.

### Requirements for Employee Training

#### California State Requirement (SB 970)

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2020

**Key Requirements:**
- **Training Duration:** At least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training.
- **Target Audience:** Employees who are likely to interact or come into contact with victims of human trafficking.
- **Timing:**
  - Existing employees as of July 1, 2019
  - New employees within six months of their start date

**Exceptions:**
- Employers who have provided this training on or before January 1, 2019 are not required to provide additional training.

**References:**
- **Source:** California Department of Justice (DOJ) | [Human Trafficking FAQs](https://oag.ca.gov/human-trafficking/model-notice)
- **URL:** [California's Human Trafficking Model Notice](https://oag.ca.gov/human-trafficking/model-notice)

### Posting Requirements

**Purpose:**
- Inform the public and victims of human trafficking about available telephone hotline numbers to seek help or report unlawful activity.

#### Required Businesses

- Hotels, motels, and bed and breakfast inns as of April 1, 2013 (SB 478).
- Additional requirements added with SB 225 and AB 260 (2017).

**Model Notice:**
- Available in English, Spanish, and additional languages.
- Businesses must post the notice in English and Spanish. In certain counties subject to federal language assistance provisions of the Voting Rights Act, a third language posting is required.

**Posting Mandates:**
- Language requirements
- Penalties for failure to post

**References:**
- **Source:** California Department of Justice (DOJ) | [Human Trafficking Model Notice](https://oag.ca.gov/human-trafficking/model-notice)
- **URL:** [California's Human Trafficking Model Notice](https://oag.ca.gov/human-trafficking/model-notice)

### Enforcement and Legal Consequences

Failure to comply with these requirements can result in fines or other legal consequences, as detailed by the California Attorney General’s Office. For specific penalties, please refer to the DOJ resources linked above.

### Conclusion

California's laws mandate comprehensive training for employees who may interact with victims of human trafficking and require specified businesses to post notices informing the public about available hotline numbers. These measures are critical in preventing and addressing human trafficking incidents within these establishments.
