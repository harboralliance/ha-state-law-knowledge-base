The bill aims to prevent human trafficking in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. It requires employers to provide their employees with training on identifying signs of human trafficking, reporting it to the appropriate authorities, and responding to instances of it. The training must be completed within six months of employment and every two years thereafter for those who are still employed. Employers can use their own human trafficking training program or that of a third party, as long as it includes certain minimum requirements. The Department of Human Services will develop a curriculum for an approved human trafficking recognition program, which will also be used by establishments that do not administer their own program. If a unit of local government or law enforcement agency discovers a violation of the act, they may provide the employer with a reasonable notice of noncompliance and subject them to a civil penalty if the violation is not corrected within 30 days. Employers who violate this act are guilty of a business offense and can be fined up to $1,500 for each offense.
