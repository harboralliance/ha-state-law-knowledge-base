**SB1422 - An Act to amend the Labor and Commerce Code to require human trafficking recognition training for certain employees**

**Section 5. Definitions**
- "Department" means the Department of Human Services.
- "Employee" means a person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- "Employer" means a person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- "Human trafficking" means depriving or violating another's personal liberty with intent to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell for commercial sex, or exploit in obscene matter.
- "Lodging establishment" includes hotels and motels under the 2017 North American Industry Classification System codes 721110 and 721120.
- "Restaurant" is a business primarily engaged in selling ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption comprising at least 51% of total sales (excluding liquor).
- "Truck stop" provides services to the trucking industry.

**Section 10. Human trafficking recognition training**
- Employers must provide this training by June 1, 2020, and every two years thereafter.
- Training must be completed within six months of employment and be at least 20 minutes long.

**Section 15. Human trafficking recognition training curriculum**
- Employers can use their own or a third-party program as long as it includes identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking, distinguishing labor from sex trafficking relevant to lodging, restaurants, and truck stops, and guidance on reporting and responding.
- The Department will develop an approved curriculum by July 1, 2020.

**Section 20. Penalties**
- Local governments or law enforcement can monitor and enforce compliance.
- If a violation is not corrected within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may sue the employer for a fine of up to $1,500 per offense.

**Effective Date: January 1, 2026**

This legislation requires employers in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide human trafficking recognition training to their employees, aiming to prevent and respond to potential incidents effectively.
