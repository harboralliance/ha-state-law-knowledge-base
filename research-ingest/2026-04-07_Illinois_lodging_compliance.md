**Title: Amended HB 1274: Human Trafficking Training for Lodging Establishment Employees**

**Summary:**
HB 1274 amends the **Human Trafficking Prevention Act** (P.A. 101-18) to require lodging establishment employees, including those working in restaurants and truck stops, to complete human trafficking recognition training. The bill also outlines penalties for non-compliance.

**Key Points:**

### Definitions:
- **Department:** The Department of Human Services.
- **Employee:** A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer:** A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** Deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty with intent to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell individuals for commercial sex, or exploit them in obscene matter.

### Human Trafficking Recognition Training:
- Effective June 1, 2020.
- Employees must complete training within 6 months after beginning employment and every 2 years thereafter if still employed.
- Training must be at least 20 minutes long and cover:
  1. Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
  2. Guidance on identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
  3. Difference between labor and sex trafficking as it relates to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
  4. Role of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

### Penalties:
- A unit of local government or law enforcement agency may monitor and enforce compliance with the Act.
- Upon discovery of a violation, they must provide reasonable notice to the employer.
- If the violation is not corrected within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney can bring a civil action against the employer.
- Violations are considered business offenses with a maximum fine of $1,500 per offense.

### Effective Date:
- The Act takes effect January 1, 2026.
