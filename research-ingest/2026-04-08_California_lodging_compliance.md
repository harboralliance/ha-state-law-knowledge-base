### Summary

**Harmonization Summary: Human Trafficking Awareness Training for Hotel and Motel Employees in California**

1. **Legal Requirement**: 
   - **Source**: California's `Senate Bill 970` (SB 970) requires hotel and motel employers to provide at least 20 minutes of training on human trafficking awareness to employees who interact with or are likely to come into contact with victims.
   
2. **Effective Date**: 
   - **Source**: The bill became law in September 2018, with compliance required by January 1, 2020.

3. **Training Content**:
   - **Source**: The training must cover human trafficking awareness and provide information on how to recognize, report, and respond to suspected cases.
   
4. **Revisions and Updates**:
   - No additional revisions or updates have been noted since the initial implementation of SB 970.

### Analysis

1. **Scope of Requirement**:
   - Applies specifically to hotel and motel employers.
   - Training is mandatory for employees who interact with or are likely to come into contact with victims of human trafficking.

2. **Training Duration and Format**:
   - Minimum training duration: 20 minutes.
   - Can be provided through classroom sessions, online courses, or other effective interactive methods.

3. **Compliance and Enforcement**:
   - No specific enforcement mechanism has been mentioned beyond the compliance deadline of January 1, 2020.
   
4. **Additional Information**:
   - The State Attorney General's Office provides a model notice for posting in various languages (English, Spanish, and up to 3 additional languages).
   - Victims can access support through designated hotlines and text lines.

### Harmonization Recommendations

1. **Training Frequency**:
   - Recommend annual refresher courses to ensure ongoing awareness and compliance with updates.
   
2. **Training Materials**:
   - Develop or update training materials based on the latest research and best practices in human trafficking prevention and response.
   
3. **Training Delivery**:
   - Ensure that training sessions are accessible and engaging, possibly offering both online and in-person options.

4. **Monitoring and Evaluation**:
   - Establish a system for monitoring compliance and evaluating the effectiveness of the training programs.

The requirement for human trafficking awareness training is clear and well-defined under California law. To ensure maximum impact and ongoing compliance, continuous improvement and adaptation of training materials and delivery methods are recommended.
