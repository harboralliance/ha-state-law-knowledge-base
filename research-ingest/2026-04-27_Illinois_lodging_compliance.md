Here are some key points from the new legislation regarding human trafficking training for employees in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops:

1. Training requirement:
Starting June 1, 2020, employers must provide their employees with annual human trafficking recognition training.

2. Minimum content of training:
- Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children
- Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for trafficking
- Differences between labor and sex trafficking in the lodging/restaurant/truck stop industry
- Role of employees in reporting and responding to trafficking

3. Department's role:
The Illinois Department of Human Services must develop a standardized curriculum by July 1, 2020.

4. Penalties for non-compliance:
Employers found to have violated the training requirements face potential fines up to $1,500 per offense.

5. Scope of application:
The law applies to lodging establishments (hotels/motels), restaurants, and truck stops.

6. Employee definition:
It includes persons employed in public-facing roles such as receptionists, housekeeping, customer transportation, etc.

7. Training frequency:
Employees must complete the training within 6 months of starting their role, then every 2 years thereafter if still employed.

This new legislation aims to increase awareness and response capabilities among employees who may come into contact with trafficking situations in their workplace environments.
