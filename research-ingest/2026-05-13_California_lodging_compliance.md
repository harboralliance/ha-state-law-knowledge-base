## Summary

### Key Points:
- **Training Requirement**: Hotels and motels are required to provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training and education regarding human trafficking awareness by January 1, 2020.
- **Initial Training**: Employers must also provide this training to new employees within six months of their employment in that role.
- **Exceptions**: Employers who have provided the initial training on or before January 1, 2019, do not need to provide additional training.

### Detailed Information:

#### Training Requirement:
**Hotel and Motel Employees**: Hotel and motel employers are mandated to provide human trafficking awareness training to all employees. This includes those employed as of July 1, 2019, and any new employees hired after that date. The training must cover essential information on identifying potential victims of human trafficking, the rights of these victims, and the steps they can take if such a situation is observed.

**Initial Training**: By January 1, 2020, employers must provide an initial 20-minute session of interactive education to all employees who are likely to interact with or come into contact with potential victims of human trafficking. This includes front desk staff, housekeeping personnel, maintenance staff, and any other roles within the hotel or motel that might be in a position to observe such situations.

**Ongoing Training**: Employers must also provide additional training to new employees within six months of their employment in the same role to ensure they are aware of their responsibilities regarding human trafficking.

#### Exceptions:
- **Prior Training**: Employers who have provided the initial 20-minute training session to an employee on or before January 1, 2019, do not need to provide additional training for that employee unless a new position is assumed.

### Additional Resources:

#### Model Notice:
The California Attorney General's Office has published a model notice that businesses are required to post. This notice informs the public and victims of human trafficking about telephone hotline numbers for seeking help or reporting unlawful activity. The mandate includes posting requirements in English, Spanish, and additional languages as needed based on county language assistance provisions.

### Conclusion:
The state of California has enacted legislation that requires hotels and motels to provide mandatory human trafficking awareness training to their employees. This ensures that these establishments are better equipped to identify, report, and support victims of human trafficking. Compliance with this requirement is crucial for the safety and well-being of both employees and potential victims within these facilities.
