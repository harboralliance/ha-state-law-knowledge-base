## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at addressing human trafficking, specifically sex and labor. The state requires specific trainings for various groups including DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry personnel, and other professionals. Legislation includes provisions to protect victims, mandate annual trafficking awareness training in hotels and motels, and expand the conditions under which human trafficking is considered a crime.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry**: Hotels, motels, inns, similar lodging; hospitality industry.
- **Training required**: Annual human trafficking awareness training for hotel/motel staff.
- **Signage required**: Not specified.
- **Reporting required**: Yes, mandatory reporting of suspected child trafficking to DCF Careline.
- **Enforcement agency**: Department of Children and Families (DCF); State Police.
- **Penalties**: Prostitution is a class C felony; commercial sexual exploitation of a minor is also a class C felony.
- **Effective date**: Varied, with the most recent significant legislation enacted in 2017.

## Harbor Safety
Harbor safety measures are not directly related to human trafficking legislation but would involve emergency response protocols and vessel inspections.

## Statewide Oversight
The Department of Children and Families (DCF) plays a key role in overseeing efforts to combat trafficking, including providing services to victims and training law enforcement.

## Public Awareness and Prevention
Public awareness campaigns are required by law, focusing on the prevention of human trafficking and sexual exploitation. These campaigns must be conducted annually.

## Data Reporting
Ongoing monitoring and data reporting are mandated to track and evaluate the effectiveness of anti-trafficking efforts across the state.

## Conclusion
Connecticut has established a robust framework for combating human trafficking through comprehensive legislation and targeted training programs. The state’s approach includes mandatory training, stringent penalties, victim services, and ongoing public awareness campaigns.
