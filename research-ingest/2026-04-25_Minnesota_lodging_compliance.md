The provided text and the linked document describe state laws and guidelines aimed at preventing sex trafficking in hotels, motels, and short-term rentals in Minnesota. Here are key points:

### Legal Requirements

1. **Training Requirement:**
   - All owners, managers, and on-site employees of hotels, motels, and short-term rentals must complete the required training.
   - New employees must take this training within 90 days of hire.
   - All required staff must retake this training every year.

2. **Display Requirements:**
   - Displaying the back-of-house poster is mandatory.

3. **Exceptions:**
   - Employees under 16 (unless involved in room cleaning) and those working exclusively in restaurants are exempt from certain aspects of the training.
   - Owners, operators, and managers who do not have direct contact with guests or rooms are still required to take the training.

### Training Content

- The training is provided through a virtual presentation and includes a companion guide for viewing the presentation.
- It covers signs of sex trafficking and how to respond to potential incidents.
- Training materials are available in multiple languages including English, Spanish, Somali, and Hmong.

### Compliance and Resources

- **MDH Learning Center:** Managers and owners can access the training package through this resource.
- **Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs):** Provides detailed answers on exceptions, costs, and compliance.
- **Contact Information:** For any questions or assistance accessing the training, email `health.safeharbor@state.mn.us`.

### Training Package

The training package includes:
- A virtual presentation with guided exercises
- A Training Companion Guide (PDF)
- A Manager & Owner Training Toolkit (PDF) to assist in compliance
- A poster set for display

### Updates and Compliance

- Updates to the training package will be provided by November 1 of each year.
- Licenses are required annually, so updates must be applied accordingly.

This comprehensive approach ensures that hotel staff are equipped with the necessary knowledge to recognize and respond to potential instances of sex trafficking, thereby contributing to a safer environment for guests and employees.
