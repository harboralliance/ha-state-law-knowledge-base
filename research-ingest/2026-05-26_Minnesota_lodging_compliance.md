Based on the information provided, here are the key points regarding sex trafficking prevention training for hotels, motels, and short-term rentals in Minnesota:

1. **Legal Requirement**: This training is mandatory under Minnesota law (specifically Minn. Stat. § 260A.37).

2. **Who Must Complete Training**:
   - All owners, managers, and on-site employees of licensed hotels, motels, and short-term rentals must complete the training.
   - New employees must take this training within 90 days of hire.
   - All required staff must retake the training every year.

3. **Training Content**: The training covers signs of sex trafficking and how to respond. It includes a virtual presentation with guided exercises, a Training Companion Guide, a Manager & Owner Training Toolkit, and posters (both front and back of house).

4. **Display Requirements**:
   - All required staff must display the back of house poster in a location all staff will see it.

5. **Training Delivery**: The training can be accessed through the Minnesota Department of Health (MDH) Learning Center. Managers and owners will need to complete a brief pre-training survey before accessing the training package.

6. **Language Availability**: While primarily available in English, the training package is being developed to include Spanish, Hmong, and Somali versions starting late fall 2023/early 2024.

7. **Exemptions**:
   - Employees under 16 unless they clean rooms.
   - Employees who work exclusively in a restaurant, providing catering services, or both.
   - Employees with no direct contact with guests or guest rooms (but this does not apply to owners, operators, and managers).

8. **Compliance Costs**: Licensees are required to pay the costs of compliance, such as printing posters.

9. **Frequent Questions**:
   - Yes, training must be completed every year.
   - No, MDH cannot replace the MDH training with own materials; only limited parts may be replaced with prior review and approval.
   - Owners, operators, and managers are still required to take the training even if they do not have direct contact with guests.
   - Seasonal employees must receive the training within 90 days of when they start working.
   - Contracted workers (like cleaners) are still subject to the training.

10. **Non-Licensed Properties**: Whole house vacation rental properties may be licensed as hotels/motels and therefore subject to following this statute.

For more detailed information, it's recommended to consult the official Minnesota Department of Health resources or contact their office directly at health.foodlodging@state.mn.us.
