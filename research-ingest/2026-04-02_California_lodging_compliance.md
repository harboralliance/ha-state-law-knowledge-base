### Overview

The question at hand is whether human trafficking awareness training is required for employees of motels and hotels. This involves examining multiple sources to understand the specific requirements and implications.

#### Sources:

1. **Source 1 (California Labor Code Section 437a)**: This source indicates that employers must provide sexual harassment prevention training, including training on preventing sexual violence by victims of human trafficking. However, it does not explicitly mention mandatory human trafficking awareness training for all employees in motels and hotels.

2. **Source 2 (Senate Bill 970)**: This source clearly states that starting January 1, 2020, hotel and motel employers must provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training and education regarding human trafficking awareness to each employee who is likely to interact with victims of human trafficking. The law applies to employees employed as of July 1, 2019, and any new employees within six months of their employment.

3. **Source 3 (Human Trafficking Model Notice)**: This source provides a model notice that must be posted in businesses required by law, including hotels, motels, and bed and breakfasts. The notice includes hotline numbers for victims to seek help or report unlawful activity.

### Analysis:

- **Training Requirement**: According to Senate Bill 970 (Source 2), hotel and motel employers are required to provide human trafficking awareness training to all employees who interact with victims of human trafficking.
  
- **Posting Requirement**: The model notice (Source 3) must be posted in these establishments, indicating a commitment to public awareness.

- **Language Requirements**: The notice must be available in English and Spanish, with additional languages required for businesses located in specific counties covered under federal language assistance provisions.

### Conclusion:

Human trafficking awareness training is explicitly required for employees of motels and hotels. Employers are responsible for providing at least 20 minutes of interactive training to those who interact with victims of human trafficking, as mandated by Senate Bill 970. In addition, a model notice must be posted in compliance with the law.

**Effective Date**: January 1, 2020

**Enforcement**: Failure to comply with these requirements could result in penalties or further actions by regulatory bodies like the California Department of Labor and Employment (DOL) or the California Attorney General's office.
