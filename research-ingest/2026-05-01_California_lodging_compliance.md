### Summary

The California state government, through Senate Bill 970 (SB 970), has introduced and enacted legislation requiring hotel and motel employers to provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training and education regarding human trafficking awareness. This training is required for employees who are likely to interact with victims of human trafficking. The law became effective on January 1, 2020.

The training requirement applies to all hotel and motel employers as of July 1, 2019, but new employees must also receive the training within six months of their employment in that role. Employers who have provided this training to an employee on or before January 1, 2019, are exempt from providing additional training.

The model notice for posting in hotel and motel establishments can be downloaded from the California Attorney General's website. Each mandated business is required to post the notice in English and Spanish. For businesses located in specific counties, a third language posting may be required, depending on the county's requirements regarding language assistance provisions of the federal Voting Rights Act.

In case of immediate danger or if you suspect human trafficking, the National Human Trafficking Hotline can be reached at 1-888-373-7888. For general inquiries and information, visit the California Attorney General’s website or contact the Office of the Attorney General directly.

### Detailed Analysis

#### Compliance Requirements
1. **Training Duration**: At least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training.
2. **Employee Scope**: Employees likely to interact with victims of human trafficking.
3. **Effective Interactive Training**: The training must be conducted in a way that is effective and interactive.

#### Timeline
- **Initial Requirement for Existing Employees**: July 1, 2019.
- **Requirement for New Employees**: Within six months of employment.

#### Exemptions
- Employers who have provided the required training on or before January 1, 2019, are exempt from providing additional training.

### Impact and Compliance
This legislation aims to enhance awareness and preparedness among hotel and motel employees to identify and respond to potential cases of human trafficking. Failure to comply with this law can result in penalties and legal consequences for the business.

### Resources and Support
For businesses seeking further guidance on compliance, resources are available online through the California Attorney General's website. For immediate assistance or reporting suspected cases, contacting the National Human Trafficking Hotline provides a lifeline of support and information.

This comprehensive approach ensures that hotel and motel employees have the necessary tools to recognize and address human trafficking effectively, thereby protecting victims and upholding ethical business practices.
