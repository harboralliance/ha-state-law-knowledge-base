The Illinois General Assembly has enacted a new law regarding human trafficking and training for employees in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.

**Key Provisions:**

1. **Training Requirement (Sec. 10):**
   - Effective June 1, 2020, employers of lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops must provide their employees with training on the recognition of human trafficking and protocols for reporting such incidents.
   - Employees must complete this training within 6 months of starting their role and every 2 years thereafter if they continue to work in that position.

2. **Training Content (Sec. 15):**
   - Employers can either use their own training programs or those provided by third parties, but the program must include:
     - A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
     - Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
     - Differentiation between labor and sex trafficking in the context of lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
     - Role guidance for employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

3. **Departmental Curriculum (Sec. 15):**
   - The Department of Human Services is required to develop a standardized curriculum for approved human trafficking training programs that can be used by establishments not providing their own program.
   - This curriculum must cover the same minimum requirements as above and must be developed and published no later than July 1, 2020.

4. **Penalties for Non-Compliance (Sec. 20):**
   - Local government units or law enforcement agencies can monitor and enforce compliance with this act.
   - Upon discovery of a violation, they must provide a reasonable notice to the employer. If not corrected within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action resulting in fines up to $1,500 per offense.

**Effective Date:**
The law takes effect January 1, 2026.

This legislation aims to improve awareness and response mechanisms for human trafficking among employees who interact frequently with the public, thereby potentially enhancing protection for vulnerable individuals.
