## Summary
Connecticut provides various training programs for service providers, including foster parents, law enforcement, and medical staff, to educate them about human trafficking. The state also has enacted several public acts to address human trafficking, expanding protections for victims and requiring hotels and motels to provide annual trafficking training.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Child protective services, foster care, schools, law enforcement, healthcare, hospitality, hotels, motels, lodging, youth, and community.
- **Training required:** Annual basic training for all Child Protective Services staff; advanced training instead of the basic one; training for various professions including teachers, police officers, EMS personnel, medical providers, hotel staff, and youth.
- **Signage required:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees annually.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and cases referred for prosecution.
- **Enforcement measures:** Hotels and motels must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months.
- **Legislation enacted:**
  - Public Act 17-32 (2017): Expands human trafficking law to include minors under age 18.
  - Public Act 16-71 (2016): Defines human trafficking as a class C felony and requires mandatory data reporting.
  - Public Act 15-195 (2015): Provides expanded protections for victims of human trafficking, including the erasure of juvenile records.
  - Public Act 14-186 (2014): Requires DCF to provide services and training for human trafficking victims and law enforcement.
  - Public Act 13-166 (2013): Aligns with federal TVPA, making commercial sexual exploitation a class C felony.

## Effective Dates
- **Trainings:** Annual basic training required from Child Protective Services staff; advanced training is optional but encouraged.
- **Legislation Implementation:** Various public acts took effect in 2015, 2016, and 2017.
