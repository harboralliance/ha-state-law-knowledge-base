## Summary
Connecticut has enacted several laws to address human trafficking, including sex and labor. The state requires training for various groups, such as DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other professionals. The laws also mandate the reporting of trafficking cases and provide support services for victims.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Hospitality industry, healthcare, law enforcement, education, social services.
- **Training required:** DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement (POST certified), emergency medical services (EMS), hospitals and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, youth. 
- **Signage required:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- **Enforcement body:** Department of Public Health (DPH), Office of Victim Services (OVS).
- **Support services:** DCF provides services to human trafficking victims classified as “uncared for,” and law enforcement receives training on handling such cases.

## Enforcement and Support
- **Data reporting:** Mandatory data reporting for trafficking cases.
- **Statewide oversight:** Statewide oversight and monitoring body to combat trafficking efforts.

## Conclusion
Connecticut has robust laws and training programs in place to address human trafficking, ensuring that various stakeholders are equipped to handle such cases effectively. The state also provides support services for victims and mandates the reporting of trafficking incidents to maintain accountability and improve intervention strategies.
