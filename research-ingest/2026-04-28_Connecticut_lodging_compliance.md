## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs in place to address human trafficking. The state requires annual basic human trafficking training for all Child Protective Services staff, and advanced training for those who choose it. Specific laws include expanding protections for victims of human trafficking, requiring hotels and motels to provide annual training on human trafficking, and creating multidisciplinary teams to handle such cases. Training programs cover various aspects including child trafficking in Connecticut, labor trafficking, and specialized trainings for schools, law enforcement, emergency medical services, and hospitality industries.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry, other professionals.
- **Training required:** Annual basic human trafficking training for all Child Protective Services staff; advanced training available.
- **Signage required:** Not explicitly stated in the provided sources.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, cases referred for prosecution, etc. Hotels and motels must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.
- **Enforcement:** Mandatory data reporting; statewide oversight and monitoring body.
- **Effective date:** Various laws have different effective dates ranging from 2010 to 2017.

## Enforcement
Connecticut has enacted several public acts addressing human trafficking, expanding protections for victims, requiring annual training for certain industries, creating multidisciplinary teams, and establishing mandatory data reporting. These measures demonstrate a comprehensive approach to combatting human trafficking in the state.
