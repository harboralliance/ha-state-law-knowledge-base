The provided text is a bill (specifically, Senate Bill 1490) from the Illinois General Assembly that amends the Illinois Human Trafficking Prevention Act. Here's a summary of its key points:

1. **Definitions**: It defines terms such as "human trafficking," "employer," and "employee" relevant to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.

2. **Training Requirement**: Effective June 1, 2020, employers must provide training on recognizing human trafficking and reporting it to appropriate authorities within 6 months of employment and every 2 years thereafter.

3. **Curriculum Standards**: The Department of Human Services must develop a curriculum that includes:
   - A definition of human trafficking.
   - Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for trafficking.
   - Differences between labor and sex trafficking in the context of lodging, restaurants, and truck stops.
   - Guidance on employee roles in reporting and responding to trafficking.

4. **Penalties**: The bill establishes penalties for non-compliance, including:
   - A notice of noncompliance from a local government or law enforcement agency.
   - A potential civil penalty up to $1,500 per violation if the employer fails to correct the issue within 30 days.
   - The possibility of a civil action by the Attorney General or State's Attorney if the violation is not corrected.

5. **Effective Date**: The bill takes effect on January 1, 2026.

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and response capabilities in businesses that interact with the public, particularly those in hospitality sectors, to combat human trafficking effectively.
