The information provided pertains to laws and training requirements for hotels, motels, and short-term rentals in Minnesota related to preventing and responding to sex trafficking. Here’s a summary:

### Legal Requirements:
- **Owners, Managers, and Employees:** All must complete the required training.
- **New Employees:** Must take the training within 90 days of hire.
- **Annual Requirement:** All required staff must retake the training every year.

### Training Content:
The training covers identifying signs of sex trafficking and how to respond appropriately. It includes:
- A virtual presentation with guided exercises
- A Training Companion Guide (PDF)
- Manager & Owner Training Toolkit (PDF)
- A set of posters for display in various areas of the property

### Exceptions:
- Employees under 16 unless they clean rooms.
- Employees who work exclusively in a restaurant, providing catering services, or both and have no direct contact with guests or guest rooms.

### Distribution:
The training package is available through the Minnesota Department of Health (MDH) Learning Center. Managers and owners can access it there after completing a brief pre-training survey.

### Languages:
While English is available, translations to Spanish, Somali, and Hmong will be provided starting late fall 2023/early 2024 upon request.

### Compliance Costs:
Licensees are responsible for paying the costs of compliance. MDH does not provide financial assistance with printing posters or other materials.

### Training Frequency:
Managers and owners, as well as employees, must take the training every year.

### Additional Resources:
- MDH encourages all staff to be trained, even if they don't fall into exemption categories.
- Contact information is provided for those needing assistance accessing the training.

This comprehensive approach ensures that hotels, motels, and short-term rentals play a vital role in preventing sex trafficking within their establishments.
