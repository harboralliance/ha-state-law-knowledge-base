### Summary of the Bill

The bill (P.A. 102-324) amends the Illinois Human Trafficking Prevention Act and requires lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops in Illinois to provide their employees with training on recognizing human trafficking and protocols for reporting observed human trafficking incidents. Here are the key points:

### Definitions
1. **Department**: The Department of Human Services.
2. **Employee**: A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who interacts with the public regularly (e.g., reception area workers, housekeeping staff).
3. **Employer**: A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
4. **Human Trafficking**: The deprivation of personal liberty with intent to obtain forced labor, commercial sex, or other exploitation.
5. **Lodging Establishment**: An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) codes 721110 and 721120.
6. **Restaurant**: Any business primarily engaged in selling ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption, comprising at least 51% of total sales (excluding liquor).
7. **Truck Stop**: An establishment providing services to the trucking industry, including fuel, food, showers, repair services, and parking.

### Training Requirements
- **Effective Date**: June 1, 2020.
- **Training Content**: Employees must receive training on:
  - Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
  - Identification of individuals at risk for human trafficking.
  - Differences between labor and sex trafficking in the context of lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
  - Role of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.
- **Frequency**: Employees must complete training within 6 months after beginning employment and every 2 years thereafter if still employed.

### Curriculum
The Department is required to develop a curriculum for an approved human trafficking recognition program. The Department can consult the U.S. Department of Justice for this purpose, and must publish the program no later than July 1, 2020.

### Penalties
- **Local Government or Law Enforcement Agency**: Upon discovery of a violation, they must provide a reasonable notice to the employer, informing them that if the issue is not corrected within 30 days, they will be subject to a civil penalty.
- **Attorney General/State's Attorney**: If the issue remains unresolved after 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney can bring a civil action against the employer. Violation of this act constitutes a business offense and can result in fines up to $1,500 for each offense.

### Effective Date
The bill takes effect on January 1, 2026.

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and preparedness among hospitality industry employees regarding human trafficking, thereby potentially preventing such incidents from occurring.
