# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Sources

- https://portal.ct.gov/dcf/knowledge-base/articles/supports-for-children-and-families/hart---sex-trafficking/human-and-sex-trafficking-legislation

## Summary
Connecticut offers various trainings for providers, foster parents, and communities on human trafficking. The state has enacted several laws to address human trafficking, including sex and labor trafficking. These laws include expanding the crime of human trafficking, requiring training for hotels and motels on awareness and prevention, and providing services to victims.

## Extracted Requirements
- Applicable industry: Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging; law enforcement; healthcare providers; schools; foster parents; and youth.
- Training required: Annual human trafficking training for hotels and motels; regular trainings for DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, emergency medical services (EMS), medical providers, hospitality industry professionals, and other professionals.
- Signage required: Hotels and motels must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees annually.
- Reporting required: Law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc. Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months.
- Enforcement body: State attorneys and local law enforcement.
- Penalties: Commercial sexual exploitation of a minor is a class C felony.

## Summary of Relevant Legislation
- **2017 Public Act 17-32**: Expands the crime of human trafficking and broadens conditions under which it can be committed.
- **2016 Public Act 16-71**: Establishes protections for victims of human trafficking, including expanded services from the Department of Public Health.
- **2015 Public Act 15-195**: Expands the crime of human trafficking and expands conditions under which a court must order the erasure of a juvenile's police and court records.
- **2014 Public Act 14-186**: Provides services to "uncared for" human trafficking victims and mandates training for law enforcement on human trafficking cases.
- **2013 Public Act 13-166**: Aligns with the federal Trafficking Victims Protection Act, making commercial sexual exploitation of a minor a class C felony.
- **2012 Public Act 12-141**: Defines commercial sexual exploitation of a minor and makes it a class C felony.
- **2011 Public Act 11-180**: Requires reporting of suspected abuse or neglect to the Department of Children and Families upon arrest for prostitution.
- **2010 Public Act 10-115**: Provides a safe harbor for exploited children, with presumptions of coercion in prosecutions.

## Summary of Recommended Actions
- **Training Providers**: Implement regular trainings on human trafficking for all relevant industries and professionals.
- **Enhance Awareness**: Require annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns in hotels and motels to ensure continued education and preparedness.
- **Documentation and Reporting**: Ensure that law enforcement documents necessary information and maintains guest transactions and receipts as required by state laws.
- **Enforcement and Compliance**: Strengthen enforcement mechanisms to hold individuals accountable for human trafficking violations, including commercial sexual exploitation of minors.

These measures will help Connecticut effectively address and mitigate the impact of human trafficking within its borders.
