# State Research Capture

Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at addressing human trafficking, including sex trafficking. The state requires training for various professionals, including DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry workers, and other professionals. Legislation such as Public Act 17-32 (PDF) specifically addresses human trafficking and includes provisions for victims' services, criminal penalties, and data reporting.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry workers, other professionals.
- **Training required:** Annual basic training on human trafficking for all Child Protective Services staff; advanced training is an option. Specific trainings are available for different sectors (e.g., school settings, law enforcement, emergency medical services).
- **Signage required:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees annually.
- **Data reporting:** Mandatory data reporting for trafficking cases.
- **Enforcement measures:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.

## Enforcement Measures
Legislation such as Public Act 15-195 (PDF) expands protections for victims of human trafficking, including expanded conditions under which the crime is committed when the victim is a minor. The law also allows the Office of Victim Services to waive the two-year limitation on crime victim compensation applications for minors who are victims of human trafficking.

## Prevention and Awareness
Connecticut's laws include provisions for public awareness and prevention, such as expanding the list of crimes for which wiretapping may be authorized, aligning with federal law, and requiring multidisciplinary teams to work with human trafficking cases. The state also emphasizes ongoing monitoring of efforts to combat trafficking and mandatory data reporting.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's comprehensive approach to addressing human trafficking includes robust training programs and legislation that cover various sectors and provide protections for victims. These measures aim to reduce the incidence of human trafficking and support those affected by it.
