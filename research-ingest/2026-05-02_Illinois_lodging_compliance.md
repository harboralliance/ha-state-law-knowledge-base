The provided text is a bill that aims to establish requirements for human trafficking recognition training for employees working in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. Here's a breakdown of the key points:

1. **Effective Date**: The provisions of this act will take effect on January 1, 2026.

2. **Definitions**:
   - "Department": Department of Human Services.
   - "Employee": A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
   - "Employer": A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
   - "Human trafficking": The deprivation or violation of personal liberty to obtain forced labor, services, or for commercial sex exploitation.
   - "Lodging establishment", "Restaurant", and "Truck stop" are defined according to the North American Industry Classification System.

3. **Training Requirement**:
   - Starting from June 1, 2020, employers must provide training to their employees in recognizing human trafficking and protocols for reporting it.
   - Employees must complete this training within six months of beginning employment and every two years thereafter if still employed by the employer.

4. **Curriculum Requirements**:
   - Employers may use their own program or a third-party program, as long as it includes: 
     - A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
     - Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
     - Differences between labor and sex-based trafficking.
     - Guidance on employees' roles in reporting and responding to human trafficking instances.

5. **Department's Role**:
   - The Department must develop a curriculum for approved human trafficking training programs.
   - This program will be used by establishments that do not administer their own program.
   - The curriculum must meet the same requirements as those for employers using third-party programs.
   - The Department should consult with the US Department of Justice for any assistance.

6. **Penalties**:
   - Local government units or law enforcement agencies can monitor and enforce compliance with this act.
   - Upon discovery of a violation, an employer will receive notice to cure within 30 days or face a civil penalty of up to $1,500 per offense.

7. **Lodging Establishment**: This is further defined as any establishment classified under specific codes in the North American Industry Classification System that provides lodging services.

Overall, this bill emphasizes education and compliance among employers to better protect their employees from potential human trafficking situations.
