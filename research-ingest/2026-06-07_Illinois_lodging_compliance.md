### Analysis of the Legislation

**Title:** Human Trafficking Prevention and Awareness Act

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026

**Objective:**
The act aims to prevent human trafficking by requiring lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide their employees with training on recognizing and reporting human trafficking.

### Key Provisions:

#### Definitions:
- **Department:** Department of Human Services.
- **Employee:** Employees of a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who interact frequently with the public.
- **Employer:** The operator of a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** Forced labor, commercial sex, or exploitation through various means like fraud, violence, etc.
- **Lodging Establishment:** A hotel or motel classified under code 721110 and casino hotels under code 721120 in the North American Industry Classification System.
- **Restaurant:** A business primarily engaged in selling ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption.
- **Truck Stop:** An establishment providing services to the trucking industry.

#### Training Requirements:
- **Training Content:** Minimum requirements include identifying individuals at risk, distinguishing labor and sex trafficking, and employees' roles in reporting and responding to human trafficking.
- **Implementation Dates:**
  - Initial training by June 1, 2020
  - Repeated every two years thereafter for those still employed

#### Training Program:
- **Approval:** Employers can use their own or third-party programs as long as they meet the minimum requirements.
- **Department Curriculum:** The Department of Human Services must develop and publish a curriculum by July 1, 2020. This curriculum should be used if an employer does not create their own program.

#### Penalties:
- **Local Government Monitoring:** Units regulating employers or law enforcement agencies can monitor compliance and issue notices.
- **Cure Period:** Employers have 30 days to correct violations before facing penalties.
- **Legal Action:** If the violation is not corrected, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action for fines up to $1,500 per offense.

### Impact and Benefits:
- **Enhanced Awareness:** Training will increase employee awareness of human trafficking signs and appropriate responses.
- **Immediate Reporting:** Employees will be better equipped to report suspicious activities immediately, potentially preventing more instances of trafficking.
- **Compliance Monitoring:** Local governments can enforce compliance, ensuring that all establishments are adhering to the required training standards.

### Potential Challenges:
- **Resource Intensive:** Developing comprehensive training materials and monitoring compliance may place additional burdens on lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
- **Employee Buy-In:** Ensuring employees fully understand and internalize the training could be challenging.
- **Legal Enforcement:** The effectiveness of penalties depends on how well violations are identified and enforced by local governments.

### Conclusion:
The Human Trafficking Prevention and Awareness Act is a significant step towards combating human trafficking in establishments that interact frequently with the public. By mandating comprehensive training and establishing clear reporting protocols, it aims to increase awareness and response capabilities among employees. However, implementation will require careful planning and resources to ensure its success.
