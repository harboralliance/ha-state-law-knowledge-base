## Summary
Connecticut provides comprehensive training and support for service providers, foster parents, schools, law enforcement, hospitals, and hospitality industry professionals to combat human trafficking. The state has implemented multiple laws, including 2017 Public Act 17-32 and 2016 Public Act 16-71, which focus on expanding protections for victims of human trafficking and strengthening penalties for traffickers.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Child protective services, foster parents, schools, law enforcement, hospitals, hospitality industry.
- **Training required:** Annual basic training for all Child Protective Services staff. Advanced training is optional. Specialized trainings are available for foster parents, schools, law enforcement (POST Certified), emergency medical services (EMS), hotels, motels, and lodging businesses.
- **Signage required:** Not specified in the provided source text.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution.
- **Penalties:** Commercial sexual exploitation of a minor is a class C felony. Forfeiture properties, affirmative defense for minors, and vacating records are provisions in place to address human trafficking.

## Enforcement
Connecticut has established a statewide oversight and monitoring body to combat trafficking and mandate data reporting.

## Prevention
Public awareness and prevention campaigns are mandated for hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging establishments. The state also supports ongoing monitoring of efforts to combat trafficking through legislation like the 2013 Public Act 13-166.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's approach to combating human trafficking is multi-faceted, involving comprehensive training, legislative protections, reporting requirements, enforcement measures, and public awareness campaigns.
