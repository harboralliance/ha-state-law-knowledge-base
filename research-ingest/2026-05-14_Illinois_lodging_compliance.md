### Summary of the Human Trafficking Recognition Training Act

**Effective Date:** June 1, 2020  
**Title:** Human Trafficking Recognition Training Act (P.A. 101-18)

This legislation requires employers, lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide their employees with training in recognizing human trafficking and protocols for reporting it. Key points include:

#### Definitions
- **Department**: Department of Human Services.
- **Employee**: Employee of a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer**: Owner or operator of a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking**: The deprivation of personal liberty for labor or services, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.

#### Training Requirements
- **Initial Training**: Employers must provide human trafficking recognition training to their employees within six months of employment. This training must be completed and should last at least 20 minutes.
- **Ongoing Training**: Employees must complete additional training every two years if still employed by the employer.

#### Training Curriculum
Employers can use their own program or a third-party one that includes:
1. A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
2. Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
3. The difference between labor and sex trafficking in relation to lodging, restaurants, and truck stops.
4. Guidance on employees' roles in reporting and responding to human trafficking incidents.

The Department must develop a curriculum by July 1, 2020, if an employer does not provide its own program.

#### Penalties
A unit of local government or law enforcement agency can monitor compliance with the Act. If a violation is discovered, they will issue a reasonable notice to cure within 30 days. If not corrected, the Attorney General or State's Attorney can bring a civil action against the employer. Violations are considered business offenses and can result in fines of up to $1,500 per offense.

#### Effective Date
The Act takes effect January 1, 2026.

### Key Takeaways:
- **Mandatory Training**: All employers must provide training within six months and every two years thereafter.
- **Training Content**: Must cover key aspects of human trafficking including definitions, risk identification, differences between labor and sex trafficking, and reporting responsibilities.
- **Compliance Enforcement**: Local governments and law enforcement can enforce compliance, with penalties for non-compliance.

This legislation aims to enhance employee awareness and responsiveness in recognizing and addressing instances of human trafficking within various establishments.
