## Summary
Connecticut has robust legislation and training programs to address human trafficking. The state requires various professionals, including foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital staff, hotel employees, and emergency medical services, to undergo specific trainings on identifying and responding to child trafficking. Legislation includes provisions for victim services, record erasure, wiretapping authorizations, and victim compensation applications.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital staff, hotel employees, emergency medical services.
- **Training required:** Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Child Trafficking for School Settings, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Emergency Medical Services, Child Trafficking Training for Medical Providers/Emergency Medicine, Introduction to Labor Trafficking in Connecticut.
- **Signage required:** Not explicitly mentioned in the provided sources.
- **Reporting required:** Hotels, motels, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months. State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and cases referred for prosecution.
- **Enforcement:** The state requires expansion of protections for victims of human trafficking, including expanding the crime of human trafficking when the victim is a minor, providing human trafficking victims with services similar to those provided to sexual assault victims, and allowing wiretapping authorizations for certain crimes, including human trafficking.
- **Victim Services:** DCF provides training to law enforcement on human trafficking of minors, creation of multidisciplinary teams to work with human trafficking cases, and services to uncared-for human trafficking victims.

## Enforcement and Reporting
Connecticut's laws require the reporting of suspected child trafficking and investigations by hotels, motels, and local law enforcement. Additionally, DCF provides comprehensive training for various professionals involved in combating human trafficking.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's approach to combat human trafficking is multifaceted, including rigorous legislative requirements and comprehensive training programs designed to equip professionals across multiple industries with the knowledge necessary to identify and respond to child trafficking effectively.
