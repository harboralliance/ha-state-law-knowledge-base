The text you provided describes legislation that aims to improve awareness and response to human trafficking in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. Here are key points from the bill:

1. **Training Requirement**: Employers must provide training on recognizing human trafficking and reporting it to appropriate authorities within 6 months of employment for new hires in roles involving public interaction.

2. **Training Frequency**: The initial training is mandatory, followed by every two years if the employee remains employed.

3. **Training Content**: Training programs must cover:
   - Definition of human trafficking
   - Identification of individuals at risk
   - Difference between labor and sex trafficking relevant to lodging, restaurants, and truck stops
   - Role of employees in reporting and responding to trafficking

4. **Curriculum Development**: The Department of Human Services is tasked with developing a standard curriculum that all establishments can use.

5. **Penalties for Non-Compliance**: 
   - Local government agencies or law enforcement can monitor compliance.
   - If not corrected within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring civil action.
   - Violators face fines up to $1,500 per offense.

6. **Effective Date**: The bill becomes effective on January 1, 2026.

This legislation seeks to enhance awareness and responsiveness of employees in these industries to potential signs of human trafficking, thereby potentially preventing victims from entering or remaining in such situations.
