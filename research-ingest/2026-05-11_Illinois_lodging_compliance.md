### Summary of SB1422

SB1422 is a bill in Illinois that aims to address human trafficking within lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. Below is a detailed summary of the key provisions:

#### Definitions:
- **Department**: The Department of Human Services.
- **Employee**: A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who interacts with the public regularly.
- **Employer**: A person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking**: The deprivation or violation of someone's personal liberty with intent to obtain forced labor or services, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.
- **Lodging Establishment**: An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the 2017 North American Industry Classification System.
- **Restaurant**: A business primarily selling ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption.
- **Truck Stop**: An establishment providing services to the trucking industry.

#### Human Trafficking Recognition Training:
Starting June 1, 2020:
- Employers must provide their employees with training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols.
- Employees must complete this training within six months of starting a role and every two years thereafter if still employed.

The training shall be at least 20 minutes in duration.

#### Human Trafficking Recognition Training Curriculum:
- **Employer Option**: Employers can use their own program or third-party programs that include:
  1. Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
  2. Guidance on identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
  3. Differences between labor and sex trafficking as it relates to lodging, restaurants, and truck stops.
  4. Role of employees in reporting and responding to human trafficking.

- **Department Curricula**: The Department must develop an approved curriculum that includes the same elements if the employer does not administer their own program. This curricula must be made available no later than July 1, 2020.

#### Penalties:
- A unit of local government or law enforcement agency can monitor and enforce compliance with the act.
- Upon discovery of a violation, they provide an employer with a reasonable notice of noncompliance.
- If the violation is not corrected within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer.
- An employer violating the act is guilty of a business offense and can be fined up to $1,500 per offense.

#### Effective Date:
This Act takes effect on January 1, 2026.

### Key Highlights:
- Ensures that employees in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops are trained to recognize human trafficking.
- Requires regular training every two years to maintain awareness and compliance.
- Imposes penalties for non-compliance, encouraging employers to adhere to the mandatory requirements.

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and proactive measures against human trafficking within specific sectors of the hospitality industry.
