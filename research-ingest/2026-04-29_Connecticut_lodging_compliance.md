## Summary
Connecticut provides various training programs for providers, foster parents, and communities to combat human trafficking, including child sex and labor trafficking. The state also has enacted several public acts to strengthen protections for victims of human trafficking and prevent commercial sexual exploitation of minors.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging; law enforcement; emergency medical services (EMS); healthcare providers; hospitality industry.
- **Training required:** Annual basic training on human trafficking for Child Protective Services staff; specialized trainings for foster parents, schools, law enforcement, EMS, healthcare providers, hospitality industry professionals, youth, and the community.
- **Signage required:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees annually and keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- **Enforcement:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees annually; keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.

## Enforcement
Connecticut’s public acts aim to strengthen protections for victims of human trafficking and prevent commercial sexual exploitation of minors. These laws include provisions such as:

- Expanding the crime of human trafficking by broadening the conditions under which it is committed when the victim is a minor.
- Requiring the Department of Public Health (DPH) to provide human trafficking victims the same services it must provide certain sexual assault victims under existing law.
- Expanding the list of crimes, including human trafficking, for which wiretapping may be authorized.

## Effective Date
The public acts mentioned in the legislation are effective as of February 8, 2025.

## Legal Framework
Connecticut’s laws address human trafficking through various statutes, such as Public Act 17-32 and Public Act 16-71. These laws define commercial sexual exploitation of a minor and establish mandatory data reporting requirements for state agencies.

## Conclusion
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at combating human trafficking in the state. The state’s enforcement measures include annual training requirements for specific industries and expanded protections for victims of human trafficking.
