**Summary of SB1422:**

SB1422 is a bill in the Illinois General Assembly that requires lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide human trafficking recognition training to their employees. Key points include:

1. **Training Requirement**: Starting June 1, 2020, employers must provide training within six months of employment to identify human trafficking and report incidents to appropriate authorities.

2. **Training Frequency**: The training must be completed by all employees who have recurring interactions with the public, such as receptionists, housekeeping staff, etc., and must be repeated every two years.

3. **Minimum Training Content**:
   - Definition of human trafficking.
   - Identification of individuals at risk for trafficking.
   - Differences between labor and sex-based trafficking.
   - Roles of employees in reporting and responding to trafficking incidents.

4. **Departmental Curriculum**: The Department of Human Services is required to develop a standard curriculum that must be used by establishments not administering their own training program.

5. **Penalties**: Non-compliance may lead to a reasonable notice from the local government or law enforcement agency, with potential fines up to $1,500 per offense if not remedied within 30 days.

6. **Effective Date**: The bill takes effect on January 1, 2026.

The purpose of this legislation is to enhance awareness and response capabilities among employees in these establishments regarding human trafficking, potentially preventing exploitation and aiding in the rescue of victims.
