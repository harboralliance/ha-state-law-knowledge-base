### Summary of Key Points:

1. **Training Requirement for Hotel and Motel Employees:**
   - **SB 970 (Source: California Civil Rights Commission, CalAGO):** 
     - Requires hotels and motels to provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training regarding human trafficking awareness.
     - Training must be provided by January 1, 2020.
     - Employees hired after July 1, 2019, must also receive training within six months of their start date.
   - **Training Content:** The training should cover identifying indicators of human trafficking and what employees can do to assist potential victims.

2. **Posting Requirement for Business Notices:**
   - **Senate Bill 1193 (Source: California Civil Rights Commission, CalAGO):**
     - Requires specified businesses, including hotels and motels, to post a notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking.
     - The notice must include contact numbers for the National Human Trafficking Hotline and any other relevant resources.
   - **Posting Languages:** Notices must be posted in English and Spanish. For specific counties, a third language may also be required as per federal Voting Rights Act provisions.

3. **Model Notice for Hotels and Motels:**
   - **California Attorney General’s Office (Source: DOJ Model Notice):**
     - Provides a model notice template for hotels and motels to post.
     - The notice includes contact information for the National Human Trafficking Hotline, as well as any other relevant resources.
   
4. **Victim Assistance and Reporting:**
   - **National Human Trafficking Resource Center:** Offers 24/7 hotline numbers (1-888-373-7888) for victims to report trafficking.
   - **Services Available:** Text-based services, as mandated by SB 225.

### Summary:
The California legislature has mandated specific training and posting requirements for hotel and motel employees regarding human trafficking awareness. These requirements are designed to help these establishments identify potential victims of human trafficking and provide assistance, while also ensuring that the public and victims have access to critical resources.
