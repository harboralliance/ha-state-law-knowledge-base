The Office of the Attorney General (OAG) in Texas is responsible for enforcing laws related to human trafficking, including requiring annual training for workers at certain commercial lodging establishments. Here are key details about the OAG's role and resources:

### Key Requirements:
1. **Training Requirement**: The OAG has passed House Bill 390, which mandates that employees of commercial lodging establishments with 10 or more rooms receive human trafficking training annually.
2. **Purpose**: The goal is to increase awareness and reporting capabilities among workers who may come into contact with potential victims of human trafficking.

### Training Resources:
1. **Approved Training Videos**:
   - Various organizations have developed approved training videos that meet the OAG's standards. These include:
     - **Office of the Attorney General**: "Human Trafficking Hotel Training" (22 minutes, English, free)
     - **Etactics**: "Human Trafficking – Texas" (26 minutes, English, $1/user/month)
     - **Unbound Now**: "Identifying and Responding to Human Trafficking: Commercial Lodging & Hospitality Industry" (25 minutes each in English and Spanish, free)

### Certification and Compliance:
1. **Certification**: Approved training providers must provide a certificate of completion to every employee who completes the training, including the date and title of the course.
2. **Retention**: Operators of commercial lodging establishments must retain copies of each annual certificate for review by the OAG and local, state, and federal law enforcement.

### Training Providers:
- The list includes various organizations such as Etactics, Unbound Now, Businesses Ending Slavery and Trafficking, Extended Stay America, Marriott International, ECPAT-USA, Refugee Services of Texas, PBJ Learning, and Compliance Learning Solutions.
  
### Delivery Methods:
- **Online**: Many training videos are available online for both free and paid options.
- **In-person**: Some organizations offer in-person training as well.

This comprehensive approach by the OAG aims to educate workers about human trafficking and empower them to recognize signs of exploitation and respond appropriately, ultimately contributing to the prevention and reporting of this serious crime.
