### Summary of Legislation: Human Trafficking Recognition Training for Employees

#### Purpose:
The legislation aims to enhance the awareness and response capabilities of employees working in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to human trafficking. It requires employers to provide training on recognizing human trafficking and reporting such incidents.

#### Key Provisions:

1. **Training Requirement (Sec. 10):**
   - Employers must provide their employees with training in the recognition of human trafficking and protocols for reporting observed instances.
   - Training must be completed within 6 months after starting employment in the role, and every 2 years thereafter if still employed by the employer.
   - The training must be at least 20 minutes long.

2. **Training Curriculum (Sec. 15):**
   - Employers may use their own human trafficking training program or that of a third party as long as it includes:
     - A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children
     - Guidance on identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking
     - The difference between labor and sex trafficking as relevant to lodging, restaurants, and truck stops
     - Guidance on the role of employees in reporting and responding to human trafficking

3. **Department-developed Curriculum:**
   - If an employer does not administer its own program, the Department shall develop a curriculum for an approved training recognition program.
   - This curriculum must include all elements required by subsection (a).
   - The Department may consult the U.S. Department of Justice on developing this curriculum.
   - The Department must publish the curriculum no later than July 1, 2020.

4. **Penalties for Non-Compliance (Sec. 20):**
   - Upon discovering a violation, local government units or law enforcement agencies may issue a reasonable notice of noncompliance.
   - If the violation is not corrected within 30 days, the employer faces a civil penalty of up to $1,500 per offense.

#### Effective Date:
The Act takes effect on January 1, 2026.

### Key Terms and Definitions:

- **Employee:** A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop with recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer:** A person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** Deprivation or violation of personal liberty for forced labor, services, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.
- **Lodging Establishment:** An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the 2017 North American Industry Classification System code 721110 or casino hotel code 721120.
- **Restaurant:** A business primarily engaged in the sale of ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption.
- **Truck Stop:** An establishment providing services to the trucking industry, including fuel and food sales, showers, repair services, and ample parking.

### Conclusion:
This legislation seeks to proactively address human trafficking by ensuring employees are trained to recognize and respond to potential incidents. It aims to protect vulnerable individuals in hospitality sectors through enhanced training requirements and accountability measures.
