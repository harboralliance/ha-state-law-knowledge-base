# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Sources

- https://portal.ct.gov/dcf/knowledge-base/articles/supports-for-children-and-families/hart---sex-trafficking/human-and-sex-trafficking-legislation

## Summary
Connecticut provides various training programs and legislative requirements for addressing human trafficking, particularly sex trafficking. These resources are aimed at different sectors including DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, healthcare professionals, hospitality industry, youth, and the broader community.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry, other professionals, youth, and communities.
- **Training required:** Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement (POST Certified), Emergency Medical Services (EMS) training, Medical Providers/Emergency Medicine training, Hotels, Motels, and Lodging training, Child Trafficking of Boys, Trafficking of LGBQ/GNCT Children, Introduction to Labor Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Labor Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement (POST Certified), Child Trafficking: What It Is, How to Recognize It, and What to Do.
- **Significant legislation:** 2017 Public Act 17-32, 2016 Public Act 16-71, 2015 Public Act 15-195, 2014 Public Act 14-186, 2013 Public Act 13-166, 2012 Public Act 12-141, 2011 Public Act 11-180, 2010 Public Act 10-115.
- **Guest transaction records:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.
- **Training for hotels:** Annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns.

## Enforcement and Reporting
- State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- Ongoing monitoring of efforts to combat trafficking is required.
- Mandatory data reporting is part of the legislative requirements.
- A statewide oversight and monitoring body may be established.

## Significance
Connecticut's comprehensive approach includes both preventive measures through training and legislative reforms aimed at holding offenders accountable. The state's efforts are crucial in addressing human trafficking, particularly sex trafficking, ensuring that victims receive necessary support and that perpetrators face justice.

### Summary of Findings:
- **Training Coverage:** Extensive training programs for various sectors to enhance awareness and response.
- **Legislative Framework:** Robust laws expanding protections for trafficking victims and penalizing offenders.
- **Guest Records Requirement:** Mandates for hotels to maintain transaction records, aiding in investigation and prevention.

Connecticut's proactive measures demonstrate a commitment to combating human trafficking comprehensively, from education to legislation.
