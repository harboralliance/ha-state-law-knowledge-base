Based on the information provided in the two sources, here are key points about human trafficking and what businesses need to do:

1. Human trafficking is a serious crime that affects both criminal and civil law.

2. The California Department of Justice (CRD) enforces California's human trafficking laws and receives complaints about it.

3. Survivors may be eligible for civil remedies under state law.

4. Businesses must post specific notices regarding human trafficking:

   - Hotels, motels, bed & breakfasts: As of April 1, 2013
   - Other establishments: May need to post as well
   
5. The notice must:
   - Be posted in English and Spanish 
   - Include hotline numbers for help/reporting
   - Provide a number that victims can text for services/support (as required by SB 225)
   
6. Non-compliance with posting requirements can result in fines.

7. There are specific language posting requirements for businesses located in certain counties due to federal voting rights laws.

8. The notice must be available for download from the California Attorney General's website.

9. Businesses need to ensure compliance, especially if they operate in multiple languages or serve diverse communities.

So in summary, while it's a complex issue with civil and criminal penalties, the main requirement is that hotels/motels post a specific notice about human trafficking in English/Spanish (and potentially additional languages) on their premises. Non-compliance can result in fines.
