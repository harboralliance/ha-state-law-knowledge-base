## Summary

The State of California requires certain businesses, including hotels and motels, to provide training to their employees regarding human trafficking awareness. Specifically, Senate Bill 970 (SB 970) mandates that hotel and motel employers provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training and education regarding human trafficking awareness to each employee who is likely to interact or come into contact with victims of human trafficking and who is employed as of July 1, 2019. Employers must also provide such training to new employees within six months of their employment in that role.

In addition to this training requirement, hotels and motels are required to post a notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking of telephone hotline numbers to seek help or report unlawful activity. This notice must be posted in English and Spanish, and may also need to be posted in a third language if the business is located in certain counties that are subject to the language assistance provisions of the federal Voting Rights Act.

The Human Trafficking Model Notice can be downloaded from the California Department of Justice website, with options available for posting in various languages including English, Spanish, and several additional languages.

## Key Points

1. **Training Requirement**: Hotel and motel employers must provide 20 minutes of training on human trafficking awareness to employees who will interact with victims.
2. **Posting Requirement**: Businesses must post a notice with hotline numbers in English and Spanish. Some businesses may need to post in a third language as well.
3. **Enforcement**: Failure to comply with these requirements can result in penalties.
