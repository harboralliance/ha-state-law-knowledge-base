The bill being discussed, which is referred to as "SB1422," is a proposed law that aims to strengthen measures against human trafficking in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. The key provisions of the bill include:

1. **Training Requirement**: Beginning June 1, 2020, employers (i.e., owners or operators) of lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops must provide their employees with training on identifying signs of human trafficking and protocols for reporting suspected cases to the appropriate authorities. This training must be completed within six months of starting employment in this role and every two years thereafter if still employed.

2. **Training Content**: The training program can either be created by the employer or provided by a third party, as long as it includes:
   - A definition of human trafficking.
   - Information on how to identify individuals most at risk for trafficking.
   - Differentiations between labor and sex trafficking as they relate to hospitality businesses.
   - Guidance on employees' roles in reporting and responding to instances of trafficking.

3. **Departmental Curriculum**: The Department of Human Services is tasked with developing a standard curriculum that will be used by establishments not creating their own program. This curriculum must meet the same minimum requirements as specified above.

4. **Penalties for Non-Compliance**: If a unit of local government or law enforcement agency discovers a violation of this act, they can issue a reasonable notice of non-compliance. If the employer fails to correct the issue within 30 days of receiving the notice, the Attorney General or State's Attorney can bring a civil action against the employer for a fine not exceeding $1,500 per offense.

The bill is set to take effect on January 1, 2026. This legislation aims at enhancing the awareness and responsiveness of hospitality industry workers towards identifying and reporting human trafficking, thereby potentially reducing instances of exploitation in these sectors.
