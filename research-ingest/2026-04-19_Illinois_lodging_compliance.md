**Title: SB1422 - Human Trafficking Recognition Training for Employees in Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops**

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026

### **Introduction**
This bill requires employers operating lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide their employees with training on recognizing human trafficking and protocols for reporting observed incidents to the appropriate authorities. The training must be completed within six months of employment in such a role and every two years thereafter.

### **Definitions**
The bill defines key terms including:
- **Department:** The Department of Human Services.
- **Employee:** A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer:** A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** The deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty for forced labor or services, commercial sex, or exploitation.
- **Lodging Establishment:** An establishment classified as a hotel or motel in the 2017 North American Industry Classification System under code 721110 or a casino hotel under code 721120.
- **Restaurant:** A business primarily engaged in selling ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption.
- **Truck Stop:** An establishment intended to provide services to the trucking industry, including fuel and food, showers, repair services, and parking.

### **Human Trafficking Recognition Training**
Employers are required to provide training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols. The training must be completed within six months of employment in such a role and every two years thereafter. The training should cover:
1. Definitions of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
2. Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
3. Differences between labor and sex trafficking as it relates to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
4. Roles of employees in reporting and responding to human trafficking incidents.

### **Curriculum Development**
The Department of Human Services is responsible for developing a standardized curriculum for human trafficking recognition training. This curriculum must be used by employers that do not develop their own training programs. The Department's curriculum should include the same essential elements as the employer-driven program and may consult the U.S. Department of Justice for guidance.

### **Penalties**
Employers who fail to comply with this Act may face penalties:
1. Upon discovery of a violation, a unit of local government or law enforcement agency will provide a reasonable notice of noncompliance.
2. If the employer does not rectify the violation within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney can bring a civil action against the employer.
3. An employer that violates this Act is guilty of a business offense and may be fined up to $1,500 for each offense.

### **Effective Date**
This bill takes effect on January 1, 2026.

---

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and response capabilities of employees in the hospitality and transportation industries regarding human trafficking, thereby potentially preventing and addressing instances of exploitation.
