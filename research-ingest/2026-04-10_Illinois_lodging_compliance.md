The bill, **SB1422**, aims to improve awareness and prevention of human trafficking in hospitality businesses by requiring mandatory human trafficking recognition training for employees in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. Below is a summary of the key provisions:

### Definitions
- **Department**: The Department of Human Services.
- **Employee**: A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer**: A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human trafficking**: Deprivation or violation of personal liberty with intent to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell for commercial sex, or exploit in obscene matter.
- **Lodging Establishment**: An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the 2017 North American Industry Classification System code 721110, and a casino hotel under code 721120.
- **Restaurant**: Any business primarily engaged in the sale of ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption comprising at least 51% of total sales (excluding liquor).
- **Truck Stop**: An establishment providing services to the trucking industry.

### Human Trafficking Recognition Training
- Employers must provide training to their employees on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols.
- Training is required to be completed within 6 months after initial employment and every 2 years thereafter if still employed by the employer.
- The training must be at least 20 minutes in duration.

### Human Trafficking Recognition Curriculum
- Employers can use their own program or a third-party's, provided it meets minimum requirements (definition of human trafficking, risk factors, labor vs. sex trafficking, and employee roles).
- The Department will develop an approved curriculum if an employer does not administer its own program.
- This Department-curriculum must include the same minimum requirements as outlined for employers using their own programs.

### Penalties
- A unit of local government or law enforcement agency can monitor compliance with the Act and issue reasonable notices to employers.
- If a violation is not corrected within 30 days, the Attorney General or State’s Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer.
- Employers found in violation are guilty of a business offense and may be fined up to $1,500 for each offense.

### Effective Date
The bill takes effect on January 1, 2026.

This legislation seeks to enhance employee awareness and responsibility within hospitality businesses to prevent human trafficking by ensuring they are equipped with the necessary knowledge and protocols.
