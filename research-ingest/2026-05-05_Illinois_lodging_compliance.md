**Title:** Illinois Human Trafficking Recognition Training Act (Public Act 101-18)

**Effective Date:** June 20, 2019

**Summary:**

This legislation is designed to address human trafficking in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. It mandates that these entities provide their employees with training on recognizing human trafficking and reporting incidents to the appropriate authorities.

**Key Components:**

1. **Definitions:**
   - The Act defines key terms including "human trafficking," "lodging establishment," "restaurant," and "truck stop."

2. **Training Requirement:**
   - Starting June 1, 2020, employers in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops must provide their employees with training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols.
   - Employees must complete this training within six months of beginning employment and every two years thereafter if they remain employed.

3. **Training Curriculum:**
   - Employers can use their own human trafficking training program or adopt one provided by a third party, as long as the program includes specific elements such as:
     1. A definition of human trafficking.
     2. Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
     3. The difference between labor and sex trafficking in relation to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
     4. Guidance on employee roles in reporting and responding to human trafficking incidents.

   - The Department of Human Services is responsible for developing a standardized curriculum that must be used by entities not providing their own program. This curriculum must also include the required elements mentioned above.

4. **Penalties:**
   - Local governments or law enforcement agencies can monitor compliance with this act and issue reasonable notices to employers regarding non-compliance.
   - If a violation is not corrected within 30 days of notice, the Attorney General or State’s Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer. Violations carry a fine of up to $1,500 per offense.

**Effective Date:**
- The Act becomes effective on January 1, 2026.

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and response capabilities among employees in key industries related to human trafficking, thereby potentially preventing and mitigating such incidents.
