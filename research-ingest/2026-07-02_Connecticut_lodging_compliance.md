# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at combating human trafficking, particularly sex trafficking. The state requires various professionals, including DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hotel industry employees, and youth, to undergo mandatory training on human trafficking. Additionally, hotels are required to keep guest transactions and receipts for six months. Connecticut's laws address the crimes of human trafficking and provide protections for victims.

## Key Legislation
1. **2017 Public Act 17-32**: Expands the crime of human trafficking by broadening conditions under which it is committed.
2. **2016 Public Act 16-71**: Requires documentation of training, investigations of missing children, and other activities related to human trafficking.
3. **2015 Public Act 15-195**: Expands protections for victims by allowing the erasure of juvenile police and court records in certain cases.
4. **2014 Public Act 14-186**: Provides training to law enforcement on human trafficking and creates multidisciplinary teams.
5. **2013 Public Act 13-166**: Aligns with the federal Trafficking Victims Protection Act, making human trafficking a class C felony if committed by someone younger than 18.
6. **2012 Public Act 12-141**: Defines commercial sexual exploitation of a minor as a class C felony.
7. **2011 Public Act 11-180**: Requires reporting suspected abuse or neglect to DCF upon the arrest of a youth for prostitution.
8. **2010 Public Act 10-115**: Provides a safe harbor for exploited children, raising the age for prostitution and presuming coercion in prosecutions.

## Training Requirements
- **Mandatory Training**: DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hotel industry employees, and youth must undergo mandatory training on human trafficking.
- **Hotel Industry**: Hotels are required to provide annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees and keep guest transactions and receipts for six months.

## Enforcement
- **Documentation**: State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and prosecution cases.
- **Data Reporting**: There is a requirement for mandatory data reporting.

Connecticut's efforts in combating human trafficking reflect a proactive and comprehensive approach, leveraging legislation, training, and public awareness to protect vulnerable populations.
