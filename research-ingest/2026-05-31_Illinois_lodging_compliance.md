**Title**: Human Trafficking Recognition Training for Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops

**Act Number**: P.A. 102-324

**Effective Date**: January 1, 2026

**Purpose**:
This Act requires employers in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide their employees with training on recognizing human trafficking and reporting observed instances of human trafficking. It also establishes penalties for non-compliance.

### **Definitions**
- **Department**: The Department of Human Services.
- **Employee**: A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who interacts regularly with the public (e.g., receptionists, housekeeping staff).
- **Employer**: A person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking**: The deprivation or violation of personal liberty to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell individuals for commercial sex, or exploit them in obscene matter.
- **Lodging Establishment**: An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the 2017 North American Industry Classification System.
- **Restaurant**: A business primarily engaged in the sale of ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption.
- **Truck Stop**: An establishment providing services to the trucking industry.

### **Human Trafficking Recognition Training**
Starting June 1, 2020:
- Employers must provide training to their employees on recognizing human trafficking and reporting it. This training must be completed within six months of starting employment in such a role.
- Employees must complete this training every two years if they continue to work for the same employer.

### **Human Trafficking Recognition Training Curriculum**
- Employers can use their own program or a third-party program that includes:
  - A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
  - Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
  - Differences between human trafficking for labor and sex within lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
  - Guidance on employees' roles in reporting and responding to human trafficking.

- The Department is required to develop a curriculum that must include all the above elements. This curriculum must be available for use by establishments that do not administer their own program.

### **Penalties**
- A unit of local government or law enforcement agency can monitor compliance with this Act.
- Upon discovery of a violation, they must provide a reasonable notice of non-compliance. If the employer does not correct the issue within 30 days, they face a civil penalty up to $1,500 per offense.
- If the issue remains unresolved after 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney can bring a civil action against the employer.

### **Effective Date**
This Act takes effect on January 1, 2026.
