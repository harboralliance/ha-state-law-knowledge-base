# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has implemented comprehensive legislation and training programs to combat human trafficking, including both sex and labor. The state requires various professionals and community members to undergo specific training, and it mandates that hotels, motels, and similar lodging provide annual trafficking awareness campaigns and keep guest transactions for at least six months. Additionally, Connecticut has enacted several public acts aimed at strengthening protections for victims of human trafficking.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry, other professionals, youth, and the community.
- **Training required:** Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut for School Settings, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement - Police Officer's Standards and Training (POST) Certified, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Emergency Medical Services (EMS), Child Trafficking Training for Medical Providers/Emergency Medicine, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Hotels/Motels, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Out of Home Care Providers.
- **Significant legislation:** Public Act 17-32, Public Act 16-71, Public Act 15-195, Public Act 14-186, Public Act 13-166, Public Act 12-141, Public Act 11-180, Public Act 10-115.

## Enforcement and Reporting
Connecticut requires state attorneys and local law enforcement to document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and cases referred for prosecution. The state also mandates mandatory data reporting for human trafficking cases. Additionally, hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide annual trafficking awareness campaigns and keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months.

## Victims' Services
Victims of human trafficking receive services from the Department of Public Health (DPH) that are comparable to those provided to certain sexual assault victims. The Office of Victim Services (OVS) may waive the two-year limitation on crime victim compensation applications for minors who are victims of human trafficking.

## Ongoing Monitoring and Oversight
Connecticut has established a statewide oversight and monitoring body to combat trafficking efforts.

## Effective Date
All the mentioned legislation and training requirements were effective as of February 8, 2025.
