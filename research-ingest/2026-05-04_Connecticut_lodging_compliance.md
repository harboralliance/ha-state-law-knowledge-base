## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation aimed at preventing and addressing human trafficking, including both sex and labor trafficking. The state requires annual training for various groups, such as DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and others. Legislation also mandates that hotels, motels, and lodging provide annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable Industry:** Hotels, motels, inns, similar lodging; Hospitality industry
- **Training Required:** Annual trafficking training for DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals.
- **Signage Required:** Not specified.
- **Reporting Required:** Yes. State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- **Enforcement Agency:** Department of Children and Families (DCF), local law enforcement
- **Penalties:** Class C felony for knowingly purchasing advertising space for an ad that includes a depiction of a minor engaged in commercial sexual exploitation.

The state aims to protect victims and prevent exploitation through comprehensive and coordinated efforts.
