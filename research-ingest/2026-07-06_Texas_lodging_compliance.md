### Summary of Texas Human Trafficking Training Requirements for Commercial Lodging Establishments

**Overview:**
The Texas Legislature has enacted House Bill 390, which requires the Office of the Attorney General (OAG) to approve training for commercial lodging establishments regarding human trafficking. This training is mandatory for all commercial lodging establishments in the State of Texas, regardless of size.

### Key Points:

1. **Training Requirements:**
   - Commercial lodging establishments must provide annual human trafficking training to their employees.
   - Training must be completed by new employees not later than 90 days after being hired.
   - Employees may not be disciplined, retaliated against, or discriminated against for making a good faith report of suspected human trafficking.

2. **Training Content:**
   - The training covers how and why human trafficking occurs in the hospitality industry.
   - It discusses commercial lodging's role in addressing sex trafficking.
   - The training identifies different types of hotel contract workers who are vulnerable to labor trafficking.
   - It guides staff on their roles in reporting and responding to human trafficking incidents.

3. **Signage:**
   - Commercial lodging establishments must display specific signage related to human trafficking.

4. **Certification & Compliance:**
   - Approved training providers must provide a certificate of completion to every employee who completes the training.
   - Operators must retain copies of these certificates for each year's training and make them available for review and inspection by the OAG and local, state, and federal law enforcement.

The Texas Attorney General's Office has established a comprehensive training program to help commercial lodging establishments identify and respond to human trafficking. These resources are designed to ensure that all employees, including those in hospitality positions, are aware of the risks and know how to report any suspicions of human trafficking.
