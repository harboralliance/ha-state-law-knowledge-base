**Title:** An Act Concerning Human Trafficking Training for Employees of Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops

**Section 1: Short Title**
This Act is known as the "Human Trafficking Prevention and Awareness Act."

**Section 2: Effective Date**
This Act takes effect January 1, 2026.

**Section 3: Human Trafficking Training for Employees**

**(a) Requirement for Training**
Beginning June 1, 2020, an employer of a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop shall provide its employees with training in the recognition of human trafficking and protocols for reporting observed human trafficking to the appropriate authority.

**(b) Timeframe for Training**
Employees must complete the training within 6 months after beginning employment in such role with the employer and every 2 years thereafter if still employed by the employer.

**(c) Duration of Training**
The training shall be at least 20 minutes in duration.

**Section 4: Human Trafficking Training Curriculum**

**(a) Employer's Option**
An employer may use its own human trafficking training program or that of a third party and be in full compliance with this Act if the human trafficking training program includes, at a minimum:

1. A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
2. Guidance on how to identify individuals who are most at risk for human trafficking.
3. The difference between human trafficking for purposes of labor and for purposes of sex as it relates to lodging establishments, restaurants, or truck stops.
4. Guidance on the role of lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

**(b) Department's Curriculum**
The Department shall develop a curriculum for an approved human trafficking training recognition program which shall be used by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop that does not administer its own human trafficking recognition program as described in subsection (a). The human trafficking training recognition program developed by the Department shall include, at a minimum:

1. A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
2. Guidance on how to identify individuals who are most at risk for human trafficking.
3. The difference between human trafficking for purposes of labor and for purposes of sex as it relates to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
4. Guidance on the role of lodging establishment, restaurant, and truck stop employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

The Department may consult the United States Department of Justice for the human trafficking recognition training program developed under this subsection.

The Department shall develop and publish the human trafficking recognition training program described in this subsection no later than July 1, 2020.

**Section 5: Penalties**

**(a) Local Government's Role**
A unit of local government regulating an employer or a law enforcement agency with jurisdiction over an employer may, in the course of its regulatory or enforcement duties, monitor and enforce compliance with this Act. Upon the discovery of a violation of this Act, the unit of local government or law enforcement agency shall provide the employer with a reasonable notice of noncompliance that informs the employer that if the employer does not cure the violation within 30 days after notice, the employer is subject to a civil penalty described in subsection (b).

**(b) Verification and Legal Action**
If the unit of local government regulating an employer or a law enforcement agency with jurisdiction over an employer verifies that the violation was not corrected within the cure period described in subsection (a), the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action against that employer. An employer that violates this Act is guilty of a business offense and may be fined not more than $1,500 for each offense.

**Section 6: Definitions**

1. **Employee**: A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
2. **Employer**: A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
3. **Human Trafficking**: The deprivation or violation of the personal liberty of another with the intent to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell the individual for commercial sex, or exploit the individual in obscene matter.
4. **Lodging Establishment**: An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the 2017 North American Industry Classification System code 721110, and an establishment classified as a casino hotel under the 2017 North American Industry Classification System code 721120.
5. **Restaurant**: Any business primarily engaged in the sale of ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption comprising at least 51% of total sales, excluding the sale of liquor.
6. **Truck Stop**: An establishment intended to provide services to the trucking industry, including selling fuel and food, providing showers, offering repair services, and ample room for long-haul truck drivers to park and rest.

**Section 7: Effective Date**
This Act takes effect January 1, 2026.
