The provided text is a draft for an Illinois state law regarding human trafficking recognition training in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. Here are the key points:

1. **Definitions**: The law defines terms such as "employee," "employer," "human trafficking," "lodging establishment," "restaurant," and "truck stop."

2. **Training Requirement**:
   - Starting June 1, 2020, employers must provide training to their employees in the recognition of human trafficking and protocols for reporting it.
   - The training must be completed within 6 months after beginning employment and every 2 years thereafter if still employed by the employer.
   - Training duration should be at least 20 minutes.

3. **Training Curriculum**:
   - Employers can use their own program or a third-party program that meets specified requirements.
   - The Department of Human Services (DHS) is tasked with developing a standard curriculum that all establishments must follow if they don't administer their own program.
   - The DHS curriculum must include definitions, risk identification guidelines, differences in labor and sex trafficking, and guidance on reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

4. **Penalties**:
   - Local government units or law enforcement agencies can monitor compliance with the act and issue reasonable notices of noncompliance.
   - If a violation is not corrected within 30 days after notice, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer.
   - Employers found to be in violation are guilty of a business offense and may be fined up to $1,500 per offense.

5. **Effective Date**: The law takes effect on January 1, 2026.

### Key Points for Implementation:
- **Training Providers**: Establishments can choose from their own programs or the state-approved curriculum.
- **Frequency of Training**: Initial training within 6 months and every 2 years thereafter if still employed.
- **Duration**: Minimum 20-minute training session.
- **Penalties**: Clear enforcement mechanism through local authorities and potential legal action by the Attorney General.

### Potential Impact:
- The law aims to enhance awareness and preparedness among employees in key industries related to human trafficking, potentially reducing incidents.
- It ensures a standardized approach to training across different establishments, improving compliance and effectiveness of anti-trafficking efforts.

This legislation is an important step towards addressing human trafficking in these sectors and aligns with broader national initiatives aimed at preventing and combating modern-day slavery.
