## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at addressing human trafficking, including both sex and labor trafficking. The state requires mandatory annual training for certain groups such as DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other relevant professionals. Training programs cover various aspects of identifying, responding to, and preventing human trafficking.

## Extracted Requirements
- Applicable industry: Child protective services, foster care, education, law enforcement, healthcare, hospitality, other professional sectors.
- Training required: Annual training for DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other relevant professionals. Specific courses include "Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut," "Child Trafficking Training for Medical Providers/Emergency Medicine," and others.
- Signage required: Not explicitly mentioned.
- Reporting required: State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc. Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.
- Enforcement actions: Mandatory data reporting, statewide oversight and monitoring body.

## Summary
Connecticut's approach to human trafficking includes robust legislative measures and comprehensive training programs. The state mandates annual training for various professionals to enhance their ability to identify and respond to cases of human trafficking. Additionally, the law requires documentation of training activities, investigations, and referrals, as well as mandatory data reporting and oversight mechanisms. These efforts aim to prevent, detect, and prosecute human trafficking effectively within the state.
