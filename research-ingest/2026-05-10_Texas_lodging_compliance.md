The provided sources outline the requirements and resources for human trafficking training in the hospitality industry, specifically commercial lodging establishments. Below is a summary of the key points from these sources:

### Requirements:
1. **Annual Training**: 
   - Commercial lodging establishments with 10 or more rooms are required to provide annual human trafficking training.
   - All lodging establishments are encouraged to provide this training regardless of size.

2. **Training Content**:
   - Training must cover identifying and responding to human trafficking within the hospitality industry.
   - It should include guidance on how to recognize individuals at risk for trafficking, contact information for reporting trafficking, and information about required human trafficking signage.

3. **Certification**:
   - Employees must complete training within 90 days of hire or before beginning their job duties.
   - Annual refresher training is mandatory for all employees.
   - Approved training providers must issue a certificate to each employee completing the course, which must include the date and title of the course.

4. **Signage**:
   - Commercial lodging establishments must display required human trafficking signage.

5. **Penalties for Non-Compliance**:
   - Employees who make good faith reports of suspected human trafficking cannot be disciplined, retaliated against, or discriminated against.

### Training Providers and Resources:
1. **Office of the Attorney General (OAG)**: 
   - The OAG provides a list of approved training videos on their website.
   - These include training from various organizations such as Etactics, Unbound Now, Businesses Ending Slavery and Trafficking, Extended Stay America, Marriott International, ECPAT-USA, Refugee Services of Texas, PBJ Learning, Compliance Learning Solutions, among others.

2. **Free Training**:
   - Some providers offer free training, including those from Etactics, Unbound Now (for Spanish), and ECPAT-USA.

3. **Paid Training**:

4. **Delivery Methods**:
   - Training can be delivered online, via internal websites, or in-person.

### Summary of Key Points:
- The Texas Legislature passed House Bill 390, mandating annual human trafficking training for commercial lodging establishments with 10 or more rooms.
- Training must cover identifying and responding to human trafficking within the hospitality industry.
- Employees must complete initial training within 90 days of hire and receive an annual refresher.
- Approved training providers issue certificates, and non-compliance can result in penalties such as not disciplining employees who report suspected trafficking.
