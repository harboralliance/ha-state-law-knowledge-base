## Summary
Connecticut provides comprehensive training and legislation to combat human trafficking. The state requires annual basic training for Child Protective Services staff, while other professionals such as foster parents, schools, law enforcement, hospitals, and the hospitality industry are offered specialized trainings. Additionally, Connecticut has enacted several public acts addressing human trafficking, including expanded protections for victims, enhanced penalties, and mandatory data reporting.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Child Protective Services, Foster parents, Schools, Law enforcement, Hospitals, Hospitality industry, Other professionals, Youth, Community
- **Training required:** Annual basic training for Child Protective Services staff; specialized trainings for foster parents, schools, law enforcement, hospitals, hospitality industry, and other professionals.
- **Signage required:** Not explicitly mentioned in the provided sources.
- **Reporting required:** Mandatory data reporting as per legislation.
- **Enforcement agency:** Department of Children and Families (DCF), Law enforcement agencies.
- **Penalties:** Expanding the conditions under which human trafficking is committed when the victim is a minor, creating affirmative defense for minors, vacating records, and forfeiting properties.
- **Effective date:** The laws mentioned have varying effective dates starting from 2010.
