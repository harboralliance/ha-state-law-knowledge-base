## Analysis of California Human Trafficking Laws and Requirements

### Overview:
California has enacted several laws aimed at preventing and addressing human trafficking. The key legislation includes Senate Bill 970 (SB 970), which mandates training for employees of hotels and motels.

### Key Legislation:

1. **Senate Bill 970 (SB 970):**
   - **Effective Date:** January 1, 2020
   - **Requirement:** By January 1, 2020, hotel and motel employers must provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training and education regarding human trafficking awareness to each employee who is likely to interact or come into contact with victims of human trafficking.
   - **Scope:** Applies to employees employed as of July 1, 2019, and any new employees within six months of their employment in that role.

2. **Senate Bill 225 (SB 225):**
   - **Effective Date:** January 1, 2018
   - **Requirement:** The model notice provided by the Attorney General must include a specified number that victims can text for services and support.
   - **Scope:** Applies to businesses required to post the model notice under Senate Bill 1193.

3. **Assembly Bill 260 (AB 260):**
   - **Effective Date:** January 1, 2018
   - **Requirement:** Hotels, motels, and bed and breakfast inns are added to the list of specified businesses required to post the model notice under Senate Bill 1193.
   - **Scope:** Applies to establishments located as of April 1, 2013.

### Training Requirements:

- **Content:** The training must cover human trafficking awareness, including recognizing signs of human trafficking and how to report suspected cases.
- **Format:** Training can be conducted in-person or through other effective interactive methods.
- **Duration:** Employees must receive at least 20 minutes of such training.

### Notification Posting:

- **Businesses Required to Post:**
  - Hotels
  - Motels
  - Bed and breakfast inns

- **Posting Requirements:**
  - Must post the Human Trafficking Model Notice in English and Spanish.
  - For businesses located in specific counties, a third language posting may be required. These counties are subject to federal Voting Rights Act language assistance provisions.

### Legal Penalties:

Failure to comply with these requirements can result in penalties for businesses, including fines and other legal consequences.

### Enforcement and Compliance:

- **Enforcement:** The California Attorney General's Office oversees compliance with these laws.
- **Reporting:** Victims of human trafficking are encouraged to report incidents through various hotline numbers provided in the notice.

### Summary:
California has enacted comprehensive laws requiring training for hotel and motel employees on human trafficking awareness and posting mandatory notices about human trafficking resources. These measures aim to increase public awareness, provide support to victims, and hold businesses accountable for preventing human trafficking.

### Recommendations:

1. **Training Providers:** Ensure that training providers are qualified and have up-to-date information on human trafficking.
2. **Employee Engagement:** Encourage employees to actively participate in the training process to ensure full understanding of their responsibilities.
3. **Regular Reviews:** Conduct regular reviews and updates of the training program to reflect new developments in human trafficking laws and practices.

By adhering to these requirements, California can play a crucial role in combating human trafficking within its hospitality industry.
