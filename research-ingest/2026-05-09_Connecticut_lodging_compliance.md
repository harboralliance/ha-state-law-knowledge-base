Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has a comprehensive set of trainings and legislation aimed at combating human trafficking, including sex and labor trafficking. The state requires DCF staff and foster parents to have annual basic training on human trafficking. Additionally, there are specialized training programs for law enforcement, emergency medical services, and various professional groups. Legislation such as Public Act 17-32 (2017) and Public Act 15-195 (2015) focus on expanding protections for victims, broadening conditions under which human trafficking is committed, and providing specific services to victims.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry, other professionals.
- **Training required:** Annual basic training for DCF staff and foster parents; specialized training for law enforcement, emergency medical services, hospital and medical staff, hotel and motel workers, and other professionals. Training programs include Introduction to Human Trafficking, Specialized Awareness Campaigns, Multidisciplinary Teams, etc.
- **Significant legislation:** Public Act 17-32 (2017), Public Act 15-195 (2015), among others.

## Significance
Connecticut's approach to combating human trafficking includes both preventive measures through education and specialized training programs, as well as legal frameworks designed to protect victims and hold perpetrators accountable. The state's efforts demonstrate a comprehensive strategy aimed at addressing human trafficking from various angles.
