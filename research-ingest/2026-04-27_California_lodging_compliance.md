### Overview

The regulation in question is **Senate Bill 970** (SB 970), which requires hotel and motel employers to provide human trafficking awareness training to their employees. The bill aims to enhance the ability of workers to identify and respond to instances of human trafficking within their workplaces.

### Key Points:

1. **Training Requirement:**
   - **Effective Date:** By January 1, 2020.
   - **Duration:** At least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training.
   - **Target Employees:** Those likely to interact with victims of human trafficking.

2. **Exemptions:**
   - Employees who have already received the required training on or before January 1, 2019, do not need additional training.

3. **Penalties:**
   - Failure to comply can result in fines and penalties.

4. **Purpose:**
   - To increase awareness among employees and improve their ability to prevent and respond to human trafficking.

### Regulatory Authority:

The regulation is enforced by the California Attorney General's office, which has jurisdiction over civil rights violations, including those related to human trafficking.

### Enforcement Mechanism:

- **Reporting:** Complaints can be filed with the California Civil Rights Commission.
- **Investigation:** The Attorney General’s office investigates complaints and enforces compliance through legal means if necessary.
- **Public Awareness Campaigns:** The state may also launch public awareness campaigns to educate the public about human trafficking.

### Public Awareness:

The bill also mandates that hotels and motels post a notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking about available telephone hotline numbers for assistance or reporting unlawful activity. These notices must be posted in English and Spanish, and in additional languages if required by federal law.

### Conclusion:

Senate Bill 970 is a proactive measure to combat human trafficking by ensuring that hotel and motel employees are trained to recognize and respond appropriately to instances of trafficking. The regulation demonstrates the state's commitment to protecting its citizens and fostering a safer environment.
