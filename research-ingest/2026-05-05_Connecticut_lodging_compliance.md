## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive laws and training programs in place to address human trafficking, including both sex and labor. The state requires various professionals, particularly those working with children and families, to undergo mandatory training on human trafficking. Legislation such as Public Act 17-32 emphasizes the crime of human trafficking and provides protections for victims.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Child protective services staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry, other professionals.
- **Training required:** Annual basic training on human trafficking for DCF staff; specific trainings for foster parents, school settings, law enforcement (POST Certified), emergency medical services, medical providers, hotels, motels, and lodging businesses.
- **Signage required:** No specific signage requirements are mentioned in the source material.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc. Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months.
- **Enforcement/Compliance:** Ongoing monitoring of efforts to combat trafficking and mandatory data reporting.
- **Oversight/Monitoring Body:** A statewide oversight and monitoring body is established.

## Enforcement/Compliance
Connecticut's laws require various professionals and institutions to report incidents of human trafficking. State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and cases referred for prosecution. Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging are required to provide annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees and keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months.

## Oversight/Monitoring Body
A statewide oversight and monitoring body ensures the implementation and effectiveness of anti-human trafficking efforts across the state. This includes tracking progress, reviewing compliance, and making recommendations for improvement.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's approach to human trafficking is well-structured with mandatory training requirements for various professionals, comprehensive legislation, reporting obligations, and a dedicated monitoring body. The state demonstrates a proactive stance in combating human trafficking through these measures.
