## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs addressing human trafficking. The state requires mandatory annual training for various professionals, including DCF staff, foster parents, school personnel, law enforcement, emergency medical services, and hotel staff. Public acts such as 17-32, 16-71, and others aim to strengthen protections for victims, expand the definition of human trafficking, and mandate data reporting.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry**: DCF staff, foster parents, schools, law enforcement, emergency medical services, hotel staff.
- **Training required**: Annual mandatory training for various professionals (DCF staff, foster parents, school personnel, law enforcement, EMS).
- **Signage required**: Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns annually.
- **Reporting required**: Statewide oversight and monitoring body, mandatory data reporting.
- **Enforcement agency**: Office of Victim Services (OVS), local law enforcement.
- **Penalties and Conditions**: Expanding the crime of human trafficking for minors, forfeiture properties, affirmative defense for minors.

## Effective Implementation
Connecticut's legislation ensures a coordinated approach to combating human trafficking through comprehensive training, mandated reporting, and ongoing monitoring. The state's efforts aim to protect victims, prevent future occurrences, and strengthen protections for those affected by this heinous crime.
