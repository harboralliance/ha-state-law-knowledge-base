## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs in place to address human trafficking, both for sex and labor. The state requires mandatory annual training for various groups, including Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and others. Hotels, motels, and lodging businesses must provide annual training and keep guest transaction records for at least six months.

These laws address various aspects of human trafficking, including age requirements for prostitution, mandatory training for law enforcement and service providers, expansion of protections for victims, wiretapping authorizations, victim compensation applications, and prevention measures.

## Legal Framework
 Connecticut's legal framework includes:
- Expansion of the crime of human trafficking to include minors.
- Requirement for DPH to provide services similar to those provided to sexual assault victims.
- Expansion of conditions under which a court must order the erasure of juvenile records.
- Authorization for specific circumstances where OVS may waive limitations on crime victim compensation applications.

## Public Awareness and Prevention
 Connecticut has enacted measures to increase public awareness and prevention efforts, including mandatory data reporting and the creation of a statewide oversight and monitoring body.

Overall, Connecticut has robust legislation and training programs aimed at combating human trafficking, demonstrating its commitment to protecting its citizens.
