## Summary
Connecticut offers various trainings for providers, foster parents, and communities to combat human trafficking. The state also has several pieces of legislation designed to protect victims and prevent trafficking.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Child protective services, foster care, education, law enforcement, healthcare, hospitality, other professionals, youth.
- **Training required:** Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut for School Settings, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Emergency Medical Services (EMS), Child Trafficking Training for Medical Providers/Emergency Medicine, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Hotels, Motels, and Lodging, Child Trafficking of Boys, Trafficking of LGBQ/GNCT Children, Introduction to Labor Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Labor Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement (POST Certified), Child Trafficking: What It Is, How to Recognize It, and How to Respond.
- **Signage/Display Requirements:** None explicitly mentioned.
- **Record Keeping Requirements:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.
- **Data Reporting:** Mandatory data reporting.

## Enforcement/Compliance
- State attorneys and local law enforcement are required to document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- The Office of Victim Services (OVS) may waive the two-year limitation on crime victim compensation applications for minors who are victims of human trafficking.

## Effective Date
