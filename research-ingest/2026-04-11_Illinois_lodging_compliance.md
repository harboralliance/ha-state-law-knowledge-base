### Summary

**SB1422 - Human Trafficking Recognition Training Act**

This bill, **SB1422**, requires employers in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide their employees with training on the recognition of human trafficking and protocols for reporting such incidents. The legislation has several key components:

#### Definitions
- **Department**: Department of Human Services.
- **Employee**: A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who interacts with the public regularly.
- **Employer**: A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking**: The deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty for forced labor, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.
- **Lodging Establishment**: An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the North American Industry Classification System.
- **Restaurant**: A business primarily engaged in selling ready-to-eat food with at least 51% of total sales.
- **Truck Stop**: An establishment for truck drivers, providing services such as fuel, food, and parking.

#### Training Requirements
- Employees must receive training on human trafficking recognition within 6 months after their initial employment and every 2 years thereafter.
- The training program must cover the following:
  - Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
  - How to identify individuals at risk for human trafficking.
  - Differences between labor and sex trafficking.
  - Roles of employees in reporting and responding to human trafficking incidents.

#### Curriculum
- Employers can use their own or a third-party training program as long as it includes the required elements.
- The Department must develop a standard curriculum, which must include the same elements as the employer-specific programs. This curriculum must be published by July 1, 2020.

#### Penalties
- If an employer violates the Act and fails to correct the violation within 30 days of receiving notice, a unit of local government or law enforcement agency can issue a reasonable notice of noncompliance.
- The Attorney General or State's Attorney can then bring a civil action against the employer, with fines up to $1,500 per offense.

#### Effective Date
The Act takes effect on January 1, 2026.

### Key Points:
1. **Employee Training**: Requires all employees in specified businesses to undergo regular human trafficking training.
2. **Training Requirements**: Comprehensive coverage of human trafficking definitions, risk identification, and reporting responsibilities.
3. **Curriculum Development**: Mandates the creation of a standardized curriculum by the Department of Human Services.
4. **Enforcement**: Provides for penalties including fines for non-compliance within 30 days.

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and response capabilities among employees in critical industries that can interact with vulnerable individuals, thereby reducing the risk of human trafficking incidents.
