# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Sources
- https://portal.ct.gov/dcf/knowledge-base/articles/supports-for-children-and-families/hart---sex-trafficking/trainings-for-providers
- https://portal.ct.gov/dcf/knowledge-base/articles/supports-for-children-and-families/hart---sex-trafficking/human-and-sex-trafficking-legislation

## Summary
Connecticut has enacted several laws to combat human trafficking, including sex and labor trafficking. The state requires training for various professionals, such as Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, and law enforcement officers. Hotels, motels, and other lodging businesses must also provide annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns. Additionally, Connecticut mandates data reporting on trafficking cases.

## Extracted Requirements
- Applicable industry: Child protective services, foster care, schools, law enforcement, hospitals, hotels/motels/lodging businesses
- Training required:
  - Child Protective Services staff (annual basic and advanced training)
  - Foster parents (three-day training program)
  - Schools (specific classes for various age groups)
  - Law enforcement (various courses including POST certified, EMS, medical providers, and hospitality industry professionals)
  - Hotels/motels/lodging businesses (annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns)
- Signage required: Not explicitly mentioned
- Reporting required: Ongoing data reporting on trafficking cases
- Enforcement: State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- Penalties: Commercial sexual exploitation of a minor is a class C felony. Exploitation by minors requires an affirmative defense.

## Effective Measures
- Multidisciplinary teams to work with human trafficking cases
- Ongoing monitoring and public awareness campaigns
- Expanding protections for victims and waiving limitations on crime victim compensation applications

## Areas for Improvement
- Specific signage requirements in high-risk areas
- More comprehensive data reporting and analysis tools

## Conclusion
Connecticut's efforts in combating human trafficking are comprehensive, covering various industries and requiring specific training. The state also mandates public awareness and ongoing monitoring, but there is room for improvement in terms of signage and data analysis.
