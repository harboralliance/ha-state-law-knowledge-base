## Summary
Connecticut provides various training programs and legislation to combat human trafficking, including sex and labor trafficking. Training is mandatory for Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and others. Legislation includes public acts that address human trafficking, set age restrictions for prostitution, mandate hotel training, expand protections for victims, and establish data reporting requirements.

## Extracted Requirements
- Applicable industry: Child Protective Services, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals

## Relevant Legislation
- **2017 Public Act 17-32**: Expands conditions under which human trafficking is committed when the victim is a minor.
- **2016 Public Act 16-71**: Establishes mandatory training for hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging to provide awareness campaigns annually.
- **2015 Public Act 15-195**: Expands protections for victims of human trafficking, including waiving crime victim compensation applications for minors who are victims of human trafficking.
- **2014 Public Act 14-186**: Provides services to uncared-for human trafficking victims and requires DCF training on human trafficking of minors.
- **2013 Public Act 13-166**: Aligns with federal Trafficking Victims Protection Act, expands conditions under which a court must order the erasure of juvenile records, and creates multidisciplinary teams to work with human trafficking cases.
- **2012 Public Act 12-141**: Defines commercial sexual exploitation of a minor as a class C felony.
- **2011 Public Act 11-180**: Requires reporting suspected abuse or neglect by officers arresting youth for prostitution.
- **2010 Public Act 10-115**: Provides a safe harbor for exploited children and sets age restrictions for prostitution.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's efforts to combat human trafficking are comprehensive, focusing on training professionals, expanding protections for victims, and establishing robust legislation. These measures aim to prevent and address human trafficking in various sectors of the state's economy and society.
