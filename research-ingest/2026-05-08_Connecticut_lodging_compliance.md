## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive laws and training programs aimed at combating human trafficking, particularly sex trafficking. The state requires mandatory annual training for foster parents, Child Protective Services staff, hotels and motels, law enforcement, healthcare providers, and other professionals to identify and respond to human trafficking cases. Legislation also mandates the preservation of guest transactions for 6 months and expands protections for victims.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Foster care services, Child Protective Services, Hotels, Motels, Law enforcement, Healthcare providers, Hospitality industry, Other professionals (youth, community)
- **Training required:** Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement, Child Trafficking Training for Medical Providers/Emergency Medicine, etc.
- **Signage required:** No specific signage requirements mentioned.
- **Reporting required:** Hotels and motels must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months. State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and prosecution cases.
- **Enforcement/Compliance:** Legislation mandates compliance through mandatory annual training and documentation.

## Enforcement/Compliance
Training is mandated annually for various professionals, including foster parents, Child Protective Services staff, hotels and motels, law enforcement, healthcare providers, and other industry professionals. Hotels and motels are required to keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months, and state attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations, and prosecution cases.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's efforts in combating human trafficking through legislation and mandatory training reflect a commitment to preventing and responding to these crimes effectively.
