# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at preventing and addressing human trafficking, including sex and labor trafficking. The state requires mandatory annual training for various groups such as DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other professionals. Additionally, Connecticut's laws include provisions to protect victims of human trafficking, expand the definition of the crime, provide services to victims, and strengthen protections for minors.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable Industry:** DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, other professionals.
- **Training Required:** Annual training on human trafficking definitions, federal and state laws, warning signs, impacts, buyers/exploiters for specific groups like DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, emergency medical services, hotels, motels, and lodging businesses. 
- **Signage Required:** Not explicitly mentioned in the sources.
- **Penalties/Mandates:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training annually and keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months. State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.

## Enforcement and Penalties
- **Penalties:** The laws include provisions for specific crimes related to human trafficking, with penalties ranging from fines to imprisonment.
- **Enforcement:** Ongoing monitoring by a statewide oversight and monitoring body.

## Victim Support
- **Services:** Victims of human trafficking are provided the same services as certain sexual assault victims under existing law. Specific protections are also provided for minors involved in human trafficking.

## Legal Framework
- **Legislation:** Multiple public acts have been passed to address human trafficking, covering various aspects including age restrictions, forfeiture properties, affirmative defense for minors, vacating records, public awareness and prevention, mandatory data reporting, and statewide oversight and monitoring.

## Conclusion
Connecticut has robust laws and training programs to combat human trafficking. These measures aim to protect victims, prevent the crime, and ensure accountability through mandatory training and stringent penalties.
