The provided documents outline requirements for sex trafficking prevention training in the hospitality industry, particularly hotels, motels, and short-term rentals in Minnesota. Here are key points from both sources:

### Legislation and Legal Requirements

1. **Minnesota Statute**: The requirement is codified in Minnesota Statutes section 627H.04, which mandates that all hotel owners, managers, and on-site employees complete sex trafficking prevention training.
   - **New Employees**: Must complete within 90 days of hire.
   - **Existing Staff**: Must retake annually.

2. **Training Content**: The training must cover identifying signs of sex trafficking, responding appropriately, and understanding the legal obligations.

### Training Materials and Formats

1. **Required Materials**:
   - A virtual presentation (video with guided exercises).
   - A Training Companion Guide.
   - A Manager & Owner Training Toolkit.
   - A set of back-of-house posters to be displayed in multiple locations within the establishment.

2. **Availability**: The training is available in English and various other languages, including Spanish, Hmong, and Somali.

### Compliance Requirements

1. **Training Completion**: All required staff must complete the training and have documentation of their completion.
   - This includes filling out a roster of names with dates of completion, which should be kept on file for inspections.
   
2. **Displaying Posters**: The back-of-house posters must be displayed in prominent locations to ensure all employees are aware.

3. **Ongoing Awareness Campaigns**: Managers and owners are required to conduct an ongoing awareness campaign for their staff regarding sex trafficking prevention.

### Exceptions and Exemptions

1. **Exempt Employees**:
   - Employees under 16 years old unless they clean rooms.
   - Employees working exclusively in restaurants or providing catering services (no direct contact with guests).
   
2. **No Direct Contact**: While exemptions are available for employees without direct contact with guests, this does not apply to hotel owners, managers, and operators.

### Additional Information

1. **Assistance**: The Minnesota Department of Health (MDH) provides the training package free of charge and supports compliance efforts.
   - There is no fee for accessing the training.
   - MDH does not provide assistance with printing posters, which must be paid for by licensees.

2. **Updates**: Updates to the training materials are provided annually by November 1st.

3. **Facility Licensing**: Only establishments holding a hotel license are required to provide this training.

### Resources

- For further questions or assistance accessing the training, contact MDH via email at `health.safeharbor@state.mn.us` or phone at 651-201-4500.
  
These requirements aim to ensure that establishments in the hospitality industry are better equipped to identify and respond to signs of sex trafficking, thereby playing a critical role in preventing such exploitation within their communities.
