The bill, which was passed by the Illinois General Assembly and signed into law by Governor J.B. Pritzker, establishes requirements for lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide their employees with training on recognizing human trafficking and protocols for reporting such incidents. The training must be completed within six months of an employee beginning employment in a relevant role and every two years thereafter if still employed by the establishment. Additionally, the law specifies that violations of this requirement could result in fines of up to $1,500 per offense.
The bill defines human trafficking as the deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty with the intent to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell an individual for commercial sex, or exploit them in obscene matter. It also includes definitions for lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops, as well as guidance on how employers can identify individuals who may be at risk of trafficking and report instances of trafficking.
Overall, this bill aims to increase awareness and prevention of human trafficking within the hospitality industry by providing employees with the necessary training and resources to recognize and respond appropriately to potential incidents.
