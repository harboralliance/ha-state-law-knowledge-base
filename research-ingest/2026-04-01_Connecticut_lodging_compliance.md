## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs in place to address human trafficking, including sex trafficking. The state requires annual training for foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and others. Hotels, motels, and lodging businesses must provide trafficking training annually and keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months. Legislation also includes protections for victims, expands the crime of human trafficking, and mandates data reporting.

## Enforcement Measures
- Legislation expands the crime of human trafficking by broadening conditions under which it is committed when the victim is a minor.
- The Department of Public Health (DPH) provides human trafficking victims the same services as sexual assault victims.
- Ongoing monitoring and mandatory data reporting are mandated to combat trafficking.

## Legislation Details
- **Public Act 17-32**: Expands conditions under which human trafficking can be committed when the victim is a minor.
- **Public Act 16-71**: Requires state attorneys and local law enforcement to document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and prosecution cases.
- **Public Act 15-195**: Expands protections for victims, wiretapping authorizations, and compensation for minors.
- **Public Act 14-186**: Provides services to human trafficking victims classified as “uncared for” and creates multidisciplinary teams.
- **Public Act 13-166**: Aligns with the federal Trafficking Victims Protection Act (TVPA), prohibiting certain actions related to commercial sexual exploitation of minors.
- **Public Act 12-141**: Defines commercial sexual exploitation of a minor as a class C felony.
- **Public Act 11-180**: Requires reporting suspected abuse or neglect to the Department of Children and Families upon arrest for prostitution.
- **Public Act 10-115**: Amends statutes to prohibit minors from committing prostitution.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's efforts in combating human trafficking are well underway, with a focus on comprehensive training and legislation. The state continues to strengthen its protections for victims and enforce strict measures against traffickers.
