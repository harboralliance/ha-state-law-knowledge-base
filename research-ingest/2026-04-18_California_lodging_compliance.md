### Source 1: Legislative Requirements and Training

**Content Summary:**
- **Training Requirement:** Hotel and motel employers in California are required to provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training on human trafficking awareness to employees who may interact with victims.
- **Effective Date:** The training must be completed by January 1, 2020, for those employed as of July 1, 2019. New employees must also receive this training within six months of starting employment.
- **Exemption:** Employees who received the training on or before January 1, 2019, are exempt from additional training.

### Source 2: Additional Support and Resources

**Content Summary:**
- **Support for Survivors:** Victims of human trafficking can contact various hotlines including:
  - National Human Trafficking Resource Center: 1-888-373-7888 or TEXT: Be Free
  - California Coalition to Abolish Slavery and Trafficking (CAST): 1-888-KEY-2FRE(EDOM)
- **Criminal Law Enforcement:** Options for survivors can be found on the websites of:
  - State of California Attorney General
  - United States Department of Justice Human Trafficking Prosecution Unit

### Source 3: Model Notice for Posting

**Content Summary:**
- **Model Notice:** Hotels, motels, and bed and breakfast inns are required to post a model notice informing the public and victims about telephone hotline numbers.
- **Languages Required:** The notice must be posted in English and Spanish. For businesses in specific counties, an additional third language is required.
- **Penalties for Non-Compliance:** There are specific posting mandates, language requirements, and penalties for failing to post the notice as mandated by SB 225.

### Analysis of Compliance

#### Source 1: Training Requirement
- **Compliance Date:** January 1, 2020 (for those employed on or before July 1, 2019), with a follow-up requirement within six months for new employees.
- **Exemption:** Employees who received training prior to the initial deadline are not required to retake it.

#### Source 3: Model Notice Requirement
- **Compliance Date:** The model notice must be posted in English and Spanish, and for certain counties, an additional third language.
- **Penalties:** Penalties for non-compliance with posting requirements can vary but may include fines or other penalties as mandated by law.

### Conclusion

Both the training requirement and the posting of a model notice are critical components of California's efforts to combat human trafficking. Businesses that employ staff who interact with the public, particularly in hotels and motels, must ensure compliance with these requirements to avoid potential legal penalties and to fulfill their ethical responsibility to protect individuals from exploitation.

**Enforcement:**
- **Training Compliance:** Employers can track training completion through records of employee participation.
- **Posting Compliance:** Businesses should regularly review posting compliance with the mandated languages and seek assistance for third-language postings in counties that require them. Regular inspections by regulatory bodies can also ensure adherence to these requirements.

By ensuring compliance with these legislative measures, California aims to provide a safer environment for potential victims and hold businesses accountable for providing essential resources and support.
