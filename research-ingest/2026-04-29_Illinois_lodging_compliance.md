The document you provided outlines a new law in Illinois regarding human trafficking recognition and reporting by employees in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. Here are the key points:

1. **Definitions**:
   - "Employee" is defined as someone who works at these establishments and interacts with the public regularly.
   - "Employer" includes those operating hotels/motels, restaurants, or truck stops.
   - "Human trafficking" is described as the deprivation of personal liberty with intent to exploit for labor, sex, or other illegal purposes.

2. **Training Requirement**:
   - Beginning June 1, 2020, all employers must provide training on recognizing human trafficking and reporting procedures to their employees who interact with the public.
   - Training must be completed within 6 months of starting the job and every 2 years thereafter if still employed by the same employer.

3. **Training Curriculum**:
   - Employers can use either their own program or a third-party one that meets minimum requirements, which include definitions, risk identification, labor vs. sex trafficking differentiation, and reporting procedures.
   - The Department of Human Services must develop and publish its curriculum by July 1, 2020.

4. **Penalties**:
   - Local governments can monitor compliance with this act.
   - If a violation is not corrected within 30 days after notification, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action resulting in fines of up to $1,500 per offense.

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and responsiveness of workers in these industries towards identifying and reporting human trafficking incidents.
