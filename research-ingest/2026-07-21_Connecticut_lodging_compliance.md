## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs to combat human trafficking, focusing on various industries including hotels, motels, schools, law enforcement, and medical providers. The state requires regular training for Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, and service providers, as well as mandatory data reporting. Legislation includes expanding the crime of human trafficking, providing victim services, and strengthening protections.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry**: Hotels, motels, schools, law enforcement, medical providers.
- **Training required**: Annual basic training for Child Protective Services staff; specific trainings for foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, emergency medical services, hotels, motels, and lodging businesses.
- **Signage required**: Not specified in the provided sources.
- **Reporting required**: Mandatory data reporting.
- **Enforcement agency**: Department of Children and Families (DCF), local law enforcement.
- **Penalties**: Classes C felonies for commercial sexual exploitation of a minor and knowingly purchasing advertising space for commercial sex acts involving minors.
- **Effective date**: Varies by legislation, with the most recent being 2017.

## Harbor Compliance
Connecticut's laws and training programs ensure that various industries are aware of and prepared to address human trafficking. Regular training requirements for Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, and service providers help in identifying and responding to potential cases. The state’s comprehensive legislation includes provisions for mandatory data reporting and expanding protections for victims, aligning with federal standards.

## Conclusion
Connecticut has robust measures in place to combat human trafficking through well-defined legislation and training programs across multiple industries. These efforts are crucial in protecting vulnerable individuals and ensuring the safety of all residents.
