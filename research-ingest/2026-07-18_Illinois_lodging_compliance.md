**Human Trafficking Recognition Training Law (820 ILCS 95)**

This law in Illinois requires that employers in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops provide their employees with training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols. The requirements include:

1. **Training Frequency**: Employees must complete the training within six months after starting their job and every two years thereafter if still employed.

2. **Duration of Training**: The training must be at least 20 minutes in duration.

3. **Content of Training**:
   - Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
   - Guidance on identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
   - Differences between labor and sex-based trafficking within the lodging, restaurant, or truck stop industry.
   - Role of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

4. **Approval of Training Programs**: Employers can use their own training programs or third-party programs as long as they meet the minimum requirements listed above.

5. **Departmental Curriculum**: The Illinois Department of Labor is tasked with developing an approved curriculum for human trafficking recognition training, which must be published by July 1, 2020.

6. **Penalties**: Violators face a fine not more than $1,500 per offense if the violation is not corrected within 30 days after notification.

7. **Enforcement**: Local government units and law enforcement agencies can monitor compliance with this act and impose penalties as necessary.

**Effective Date**: This Act becomes effective on January 1, 2026.

This law aims to enhance awareness and prevent human trafficking by ensuring that employees in key industries are better equipped to recognize and respond to potential instances of human trafficking.
