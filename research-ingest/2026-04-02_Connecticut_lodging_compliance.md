## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs in place to address human trafficking. The state requires annual basic training for Child Protective Services staff, specialized trainings for various industries and professionals, and mandates certain businesses to provide trafficking awareness campaigns and training annually.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging; emergency medical services (EMS); child protective services.
- **Training required:** Annual basic training for Child Protective Services staff; specialized trainings for foster parents, schools, law enforcement, medical providers, hospitality industry professionals, youth, and the community.
- **Signage required:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees annually. There are no specific signage requirements mentioned.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- **Enforcement agency:** The Department of Children and Families (DCF), state attorneys, and local law enforcement enforce the legislation.
- **Penalties:** Engaging in commercial sexual exploitation of a minor is a class C felony. Exploiting a child younger than 18 is also punishable by a class C felony.

## Effective Date
The legislation was enacted starting from February 2017 with subsequent updates and amendments through various public acts.
