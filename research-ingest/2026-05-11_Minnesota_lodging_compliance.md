Minnesota has enacted legislation requiring hotel, motel, and short-term rental operators to provide training on sex trafficking prevention and response. The law mandates that:

1. All owners, managers, and on-site employees must complete the required training.
2. New employees must take this training within 90 days of hire.
3. All required staff must retake this training every year.

The training aims to help hotel, motel, and short-term rental staff recognize the signs of sex trafficking and understand how to respond effectively. The law covers a comprehensive package that includes a virtual presentation, a training companion guide, a manager and owner toolkit, and specific posters for display.

Key points from the Minnesota law and official guidance include:

- Training requirements apply to all hotels, motels, and short-term rentals in Minnesota.
- There are few exemptions, such as employees under 16 unless they clean rooms or those working exclusively in restaurants without direct contact with guests.
- Owners, operators, and managers are required to take the training even if they do not have direct contact with guests.
- The law does not explicitly exempt seasonal employees; however, all covered employees must be trained within 90 days of starting work.
- Contracted workers, including cleaners, are subject to the same requirements.

The Minnesota Department of Health (MDH) provides free training materials in English and multiple languages. Training is mandatory and must be completed annually by licensure. Licensees are responsible for ensuring compliance with the costs, such as printing posters, although MDH does not provide financial assistance beyond offering the free training package.

For more detailed information and support in accessing or completing the required training, hotel operators can contact the MDH Learning Center at health.safeharbor@state.mn.us.
