### Extracted Information from Provided Sources

#### 1. **Training Requirement for Motel and Hotel Employees**
   - **Source:** SB 970 (external site) and SOURCE 1.
   - **Key Points:**
     - By January 1, 2020, hotel and motel employers are required to provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training and education regarding human trafficking awareness.
     - The training must cover employees who are likely to interact with victims of human trafficking.
   - **Exception:** Employees who received training on or before January 1, 2019, do not need additional training.

#### 2. **Posting Requirement for Businesses**
   - **Source:** Senate Bill 1193 and SOURCE 3.
   - **Key Points:**
     - Specified businesses (including hotels and motels) are required to post a notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking about telephone hotline numbers to seek help or report unlawful activity.
     - The notice must be in English and Spanish, and may require additional languages depending on the location of the business.

#### 3. **Human Trafficking FAQs**
   - **Source:** SOURCE 1.
   - **Key Points:**
     - Human trafficking is a violation of both criminal and civil law.
     - CRD enforces California's human trafficking civil rights law.
     - Survivors may have access to civil remedies, which can be found in the linked document.

#### 4. **Model Notice for Human Trafficking**
   - **Source:** SOURCE 3.
   - **Key Points:**
     - The model notice must provide telephone hotline numbers and text service numbers for victims of human trafficking.
     - Additional languages are available for posting requirements depending on business location.

### Summary
- **Training Requirement:** Hotel and motel employers must provide training by January 1, 2020, to employees likely to interact with human trafficking victims.
- **Posting Requirement:** Specified businesses (including hotels and motels) must post a notice in English and Spanish about human trafficking hotline numbers.
- **Resources for Victims:** Information on civil remedies and available hotlines can be found through the California Department of Justice and national resources.
