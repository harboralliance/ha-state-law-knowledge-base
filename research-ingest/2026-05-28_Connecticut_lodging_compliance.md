## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at preventing, identifying, and responding to human trafficking. The state requires mandatory human trafficking training for DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other relevant groups. Legislation includes expanding protections for victims and enhancing the ability of courts to handle trafficking cases.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable Industry**: All service providers, foster parents, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other relevant groups.
- **Training Required**: Mandatory human trafficking training for DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other relevant groups. Additionally, child trafficking prevention curriculum for youth.
- **Signage Required**: Not explicitly mentioned in the source materials.
- **Reporting Required**: State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- **Enforcement/Compliance Measures**: Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees annually and keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.
- **Victim Support**: DCF provides services to human trafficking victims classified as "uncared for," and law enforcement receives training on handling human trafficking cases.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's approach to combat human trafficking is multifaceted, incorporating both legislative efforts and training programs for various stakeholders. This comprehensive strategy aims to protect victims and prevent the exploitation of minors through targeted education and enforcement measures.
