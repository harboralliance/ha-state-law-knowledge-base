The Texas Attorney General's office has implemented a program to provide training for employees of commercial lodging establishments regarding human trafficking. The requirement is part of House Bill 390 and applies only to establishments with 10 or more rooms, although it is encouraged for all lodging establishments in the state.
To comply with the law, these establishments must provide annual human trafficking training to their employees. The training must be approved by the Attorney General's office and can be either online or in-person. Approved training organizations can be found on the Attorney General's website.
In addition to providing training, establishments are required to display specific signage related to human trafficking in their facilities. The signage must include information about how employees can report suspected instances of human trafficking and where they can receive assistance.
The law also prohibits discrimination against employees who make good faith reports of suspected instances of human trafficking.
Overall, the requirement for human trafficking training is aimed at increasing awareness and providing guidance on how to identify and respond to potential instances of human trafficking in commercial lodging establishments.
