### Overview

The provided sources discuss human trafficking, including awareness training requirements for employees in motels and hotels. Key points include:

1. **Legislation**: California Senate Bill 970 requires hotel and motel employers to provide at least 20 minutes of training on human trafficking by January 1, 2020.
   
2. **Training Details**: This includes both current employees (employed as of July 1, 2019) and new employees likely to interact with victims of trafficking within six months of their employment.

3. **Penalties**: Failure to comply could result in legal action by the California Attorney General's office.

4. **Support Resources**: Victims can contact a 24-hour hotline for help or report suspected human trafficking incidents, including options such as texting a specific number for support.

5. **Model Notices**: The State of California provides model notices that businesses must post to inform the public about human trafficking resources.

6. **Legal Consequences**: Violators could face fines and other penalties.

### Detailed Analysis

1. **SB 970 (2019)**:
   - **Effective Date**: January 1, 2020.
   - **Training Requirements**: Employees must receive at least 20 minutes of classroom or interactive training on human trafficking.
   - **Employee Eligibility**: Training applies to employees employed as of July 1, 2019.
   - **New Employees**: New hires likely to interact with trafficking victims within six months also need this training.

2. **Penalties**:
   - Non-compliance can lead to legal action by the Attorney General's office.
   - Potential fines and other penalties could result from non-compliance.

3. **Support Services**:
   - Victims can contact various helplines, including the National Human Trafficking Hotline at 1-888-373-7888 or text "Be Free" or "233733."
   - These resources provide assistance and guidance to victims.

4. **Model Notices**:
   - The State of California provides model notices in multiple languages (English, Spanish, and additional languages) that businesses are required to post.
   - Posting requirements include specific language guidelines for counties subject to federal language assistance provisions.

### Conclusion

The legislation requires hotel and motel employers to provide training on human trafficking to current and new employees. Non-compliance could result in legal action and penalties. Victims have access to various support resources, including helplines, for reporting and receiving assistance. The State provides model notices to assist businesses in fulfilling their posting requirements.
