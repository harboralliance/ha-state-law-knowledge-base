## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive laws and training programs in place to combat human trafficking, including sex and labor. The state requires various groups to undergo annual trainings, such as DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other community members.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, other community members.
- **Training required:** Annual trainings on human trafficking definitions, laws, warning signs, impacts, and how to respond.
- **Signage required:** No specific signage requirements mentioned in the source materials.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and cases referred for prosecution.
- **Enforcement agency:** Department of Children and Families (DCF), state attorneys, local law enforcement.
- **Penalties:** Class C felonies for certain human trafficking offenses, presumptions of coercion in prosecutions.
- **Effective date:** The laws mentioned date back to 2010 and have been updated with subsequent acts up to 2017.

## Conclusion
Connecticut’s efforts in combating human trafficking are well underway through mandatory trainings and stringent legislation. However, there is no specific mention of signage requirements or reporting formats beyond the minimum legal standards.
