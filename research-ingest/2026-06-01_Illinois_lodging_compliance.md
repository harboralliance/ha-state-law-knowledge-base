The provided document outlines the Illinois Human Trafficking Prevention Act, enacted in 2019 with a revised version taking effect in 2026. Here are key points from the legislation:

### Definitions:
- **Department:** The Department of Human Services.
- **Employee:** A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who interacts with the public regularly (e.g., reception area staff, housekeeping).
- **Employer:** A person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** The deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty to obtain forced labor, services, or commercial sex.
- **Lodging Establishment:** An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS).
- **Restaurant:** Any business primarily engaged in selling ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption.
- **Truck Stop:** An establishment providing services to the trucking industry, including fuel, food, showers, repairs, and parking.

### Training Requirements:
- Beginning June 1, 2020, employers must provide training on recognizing human trafficking to their employees who interact with the public. 
- Employees must complete this training within 6 months of starting a job in that role and every 2 years thereafter if still employed.
- The training should be at least 20 minutes long.

### Training Curriculum:
Employers can use their own training programs or those provided by third parties, as long as the program includes:
1. Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
2. Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
3. Differences between labor and sex trafficking in relation to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
4. Roles of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

The Department is tasked with developing a model curriculum that must include all these elements by July 1, 2020.

### Penalties:
- A unit of local government or law enforcement agency can monitor and enforce compliance with the Act.
- If an employer fails to correct a violation within 30 days after being notified, they may face a civil penalty of up to $1,500 per offense.

### Effective Date:
The revised version of the Act takes effect on January 1, 2026.

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and response capabilities in businesses dealing with the public, thereby helping prevent human trafficking.
