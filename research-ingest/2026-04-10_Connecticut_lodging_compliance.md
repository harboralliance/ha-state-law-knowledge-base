# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at addressing human trafficking, including both sex and labor. The state requires mandatory training for various professionals such as Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, service providers, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry personnel, and others. Legislation includes provisions for victim services, data reporting, and penalties for those involved in trafficking.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable Industry**: Child protective services, foster care, service providers, schools, law enforcement, healthcare (including hospitals, doctors, nurses), hospitality, youth, community.
- **Training Required**: Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement (POST Certified), Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Emergency Medical Services (EMS), Child Trafficking Training for Medical Providers/Emergency Medicine, Introduction to Labor Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Labor Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement (POST Certified).
- **Signage Required**: Not explicitly stated.
- **Mandatory Reporting**: Yes, for missing children and trafficking referrals.
- **Data Reporting**: Mandatory data reporting.
- **Victim Services**: DPH provides services to human trafficking victims classified as “uncared for.”
- **Penalties**: Forfeiture properties, affirmative defense for minors, vacating records.

## Harbor Compliance
1. **Training Mandates**: Ensures mandatory training across various sectors including healthcare and hospitality to prevent and respond to trafficking.
2. **Victim Support**: Provides services to human trafficking victims classified as “uncared for.”
3. **Legislative Protections**: Includes provisions for victim services, data reporting, and penalties.
4. **Data Reporting**: Ensures mandatory data reporting to combat trafficking effectively.

## Potential Improvements
- **Enhanced Signage**: Consider adding visible signage in high-risk areas like hotels and healthcare facilities to raise awareness about human trafficking.
- **Community Engagement**: Increase community engagement through public awareness campaigns and educational programs.
- **Ongoing Monitoring**: Establish an ongoing monitoring body to assess the effectiveness of current laws and training programs.

This research captures the comprehensive approach Connecticut has taken to combat human trafficking, highlighting both legislative protections and practical training initiatives.
