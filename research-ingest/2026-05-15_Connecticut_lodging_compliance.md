## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs in place to address human trafficking, including both sex and labor trafficking. The state requires annual basic training for all Child Protective Services staff on human trafficking. Additionally, specialized trainings are available for various professions such as foster parents, schools, law enforcement, emergency medical services, hotel industry, and other professionals.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry**: Child protective services, schools, law enforcement, emergency medical services, hospitality industry (hotels, motels, and lodging), youth, and the community.
- **Training required**: Annual basic training for all Child Protective Services staff, specialized trainings for foster parents, schools, law enforcement, emergency medical services, hotel industry, and other professionals.
- **Signage required**: Not specified in the provided sources.
- **Reporting required**: State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc. Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.
- **Enforcement/penalties**: The crime of human trafficking is a class C felony if the person knew or reasonably should have known that such other person was either younger than the age of eighteen.
- **Monitoring/reporting**: Ongoing monitoring of efforts to combat trafficking, mandatory data reporting.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's approach to combating human trafficking is well-structured with both preventive measures through training and mandatory documentation, as well as stringent legal penalties. However, further improvements in public awareness and the enforcement mechanisms could be beneficial to fully address the issue effectively.
