## Summary
Connecticut offers comprehensive training programs for various groups to combat human trafficking, including foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospitals, hospitality industry professionals, and youth. The state also has enacted legislation aimed at preventing and addressing human trafficking.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Foster care services, service providers, education, law enforcement, healthcare, hospitality, youth.
- **Training required:** Regular annual training for foster parents, Child Protective Services staff, schools, law enforcement (POST certified), emergency medical services, hotels, motels, and lodging businesses. Specialized training for youth, including 2-day training for the Department of Children and Families Academy.
- **Signage required:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns annually.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and trafficking cases referred for prosecution. Mandatory data reporting on human trafficking cases.
- **Enforcement:** Legislation expands the crime of human trafficking, broadens conditions under which it is committed, and includes provisions for forfeiture properties, affirmative defense for minors, vacating records, public awareness and prevention, mandatory data reporting, statewide oversight, and monitoring.
- **Penalties:** Commercial sexual exploitation of a minor is a class C felony. Forfeiture of properties and affirmative defense provisions for minors are included.

## Contact Information

These requirements ensure that all relevant parties in Connecticut are equipped to recognize, respond to, and prevent human trafficking.
