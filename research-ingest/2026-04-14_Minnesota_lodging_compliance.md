**Minnesota Law on Sex Trafficking Prevention for Hotels, Motels, and Short-Term Rentals**

### Overview

Minnesota requires hotels, motels, and short-term rentals to provide sex trafficking prevention and response training to their employees as part of the broader effort to combat human trafficking in the state. This legislation is designed to ensure that those in these roles are aware of the signs and risks of sex trafficking and equipped with the knowledge and tools to intervene and respond appropriately.

### Key Requirements

1. **Training Obligation**:
   - All owners, managers, and on-site employees must complete the required training.
   - New employees must take this training within 90 days of hire.
   - All required staff must retake this training every year.

2. **Displaying a Back-of-House Poster**:
   - The back-of-house poster is a critical component of the training package and must be displayed in a location where all staff can see it.

3. **Conducting an Ongoing Awareness Campaign**:
   - In addition to formal training, there is a requirement for ongoing education and awareness campaigns for employees to reinforce their understanding and readiness to respond to potential incidents of sex trafficking.

### Exceptions

- Employees under 16 years old unless they work in the room service role.
- Employees who work exclusively in restaurants or other food preparation roles that do not involve direct guest interaction.
- Employees with no direct contact with guests or guest rooms.

### Legal Compliance and Costs

- Training is mandatory and must be completed by all covered employees.
- The Minnesota Department of Health (MDH) provides the training package at no cost to licensees.
- Licensees are responsible for paying the costs associated with compliance, such as printing posters.

### Training Content and Delivery

The training package includes:
- A virtual presentation with guided exercises
- A Training Companion Guide in English, Spanish, Somali, and Hmong
- A Manager & Owner Training Toolkit with instructions on how to comply with the law
- Back-of-house posters for display

### Accessing the Training

- Managers and owners can access the training through the MDH Learning Center, which requires a brief pre-training survey.
- Employees can access the training in their preferred language (English or Spanish/Español).

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. **Re-taking Training**: Even if you have taken training on sex trafficking before, you must complete the Minnesota-specific lodging training package to comply with the law.

2. **Training for Non-Lodging Businesses**: Resorts, bed and breakfasts, vacation shares, and short-term rentals are only required to provide the training if they hold a hotel license.

3. **Tribal Nations**: While tribal nations are not exempt from state laws, MDH encourages their participation in providing the training.

4. **Seasonal Employees**: Seasonal employees must receive training within 90 days of starting work.

5. **Temporary Employees**: Even if an employee's employment is less than 90 days, it is encouraged to complete the training.

6. **Contracted Workers**: Contracted workers, such as cleaners, are required to take the training.

### Conclusion

The Minnesota law on sex trafficking prevention for hotels, motels, and short-term rentals is a critical step in combating human trafficking within the state. By ensuring that employees are trained, informed, and equipped with the necessary tools, these businesses can play a crucial role in preventing and responding to sex trafficking incidents.
