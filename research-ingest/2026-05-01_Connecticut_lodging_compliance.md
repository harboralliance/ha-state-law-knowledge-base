# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at addressing human trafficking, including both sex and labor trafficking. The state requires specific trainings for various groups such as foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other relevant sectors. Legislation includes mandatory annual training for Child Protective Services staff and hotel employees. It also provides resources for victims and mandates reporting requirements.

## Extracted Requirements
- Applicable industry: Foster care services, service providers, schools, law enforcement, healthcare (emergency medicine), hospitality industry, other professionals.
- Training required: Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement, Emergency Medical Services (EMS) training, Child Trafficking Training for Medical Providers/Emergency Medicine, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Hotels, Motels, and Lodging, Introduction to Labor Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Labor Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement.
- Signage required: Not explicitly mentioned in the legislation but recommended as part of awareness campaigns.
- Reporting requirements: Mandatory data reporting by hotels and motels, Child Protective Services, and law enforcement.
- Victim services: DCF provides services to human trafficking victims classified as “uncared for,” including training for law enforcement on human trafficking of minors and creation of multidisciplinary teams.

## Enforcement and Penalties
Connecticut's laws expand the crime of human trafficking by broadening conditions under which it is committed, especially when involving minors. For instance, commercial sexual exploitation of a minor is a class C felony, as is knowingly purchasing advertising space for a commercial sex act that includes a depiction of a minor.

## Conclusion
Connecticut has robust legislative and training frameworks in place to combat human trafficking. Regular trainings and mandatory reporting requirements ensure that various sectors are well-equipped to identify, respond to, and prevent human trafficking incidents.
