### Summary of SB1422 - Illinois Human Trafficking Recognition Training Act

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026  
**Purpose:** To ensure that employers in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops provide training for their employees to recognize human trafficking.

#### Key Points:

1. **Definitions:**
   - **Department:** Department of Human Services
   - **Employee:** A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop with recurring interactions with the public.
   - **Employer:** A person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
   - **Human Trafficking:** The deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell individuals for commercial sex, or exploit individuals in obscene matter.

2. **Training Requirement:**
   - Employers must provide training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols to their employees.
   - Training must be completed within 6 months of employment for new roles and every 2 years thereafter if still employed.
   - The training must cover at least:
     - Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children
     - How to identify individuals at risk for human trafficking
     - Difference between labor and sex trafficking in the context of lodging, restaurants, and truck stops
     - Role of employees in reporting and responding to human trafficking instances

3. **Training Curriculum:**
   - Employers can use their own program or a third-party program that meets the minimum requirements.
   - The Department must develop a curriculum for approved programs not administering their own.

4. **Penalties:**
   - Units of local government or law enforcement agencies may monitor and enforce compliance with this act.
   - Upon discovery of non-compliance, employers are given 30 days to cure the violation; otherwise, they can be subject to a civil penalty of up to $1,500 per offense.

#### Significance:
SB1422 aims to enhance awareness and response capabilities within key industries that often interact with vulnerable individuals. This legislation is critical in preventing human trafficking by equipping employees with essential knowledge and skills to identify and report suspected instances effectively.
