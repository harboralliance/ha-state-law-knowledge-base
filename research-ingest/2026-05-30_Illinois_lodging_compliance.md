The provided text is a bill, specifically **House Bill 704**, introduced in the Illinois General Assembly to amend the Human Trafficking Prevention Act to require employers in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide human trafficking recognition training. Below are key points from the bill:

### Definitions:
- **Department:** The Department of Human Services.
- **Employee:** A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public (e.g., receptionists, housekeeping staff).
- **Employer:** A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** The deprivation or violation of personal liberty with the intent to obtain forced labor, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.

### Human Trafficking Recognition Training:
- **Requirement:** Beginning June 1, 2020, employers must provide training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols.
- **Duration:** Employees must complete the training within six months of starting their role and every two years thereafter if still employed by the employer.
- **Training Content:** Minimum requirements include definitions of human trafficking, identifying individuals at risk, distinguishing labor from sex trafficking in lodging, restaurants, and truck stops, and employee roles in reporting and responding to human trafficking.

### Human Trafficking Recognition Training Curriculum:
- **Employer Choice:** Employers may use their own program or a third-party program that meets the minimum requirements.
- **Department’s Curriculum:** The Department must develop a curriculum for approved programs used by establishments not administering their own program. This curriculum must include all required elements and consult with the U.S. Department of Justice.

### Penalties:
- **Local Government Enforcement:** Units of local government may monitor and enforce compliance with the act.
- **Noncompliance Notice:** Upon discovery of a violation, employers are given 30 days to correct it; otherwise, they face civil penalties.
- **Civil Action:** The Attorney General or State's Attorney can bring a civil action if the employer does not correct the violation within the cure period.

### Effective Date:
The act takes effect on January 1, 2026.

This bill aims to enhance awareness and preparedness among employees in the hospitality and transportation industries regarding human trafficking, thereby potentially preventing exploitation.
