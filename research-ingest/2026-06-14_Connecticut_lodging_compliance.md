## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs in place to combat human trafficking, including sex and labor. The state requires mandatory annual training for foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other relevant groups. Specific laws include the Prevention of Human Trafficking Act (2017), which expands protections for victims, and the Commercial Sexual Exploitation of a Minor Act (2012), which defines commercial sexual exploitation. The state also mandates data reporting and ongoing monitoring efforts to combat trafficking.

## Extracts from Sources

### Trainings
**Foster Parents:** Annual mandatory training on human trafficking.
**Service Providers, Schools, Law Enforcement, Healthcare Staff:** Annual or bi-annual training on human trafficking of minors.
**Hotel and Motel Employees:** Annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns.

### Legislation
**Public Act 17-32 (2017):**
- Expands the conditions under which a person can be guilty of prostitution to include individuals younger than 18.
- Mandates hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging to provide annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns.

**Public Act 16-71 (2016):**
- Defines commercial sexual exploitation of a minor.
- Requires state attorneys and local law enforcement to document certain activities related to human trafficking.

### Prevention and Awareness
- The state is required to monitor efforts to combat trafficking.
- Public awareness and prevention campaigns are ongoing.

### Reporting
- Data reporting on human trafficking cases must be mandatory.
- There is a statewide oversight and monitoring body dedicated to combating trafficking.

### Victim Protections
- Expands protections for victims of human trafficking, including the provision of services similar to those given to sexual assault victims.
- Allows for the waiver of crime victim compensation limitations in certain circumstances.

## Applicability

The trainings mentioned are applicable to foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, healthcare staff, hotel and motel employees, and other relevant groups. Legislation such as Public Act 17-32 and Public Act 16-71 address specific aspects of human trafficking, including age-related offenses and mandatory training requirements.

## Enforceability

Training compliance is mandated for foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, healthcare staff, hotel and motel employees. Legislation like Public Act 17-32 and Public Act 16-71 are enforceable through state regulations and legal actions, including potential fines and penalties for non-compliance.

## Conclusion

Connecticut's approach to human trafficking combat is multifaceted, combining comprehensive legislation with extensive training programs and ongoing monitoring efforts. These measures aim to ensure the safety and rights of victims while providing resources and support to those in need.
