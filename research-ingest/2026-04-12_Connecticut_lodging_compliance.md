## Summary
Connecticut has robust legislation and training programs to address human trafficking, both for sex and labor. Training is required for various groups including DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and youth. Legislation includes mandatory data reporting and the creation of multidisciplinary teams to handle cases.

## Extracted Requirements
- Applicable industry: Hospitality, healthcare, education, law enforcement, social services.
- Training required: Annual basic training for DCF staff; advanced training available.
- Signage required: Hotels, motels, and lodging must provide trafficking training annually.
- Reporting required: State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution.
- Enforcement agency: The Department of Children and Families (DCF).
- Penalties: Commercial sexual exploitation of a minor is a class C felony. Forfeiture properties, affirmative defense for minors, vacating records.
- Effective date: Laws from 2010 onwards.

## Harbor Information
