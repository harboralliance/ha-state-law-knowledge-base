### Summary of the Human Trafficking Recognition Training Act

**Title:** Human Trafficking Recognition Training Act  
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026  

#### Definitions
- **Department:** Department of Human Services
- **Employee:** Person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer:** Person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** Deprivation or violation of personal liberty for forced labor or services, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.
- **Lodging Establishment:** Hotel or motel under code 721110; casino hotel under code 721120.
- **Restaurant:** Business primarily selling ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption comprising at least 51% of sales.
- **Truck Stop:** Establishment providing services to the trucking industry.

#### Training Requirements
- **Effective Date:** June 1, 2020  
- **Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops** must provide training in human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols.
- **Duration of Training:** At least 20 minutes.
- **Frequency of Training:** Within 6 months after starting employment and every 2 years thereafter if still employed.

#### Human Trafficking Recognition Training Curriculum
- Employers may use their own program or a third-party program.
- Curriculum must include:
  1. Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
  2. Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
  3. Difference between labor and sex trafficking related to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
  4. Role of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

- **Department’s Curriculum:**
  - Developed by the Department if not using their own program.
  - Must include the same elements as specified above.
  - The Department may consult with the U.S. Department of Justice for development.
  - Published no later than July 1, 2020.

#### Penalties
- **Unit of Local Government/Law Enforcement Agency:**
  - May monitor and enforce compliance.
  - Provide a reasonable notice of noncompliance.
  - If not cured within 30 days, subject to a civil penalty.
  
- **Attorney General/State’s Attorney:**
  - Can bring a civil action if the violation is not corrected.

- **Fines:** Up to $1,500 for each offense.

#### Effective Date
- This Act becomes effective January 1, 2026.
