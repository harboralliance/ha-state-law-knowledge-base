The training for preventing sex trafficking in hotels, motels, and short-term rentals is required by law in Minnesota. It is a mandatory requirement for all owners, managers, and on-site employees to complete the training within 90 days of hire and retake it every year.

New employees must complete the training within 90 days of their start date. The training includes a virtual presentation, a training companion guide, a manager/owner toolkit, and a set of posters that must be displayed in various areas of the property.

The training covers identifying signs of sex trafficking, understanding the role of hospitality workers in prevention, and knowing how to respond appropriately if they suspect a violation. It also emphasizes ongoing awareness campaigns for all employees.

Exemptions include:
- Employees under 16 unless they are involved in cleaning rooms
- Those who work exclusively in restaurants providing catering services 
- Those with no direct contact with guests or guest rooms

The law does not explicitly exempt seasonal employees, but MDH encourages them to receive the training within 90 days of starting.

Contracted workers like cleaners are subject to the same requirements as regular employees. Whole house vacation rental properties that fall under hotel/motel licensing are also required to comply with the statute.

Compliance involves maintaining a roster of employees who have completed the training, along with their completion dates. This information does not need to be submitted to MDH for inspection.

This legislation aims to ensure that hospitality workers in Minnesota are equipped to recognize and respond appropriately to potential cases of sex trafficking, thereby preventing such incidents from occurring within their establishments.
