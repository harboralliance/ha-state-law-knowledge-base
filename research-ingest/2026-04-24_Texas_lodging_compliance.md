The Office of the Attorney General in Texas has implemented measures to address human trafficking within the hospitality industry. Here are key points about their approach:

1. **House Bill 390 (87th Regular Session)**: This legislation mandates that commercial lodging establishments with ten or more rooms provide annual human trafficking training to all employees.

2. **Training Requirements**: The training must cover identifying risk factors, reporting suspected incidents of human trafficking, and understanding the importance of not retaliating against individuals who report such activities in good faith.

3. **Signage Requirement**: Businesses must also display specific signage that outlines their policy on human trafficking prevention.

4. **Certification**: Employees must complete approved training by their 90th day after hire and receive an annual refresher course. Training providers are required to issue a certificate of completion for each participant.

5. **Approved Trainings**: The Office of the Attorney General has approved several training videos that meet the requirements. These include:
   - "Human Trafficking Hotel Training" by Etactics
   - "Human Trafficking – Texas" by Unbound Now
   - "Identifying and Responding to Human Trafficking: Commercial Lodging & Hospitality Industry" by Unbound Now (available in English and Spanish)
   - Various other trainings from organizations like Businesses Ending Slavery and Trafficking, Extended Stay America, Marriott International, ECPAT-USA, Refugee Services of Texas, PBJ Learning, and Compliance Learning Solutions.

6. **Costs and Delivery**: Training costs can vary. Some are free, while others may require a monthly subscription or a one-time fee per user. The methods include online, in-person, or via internal company websites.

7. **Reporting Mechanisms**: Training includes instructions on how to contact appropriate entities for reporting human trafficking incidents, such as the National Human Trafficking Hotline and local law enforcement agencies.

8. **Compliance Records**: Establishment operators are required to maintain records of training completion, which must be available for review by the Attorney General and other relevant authorities.

This comprehensive approach aims to enhance awareness and response capabilities within the hospitality industry, helping to protect individuals at risk of human trafficking.
