# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs in place to address human trafficking, both for sex and labor. Legislation includes laws that expand the scope of human trafficking crimes, provide protections for victims, and establish mandatory data reporting. Training programs are available for various professionals, including Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, service providers, law enforcement, emergency medical services personnel, and hotel employees.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging; emergency medical services (EMS); healthcare providers; schools; child protective services; foster care; youth programs.
- **Training required:** Annual training for Child Protective Services staff on human trafficking. Training for hotels, motels, and lodging businesses to provide awareness campaigns and keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months. Training for law enforcement, emergency medical services personnel, healthcare providers, schools, foster parents, and youth prevention programs.
- **Signage required:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must display trafficking awareness signs annually.
- **Mandatory data reporting:** Reporting of human trafficking cases by state attorneys and local law enforcement.
- **Enforcement measures:** Forfeiture properties; affirmative defense for minors; vacating records.

## Enforcement Measures
- The Department of Public Health (DPH) provides services to victims similar to those provided to sexual assault victims.
- Victim Services Office may waive the two-year limitation on crime victim compensation applications for minors who are victims of human trafficking.
- Ongoing monitoring and efforts to combat trafficking, including statewide oversight.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's approach to combating human trafficking is multifaceted, combining strong legal measures with comprehensive training programs across various sectors. The state aims to protect victims, enhance public awareness, and ensure that professionals are equipped to recognize and respond effectively to cases of human trafficking.
