# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut provides various trainings and legislation to address human trafficking. The state requires training for DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and youth. Legislation includes public acts that expand protections for victims of human trafficking and strengthen investigation processes.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Hotels, motels, inns, lodging businesses; law enforcement; healthcare providers; service providers; schools; foster parents; youth.
- **Training required:** Annual basic training on human trafficking for DCF staff. Specific trainings for foster parents, school personnel, law enforcement, emergency medical services, and hospitality industry professionals.
- **Signage required:** Not specified in the provided sources.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and cases referred for prosecution.
- **Enforcement agency:** The Department of Children and Families (DCF) provides services to human trafficking victims and trains law enforcement on these issues.
- **Penalties:** Commercial sexual exploitation of a minor is a class C felony. Forfeiture properties are allowed. Affirmative defense for minors in certain circumstances.
- **Public awareness and prevention:** Ongoing monitoring, mandatory data reporting, statewide oversight, and public education campaigns.

## Summary
Connecticut mandates specific trainings for various professionals and expands protections for victims of human trafficking through legislation. The state aims to combat human trafficking by enhancing training requirements and providing comprehensive support services for victims.
