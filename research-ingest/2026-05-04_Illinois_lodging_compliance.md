**Title: SB1422 - Human Trafficking Recognition Training for Employees in Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops**

---

### **Section 5 - Definitions**
In this Act:
- "Department" means the Department of Human Services.
- "Employee" means a person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public, including those working in reception areas, performing housekeeping duties, assisting customers in moving their possessions, or transporting customers by vehicle.
- "Employer" means a person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- "Human trafficking" means the deprivation or violation of personal liberty with intent to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell an individual for commercial sex, or exploit them in obscene matters.
- "Lodging establishment" means an establishment classified as a hotel or motel under code 721110 or 721120 in the 2017 North American Industry Classification System.
- "Restaurant" means any business primarily engaged in the sale of ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption, comprising at least 51% of total sales (excluding liquor).
- "Truck stop" means an establishment intended to provide services to the trucking industry.

---

### **Section 10 - Human Trafficking Recognition Training**
Beginning June 1, 2020, employers must provide their employees with training on recognizing human trafficking and reporting it. The training must be completed within six months of employment and every two years thereafter if still employed. It should be at least 20 minutes in duration.

---

### **Section 15 - Human Trafficking Recognition Training Curriculum**
Employers can use their own program or a third-party program that includes:
1. A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
2. Guidance on identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
3. The difference between labor and sex trafficking as it relates to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
4. Guidance on the role of employees in reporting and responding to human trafficking.

The Department shall develop a curriculum for approved programs that include these elements and publish it no later than July 1, 2020.

---

### **Section 20 - Penalties**
- Local government or law enforcement can monitor and enforce compliance with the Act.
- If a violation is discovered, employers are given 30 days to correct it; otherwise, they face a civil penalty of up to $1,500 per offense.

---

### **Effective Date**
This Act takes effect on January 1, 2026.
