## Summary
Connecticut offers various training programs and legislation to address human trafficking, including sex trafficking. The state requires Child Protective Services staff to have annual basic training on human trafficking, and providers and communities can access different courses tailored for their needs.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry**: Child protective services, foster parents, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry, other professionals.
- **Training required**: 
  - Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut (2.5 hours)
  - Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut for School Settings (2 hours)
  - Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement (POST Certified) (2.5 hours)
  - Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Emergency Medical Services (EMS) (2 hours)
  - Child Trafficking Training for Medical Providers/Emergency Medicine (1-2 hours)
  - Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Hotels, Motels, Inns, and Similar Lodging (Annual training)
  - Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut for Hotels, Motels, Inns, and Similar Lodging Awareness Campaigns (6 months of records retention)
- **Legislation**: Various public acts that address human trafficking, including sex trafficking. Key legislation includes:
  - Public Act 17-32

  - Public Act 14-186
  - Public Act 13-166
  - Public Act 12-141
  - Public Act 11-180
  - Public Act 10-115

## Harbor Compliance and Training
Connecticut requires mandatory training for Child Protective Services staff, hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging employees on human trafficking. This ensures that professionals are equipped to identify and respond appropriately to cases of human trafficking.

## Legal Framework
The state has enacted multiple laws to address human trafficking, including sex trafficking. These laws include provisions for enhanced protections for victims, forfeiture properties, affirmative defense for minors, and mandatory data reporting. The legislation also includes a statewide oversight and monitoring body to combat trafficking efforts continuously.

In conclusion, Connecticut actively addresses human trafficking through comprehensive training programs and stringent legislative frameworks designed to protect victims and prevent trafficking.
