## Human Trafficking Training Requirement for Hotel and Motel Employees

### Summary:
The California Legislature has enacted several laws requiring businesses to provide training on human trafficking awareness to their employees. Specifically, Senate Bill 970 (SB 970) requires hotel and motel employers to provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training and education regarding human trafficking awareness to each employee who is likely to interact or come into contact with victims of human trafficking.

### Timeline:
- **Training Requirement**: By January 1, 2020, hotel and motel employers must provide the required training to employees employed as of July 1, 2019.
- **New Employees**: Employers must also provide such training to new employees likely to interact or come into contact with victims of human trafficking within six months of their employment in that role.

### Exceptions:
- Employers who have provided this training and education to an employee on or before January 1, 2019, are not required to provide additional training.

### Compliance Documentation:
Employers must document the provision of this training. The documentation can be maintained as part of the employer's employee records and should include details such as the date of training, name of the trainer, and any materials used in the training.

### Reporting and Investigation:
The California Attorney General's Office is responsible for enforcing these requirements and investigating complaints related to non-compliance.
