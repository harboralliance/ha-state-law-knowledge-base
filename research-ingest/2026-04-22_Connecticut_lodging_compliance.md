## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs in place to combat human trafficking, both for sex and labor. The state requires annual training for certain groups such as DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospitals, medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other relevant personnel. Legislation includes expanding protections for victims and broadening conditions under which the crime of human trafficking can be committed when the victim is a minor.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable Industry:** DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospitals, medical staff, hospitality industry professionals.
- **Training Required:** Annual training on human trafficking for various groups including DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospitals, and medical staff. Specific courses include "Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut," "Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement," and more.
- **Signage Required:** Hotels, motels, and lodging businesses must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns annually.
- **Mandatory Data Reporting:** Ongoing monitoring and mandatory data reporting are required to combat human trafficking.
- **Statewide Oversight and Monitoring Body:** The state has a statewide oversight and monitoring body for combating trafficking.

## Enforcement and Penalties
Connecticut's legislation includes several penalties and protections, such as:
- Expanding the crime of human trafficking by broadening conditions under which it can be committed when the victim is a minor.
- Allowing the Office of Victim Services (OVS) to waive the two-year limitation on crime victim compensation applications for minors who are victims of human trafficking.
- Expanding the list of crimes, including human trafficking, for which wiretapping may be authorized.

## Prevention and Awareness
Public awareness campaigns and ongoing monitoring of efforts to combat trafficking are also part of Connecticut's approach. The state requires hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging to provide training and awareness campaigns annually and retain guest transactions and receipts for at least six months.
