### Summary

**Bill: SB 1422**
**Effective Date: January 1, 2026**

#### Purpose
The purpose of **SB 1422** is to require employers operating lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide their employees with training on the recognition of human trafficking and protocols for reporting observed instances. The bill aims to enhance employee awareness and response capabilities in identifying and addressing potential human trafficking situations within these establishments.

#### Key Components

1. **Definitions:**
   - **Department:** Refers to the Department of Human Services.
   - **Employee:** Includes persons employed by lodging, restaurant, or truck stop establishments who interact with the public regularly.
   - **Employer:** Refers to any person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
   - **Human Trafficking:** Defined as depriving or violating another's personal liberty for forced labor, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.

2. **Training Requirement:**
   - Effective June 1, 2020, employers must provide their employees with training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols.
   - Training must be completed within six months of employment in the relevant role and every two years thereafter if still employed by the employer.
   - The training duration must be at least 20 minutes.

3. **Curriculum:**
   - Employers can use their own human trafficking training program or a third-party program, provided it meets minimum requirements including:
     - Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
     - Guidance on identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
     - Difference between labor and sex trafficking relevant to lodging, restaurants, and truck stops.
     - Role of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

4. **Penalties:**
   - Local government units or law enforcement agencies can monitor compliance with the Act.
   - Upon discovery of a violation, they must provide a reasonable notice of noncompliance.
   - If the violation is not corrected within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer.
   - Violation of the Act constitutes a business offense, and employers can be fined up to $1,500 for each offense.

#### Effective Date
This act takes effect on January 1, 2026.
