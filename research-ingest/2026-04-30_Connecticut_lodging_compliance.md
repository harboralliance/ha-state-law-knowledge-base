## Summary
Connecticut has implemented comprehensive legislation and training programs to address human trafficking, including sex and labor. The state requires annual training for various professionals, such as Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry workers, and other professionals. Additionally, the state mandates that hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging provide trafficking training annually and keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Child Protective Services, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry, other professionals.
- **Training required:** Annual training on human trafficking definitions, federal and state laws, warning signs, impacts, and buyer/exploiters for DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, emergency medical services (EMS), medical providers/emergency medicine, hotels, motels, and lodging businesses. 
- **Signage required:** Not explicitly mentioned in the sources.
- **Mandatory data reporting:** Yes, state-wide oversight and monitoring body for mandatory data reporting.
- **Effective date:** The laws are effective immediately upon enactment.
