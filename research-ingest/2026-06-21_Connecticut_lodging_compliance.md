## Summary
Connecticut provides various training programs for providers, foster parents, and communities to address human trafficking. The state also has enacted multiple public acts aimed at combating human trafficking, including laws related to the protection of victims and the prevention of commercial sexual exploitation.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Child protective services, foster care, schools, law enforcement, hospitals, hospitality industry, emergency medical services.
- **Training required:** Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement (POST Certified), Introduction to Labor Trafficking in Connecticut, etc.
- **Signage required:** Not explicitly mentioned in the source text.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- **Enforcement agency:** Department of Children and Families (DCF), local law enforcement.
- **Penalties:** Various penalties are outlined in different public acts, including class C felonies.
- **Effective date:** The source text mentions laws enacted from 2010 to 2017.
