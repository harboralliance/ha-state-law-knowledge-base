**Human Trafficking Recognition Training Requirements for Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops in Illinois**

Effective June 1, 2020, Illinois requires employers in the hospitality industry to provide their employees with training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols. This article outlines the key requirements and expectations set forth by the Illinois Department of Human Services.

### Definitions
- **Department:** Refers to the Illinois Department of Human Services.
- **Employee:** Any person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer:** A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** The deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty with intent to obtain forced labor, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.

### Training Requirements
Beginning June 1, 2020, all employers operating lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops must provide their employees with training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols. This training is mandatory for new employees within 6 months of starting their role and every 2 years thereafter while they remain employed.

### Training Curriculum
Training programs must include at least the following:
1. **Definition of Human Trafficking and Commercial Exploitation:** Clear definitions of human trafficking and commercial exploitation, particularly as it relates to children.
2. **Risk Identification:** Guidance on identifying individuals who are most at risk for human trafficking.
3. **Labor vs. Sex Trafficking:** Differentiating between labor and sex trafficking in the context of lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
4. **Employee Roles in Reporting and Responding:** Information on how employees can effectively report and respond to instances of human trafficking.

### Curriculum Development
The Department of Human Services is responsible for developing a curriculum that will be used by employers who do not develop their own training programs. This curriculum must include the same elements as required by the employer-led training programs, including:
1. **Definition of Human Trafficking and Commercial Exploitation:** Clear definitions of human trafficking and commercial exploitation.
2. **Risk Identification:** Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
3. **Labor vs. Sex Trafficking:** Differentiating between labor and sex trafficking in the context of lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
4. **Employee Roles in Reporting and Responding:** Information on how employees can effectively report and respond to instances of human trafficking.

### Penalties
Employers who fail to comply with these requirements may face penalties. A local government or law enforcement agency, upon discovery of a violation, will provide the employer with a reasonable notice of noncompliance. If the violation is not corrected within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney can bring a civil action against the employer.

The fine for violations of this Act can be up to $1,500 per offense.

### Effective Date
This act takes effect on January 1, 2026.

By following these guidelines and providing comprehensive training to their employees, lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops in Illinois are better equipped to recognize and respond effectively to human trafficking incidents.
