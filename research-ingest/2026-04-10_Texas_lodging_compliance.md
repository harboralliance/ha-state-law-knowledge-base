Based on the information provided from the various sources, I've compiled a brief summary of key points regarding human trafficking training for commercial lodging establishments in Texas:

1. The Texas Legislature passed House Bill 390 (87th Regular Session) which requires the Office of the Attorney General to approve training for the hospitality industry. This requirement applies only to commercial lodging establishments with 10 or more rooms, but is encouraged for all lodging establishments regardless of size.

2. The training must be completed by new employees within 90 days of hire and annually thereafter. All employees must receive this training.

3. Approved training providers must provide a certificate of completion for each employee who completes the training. Certificates should include the date taken and course title.

4. Operators of commercial lodging establishments must retain copies of all annual certificates of completion. They must also maintain sufficient records to show compliance with Section 114.0051, Texas Business and Commerce Code. 

5. The training must be approved by the Office of the Attorney General before it can be considered compliant.

6. Approved training videos are available on the OAG website. Some require a subscription fee for ongoing access.

7. Various organizations have developed approved training courses, including:
   - Office of the Attorney General: "Human Trafficking Hotel Training"
   - Etactics: "Human Trafficking – Texas"
   - Unbound Now: Multiple titles
   - Businesses Ending Slavery and Trafficking: Inhospitable to Human Trafficking Training
   - Extended Stay America: Human Trafficking Training
   - Marriott International: Recognize and Respond training
   - ECPAT-USA: Your Role in Preventing Human Trafficking
   - Refugee Services of Texas: Survivor Services/HT101
   - PBJ Learning: Human Trafficking Essentials: Texas Hospitality Awareness HB 390
   - Compliance Learning Solutions: Combating Human Trafficking in the Hospitality Industry

8. Most approved training courses are available online, though some may require in-person sessions.

Overall, there is now a wide range of approved training options available for commercial lodging establishments to comply with the new law and help prevent human trafficking in the hospitality industry.
