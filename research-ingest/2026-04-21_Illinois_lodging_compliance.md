This bill amends the Human Trafficking Prevention Act to require employers in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to provide their employees with training on identifying and reporting human trafficking. The training must be completed within 6 months of employment and every 2 years thereafter if still employed by the employer. Employers can either use their own human trafficking training program or a third-party program that includes specific elements such as definitions of human trafficking, guidance on identifying individuals at risk, the difference between labor and sex trafficking related to lodging establishments, restaurants, or truck stops, and guidance on employees' roles in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking. The Department of Human Services is tasked with developing a curriculum for an approved human trafficking training recognition program that will be used by employers who do not administer their own program. Additionally, the bill includes penalties for employers who fail to comply with this Act, including reasonable notice of noncompliance and potential fines up to $1,500 for each offense. The act takes effect on January 1, 2026.
