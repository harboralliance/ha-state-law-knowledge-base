### Overview of Human Trafficking Training in Commercial Lodging in Texas

#### Background
The Texas Legislature passed House Bill 390 in the 87th Regular Session, which requires commercial lodging establishments with 10 or more rooms to provide annual human trafficking training to their employees. The bill aims to increase awareness and reporting of human trafficking incidents within the hospitality industry.

#### Required Training Components
According to the Texas Business and Commerce Code (Section 114.0051), the required training must cover:
- How and why human trafficking occurs in the hospitality industry.
- Commercial lodging's role in addressing sex trafficking.
- Different types of hotel contract workers who are vulnerable to labor trafficking.
- The employee's role in reporting and responding to human trafficking, including contact information for appropriate entities like the National Human Trafficking Hotline.

#### Training Methods and Content
The training can be delivered through various methods:
1. **Online**: Free courses include those from the Office of the Attorney General, Harbor Alliance, Inc., Unbound Now, and others.
2. **In-person**: Some courses are available for in-person delivery as well, such as "Identifying and Responding to Human Trafficking: Commercial Lodging & Hospitality Industry" by Business Ending Slavery and Trafficking.

#### Approved Training Providers
Several organizations have developed approved human trafficking training videos:
- **Office of the Attorney General**: Provides a free 22-minute online course titled "Human Trafficking Hotel Training."
- **National Course Portal**: Offers a 26-minute self-paced course in English and Spanish with a $4.99 individual learner price or $0.99 per employee for bulk seats (minimum 5 seats).
- **Harbor Alliance, Inc.**: Free 20-minute online training.
- **Unbound Now**: Two free courses: "Identifying and Responding to Human Trafficking: Commercial Lodging & Hospitality Industry" in English and Spanish.

#### Cost
Many of the approved courses are free or have low costs:
- The Office of the Attorney General's course is free.
- National Course Portal offers a subscription-based model for $1/user/month.
- Some organizations charge between $25.95 and $49.99 per individual learner, depending on delivery method.

#### Reporting Requirements
To ensure compliance with Section 114.0051, commercial lodging operators must:
- Retain copies of completed training certificates for each employee.
- Maintain records to demonstrate compliance with the law.
- Make these records available for review and inspection by the OAG and local, state, and federal law enforcement.

#### Conclusion
The requirement for human trafficking training in commercial lodging in Texas is designed to enhance awareness and reporting mechanisms within the hospitality industry. By providing a range of free and low-cost training options, the state encourages establishments to meet their legal obligations while supporting employee education and response capabilities.
