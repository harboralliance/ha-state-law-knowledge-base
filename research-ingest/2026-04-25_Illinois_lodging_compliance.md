**Title: Human Trafficking Recognition Training Requirements for Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops in Illinois**

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026

**Summary:**
This Act requires employers operating lodging establishments, restaurants, or truck stops to provide their employees with training on recognizing human trafficking and reporting observed incidents. The training must be completed within 6 months of employment and every 2 years thereafter. Employers can choose their own human trafficking training program or use the curriculum developed by the Department of Human Services.

**Key Provisions:**

1. **Definitions:**
   - "Department": Department of Human Services
   - "Employee": Person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who interacts with the public regularly.
   - "Employer": A person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
   - "Human Trafficking": Deprivation or violation of personal liberty with the intent to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell individuals for commercial sex, or exploit them in obscene matter.
   - "Lodging Establishment": Hotel or motel classified under code 721110 in the 2017 North American Industry Classification System.
   - "Restaurant": Business primarily engaged in selling ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption comprising at least 51% of total sales.
   - "Truck Stop": Establishment providing services to the trucking industry.

2. **Training Requirements:**
   - Employers must provide human trafficking recognition training to employees within 6 months of employment and every 2 years thereafter.
   - Training must be completed in at least 20 minutes.
   - Training should include identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking, understanding the differences between labor and sex trafficking related to lodging establishments, restaurants, or truck stops, and reporting on instances of human trafficking.

3. **Training Curriculum:**
   - Employers can use their own program or the Department’s curriculum.
   - The Department must develop a minimum curriculum within 6 months of the Act's effective date.

4. **Penalties:**
   - Local government units or law enforcement agencies can monitor compliance and issue reasonable notices to employers who do not correct violations within 30 days.
   - If violations are not corrected, the Attorney General or State’s Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer, with penalties of up to $1,500 for each offense.

**Purpose:**
The Act aims to enhance awareness and preparedness among employees working in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to recognize and respond appropriately to human trafficking incidents. This proactive measure helps protect vulnerable individuals from exploitation.

**Implementation Steps:**
- The Department of Human Services should develop the minimum curriculum by July 1, 2020.
- Employers must adopt either their own approved program or use the Department’s curriculum.
- Training completion must be tracked and documented to ensure compliance with the Act's requirements.

**Conclusion:**
This legislation is crucial for safeguarding individuals in vulnerable industries from human trafficking. By requiring regular training and providing guidelines on reporting, employers can play a pivotal role in combating this pervasive issue.
