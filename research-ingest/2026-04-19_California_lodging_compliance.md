### Summary

**Human Trafficking Awareness Training Requirement for Employees in Hotels and Motels:**

1. **Regulation and Compliance:**
   - **SB 970 (2019):** This state law mandates that, by January 1, 2020, hotel and motel employers provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training regarding human trafficking awareness to employees who are likely to interact with victims of trafficking.
   - **Additional Requirements:**
     - Employers must also provide this training to new employees within six months of their hire if the employee is likely to interact with trafficking victims.
     - Employers who have already provided the required training before January 1, 2020, are not required to provide additional training.

2. **Training Content:**
   - The training should focus on recognizing signs and indicators of human trafficking.
   - It should also cover how to respond appropriately if they encounter a situation that could involve human trafficking.
   - Training must be delivered in an effective and interactive manner to ensure comprehension.

3. **Enforcement:**
   - Violations of the law can result in fines and other penalties as specified by the California Labor Code, Title 26, Division 4, Chapter 102.

4. **Supporting Resources:**
   - **Human Trafficking FAQs:** This resource provides detailed information on how to recognize human trafficking, what steps to take if suspected, and where to report incidents.
   - **National Human Trafficking Resource Center Hotline:** Call 1-888-373-7888 or text Be Free (233733) to access help, support, or report a situation.

5. **Public Awareness:**
   - Hotels and motels are required to post model notices in English and Spanish informing the public about human trafficking and providing hotline numbers for assistance. Additional languages may be required based on local regulations.

6. **Reporting Requirements:**
   - Employees who suspect human trafficking should report it immediately to their employer or law enforcement.
   - Failure to comply with reporting requirements can lead to disciplinary action, including termination of employment.

### Detailed Analysis

1. **Training Requirement:**
   - The primary requirement is that hotel and motel employees receive 20 minutes of interactive training on human trafficking awareness by January 1, 2020.
   - This training must be provided both initially for existing employees who are likely to interact with victims and subsequently for new hires within six months if they will have such interactions.

2. **Training Content:**
   - The training should educate employees about recognizing signs of human trafficking in various forms (e.g., sexual exploitation, labor exploitation).
   - It must also cover the proper procedures for responding to suspected cases, which may include calling law enforcement or notifying management.
   - Training methods should be interactive and engaging to ensure maximum learning retention.

3. **Enforcement:**
   - Employers who fail to provide the required training can face fines and other penalties as specified in the California Labor Code (Title 26, Division 4, Chapter 102).
   - Regular audits or inspections by labor agencies may be conducted to ensure compliance.

4. **Supporting Resources:**
   - Various resources are available to assist employers and employees in understanding human trafficking:
     - **Human Trafficking FAQs:** Provides comprehensive information on recognizing, responding to, and reporting suspected cases.
     - **National Human Trafficking Resource Center Hotline:** Offers immediate assistance and support through phone calls or text messages.

5. **Public Awareness:**
   - Hotels and motels are required to post model notices informing the public about human trafficking and providing hotline numbers for assistance.
   - The notice must be posted in English and Spanish, and in additional languages if specified by local regulations.

6. **Reporting Requirements:**
   - Employees who suspect human trafficking should report it immediately to their employer or law enforcement.
   - Failure to comply with reporting requirements can lead to disciplinary action, including termination of employment.

### Conclusion

The regulation on human trafficking awareness training for employees in hotels and motels is a critical measure aimed at preventing and responding to human trafficking incidents. By requiring regular and comprehensive training, employers are better equipped to recognize and handle such situations appropriately. The compliance with this law not only protects victims but also holds employers accountable for ensuring their workplaces remain safe environments.
