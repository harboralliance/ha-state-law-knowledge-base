## Summary
Connecticut has robust legislation and training programs in place to address human trafficking. The state requires annual training for Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, and hotel employees. Legislation such as Public Act 17-32 and Public Act 15-195 strengthen protections for victims and expand the conditions under which human trafficking can be committed. Training programs cover various aspects of human trafficking, including its impact on different populations like boys and LGBTQ+ children.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Child Protective Services, foster parents, hotel employees, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry, other professionals.
- **Training required:** Annual basic training for Child Protective Services staff; specific trainings for foster parents, hotel employees, law enforcement, hospitals, and other professionals.
- **Signage required:** Not explicitly mentioned in the provided sources.
- **Reporting required:** Yes, state attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and trafficking cases referred for prosecution.
- **Enforcement:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees and keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months.
- **Mandatory data reporting:** Yes, there is an ongoing requirement for mandatory data reporting.

## Enforcement and Monitoring
Connecticut’s laws mandate the provision of specific services to victims of human trafficking and require hotels to provide annual trafficking training. The state also mandates the reporting of investigations and referrals related to human trafficking. Additionally, there are provisions for the erasure of juvenile records in certain circumstances and affirmative defenses for minors involved in human trafficking.

## Legal Framework
Connecticut's legal framework includes several public acts that address human trafficking, including:
- **2017 Public Act 17-32:** Expands conditions under which human trafficking can be committed.
- **2016 Public Act 16-71:** Affects the age of consent for prostitution and requires documentation of training by state attorneys and law enforcement.
- **2015 Public Act 15-195:** Expands protections for victims of human trafficking, including erasure of records and wiretapping authorizations.
- **2014 Public Act 14-186:** Provides services to human trafficking victims classified as "uncared for" and requires DCF training on human trafficking.
- **2013 Public Act 13-166:** Aligns with the federal Trafficking Victims Protection Act, expanding the conditions under which human trafficking can be committed and creating a statewide oversight and monitoring body.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's efforts to combat human trafficking are comprehensive, encompassing legislation, training programs, and mandatory data reporting. The state's robust framework demonstrates its commitment to protecting victims and preventing human trafficking in various sectors of society.
