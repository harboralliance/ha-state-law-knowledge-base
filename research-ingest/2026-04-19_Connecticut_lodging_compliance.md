## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at combating human trafficking, including both sex and labor trafficking. The state requires various groups, such as DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other professionals, to receive annual basic training on human trafficking. Advanced training is also available for those who choose it. Specific laws include Public Act 17-32, 16-71, 15-195, and others that expand protections for victims, allow certain offenses related to human trafficking to be prosecuted, and mandate data reporting.

## Enforcement and Monitoring
Connecticut has a statewide oversight and monitoring body to combat human trafficking. The state requires mandatory data reporting to track and analyze trafficking cases. Specific laws allow for the waiver of crime victim compensation application limitations for minors who are victims of human trafficking and provide protections for victims' records.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's approach to combating human trafficking is multifaceted, incorporating both legislative measures and comprehensive training programs across various sectors to ensure a coordinated response.
