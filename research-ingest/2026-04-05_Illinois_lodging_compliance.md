### Summary and Key Points from SB1422

#### Purpose of the Bill:
SB1422 aims to address human trafficking in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops by requiring these businesses to provide their employees with training on recognizing and reporting human trafficking.

#### Definitions:
- **Department**: The Department of Human Services
- **Employee**: A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer**: A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking**: The deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty for labor, commercial sex, or other exploitation.
- **Lodging Establishment**: An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the 2017 North American Industry Classification System.
- **Restaurant**: Any business primarily engaged in the sale of ready-to-eat food with at least 51% of total sales (excluding liquor).
- **Truck Stop**: An establishment providing services to the trucking industry.

#### Training Requirements:
- **Training Content**: Minimum requirements for training include definitions of human trafficking, identifying individuals at risk, distinctions between labor and sex trafficking relevant to lodging establishments, and guidance on reporting and responding to instances of trafficking.
- **Frequency**: Employees must complete initial training within 6 months of employment in their role with the employer and every 2 years thereafter if they remain employed by the same employer.

#### Curricula:
- **Self-Created Programs**: Employers may use their own program or third-party programs that meet the minimum requirements.
- **Departmental Curriculum**: The Department must develop a standard curriculum, which must include all minimum requirements. This curriculum should be developed no later than July 1, 2020.

#### Penalties:
- **Civil Penalty**: If an employer does not comply with the training requirements within 30 days of receiving notice, the unit of local government or law enforcement agency may impose a civil penalty.
- **Enforcement by Government Agencies**: Units of local government and law enforcement agencies can monitor compliance and enforce the Act.

#### Effective Date:
The bill takes effect on January 1, 2026.

### Conclusion
SB1422 is designed to strengthen efforts against human trafficking in specific industries by ensuring that employees are adequately trained and prepared to recognize and report instances of trafficking. This legislation aims to prevent exploitation through better awareness and response mechanisms within the hospitality and transportation sectors.
