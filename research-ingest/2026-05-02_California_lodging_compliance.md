### Overview of Human Trafficking Laws and Requirements for Hotel/Motel Employees in California

**Legal Requirement:**
- **California State Law (SB 970):** Effective January 1, 2020, this law mandates that hotel and motel employers provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training regarding human trafficking awareness to employees likely to interact with victims. This requirement applies to employees already employed as of July 1, 2019, and to new hires within six months of their employment.

**Purpose:**
- The primary goal is to raise awareness among hotel and motel staff about the signs of human trafficking, potential risks, and how to respond appropriately. This includes recognizing indicators such as individuals who appear lost, disoriented, or in distress.

**Training Content:**
- Training should cover basic knowledge of human trafficking, including definitions, statistics, risk factors, and typical scenarios where victims may be encountered. It should also include information on the legal obligations of employers to report suspected trafficking and resources available for victims.

### Legal Framework

1. **Civil Rights Law:**
   - California's Human Trafficking Civil Rights Act protects individuals from discrimination based on their status as a victim or witness of human trafficking.

2. **Criminal Law:**
   - The State Attorney General can prosecute individuals involved in human trafficking, and victims may seek civil remedies such as restitution, injunctions, and emotional support.

### Implementation and Enforcement

1. **Training Program:**
   - Employers must develop a comprehensive training program that adheres to the requirements set forth by SB 970. This includes both initial training for existing employees and ongoing education for new hires.
   - Training can be provided through various methods such as in-person sessions, webinars, or online courses.

2. **Documentation:**
   - Employers must keep records of the training provided, including attendance sheets, course materials, and completion certificates. These records should be retained for at least three years.

3. **Public Awareness:**
   - Hotels and motels are required to post a model notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking about available hotline numbers for assistance. This notice must be posted in multiple languages as required by law.
   - The model notice includes information on how to recognize potential victims, what services are available, and where to report suspicious activity.

### Legal Compliance

1. **Penalties:**
   - Failure to comply with the training requirements can result in fines and legal penalties. Employers must ensure that their compliance is verified regularly to avoid non-compliance issues.
   
2. **Reporting Requirements:**
   - Employers are required to report any suspected human trafficking activities to the appropriate authorities, such as local law enforcement or the Department of Justice.

### Resources and Support

1. **Hotlines:**
   - Victims can seek assistance through various hotlines, including the National Human Trafficking Resource Center at 1-888-373-7888 or TEXT: Be Free.
   
2. **Legal Aid:**
   - Victims may also access legal aid to pursue civil remedies and ensure their rights are protected.

### Conclusion

The training requirement for hotel and motel employees regarding human trafficking awareness is a critical step in preventing and responding to human trafficking incidents. By providing mandatory training, employers can help protect vulnerable individuals and foster a safer environment for guests and employees alike. Compliance with this law not only adheres to ethical standards but also helps fulfill the state's duty to protect its citizens from modern-day slavery.
