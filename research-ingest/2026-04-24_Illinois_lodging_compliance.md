### Bill Summary: Human Trafficking Recognition Training for Employees in Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops

#### Purpose:
The bill aims to enhance the awareness and preparedness of employees at lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops to recognize and respond appropriately to instances of human trafficking. It mandates mandatory training for employees and outlines penalties for non-compliance.

#### Key Components:

1. **Definitions:**
   - "Department": Refers to the Department of Human Services.
   - "Employee": A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop with recurring interactions with the public.
   - "Employer": A person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
   - "Human trafficking": The deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty for various illegal purposes.

2. **Training Mandate:**
   - Starting June 1, 2020, employers must provide their employees with training in the recognition of human trafficking and reporting protocols within 6 months of employment and every 2 years thereafter.
   - Training duration should be at least 20 minutes.

3. **Curriculum Requirements:**
   - Employers may use their own training program or a third-party program that includes:
     - A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
     - Guidance on identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
     - Differences between labor and sex trafficking, as it pertains to the respective establishments (lodging, restaurant, truck stop).
     - Guidance on employees' roles in reporting and responding to human trafficking.

4. **Departmental Curriculum:**
   - The Department of Human Services is tasked with developing a standardized curriculum for approved training programs that all employers must follow if they do not provide their own training.
   - The curriculum must include the same components as those outlined above.
   - The Department may consult with the United States Department of Justice in developing this curriculum.

5. **Penalties:**
   - Local government units or law enforcement agencies can monitor and enforce compliance with the Act.
   - Upon discovery of a violation, employers are given 30 days to correct the issue; if not corrected, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action.
   - An employer found in violation is guilty of a business offense and faces fines up to $1,500 per offense.

#### Effective Date:
- The Act becomes effective on January 1, 2026.

### Conclusion:
This bill is designed to improve the safety and well-being of employees and guests by ensuring they are trained to recognize and address human trafficking. By mandating comprehensive training and setting clear penalties for non-compliance, the legislation aims to create a safer environment within lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
