## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at combating human trafficking, particularly sex trafficking. The state requires specific trainings for various professionals, including Child Protective Services staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital staff, hospitality industry workers, and others. Legislation includes laws that enhance protections for victims, mandate data reporting, and establish a statewide oversight body.

## Extracted Requirements

- Training required: 
  - DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement (POST Certified), emergency medical services (EMS), medical providers, hotels, motels, lodging businesses, child trafficking of boys, trafficking of LGBTQ/GNCT children, labor trafficking.
- Signage required: Hotels, motels, and lodging businesses must provide annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees.

## Summary of Legislation
Connecticut has enacted several laws to address human trafficking:
- **Prostitution Age**: A person must be eighteen years old or older to be guilty of prostitution.
- **Hotels, Motels Training**: Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees.
- **Documentation Requirements**: Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months.
- **Expanding Protections**: Expands the crime of human trafficking by broadening conditions when the victim is a minor, requires DPH to provide services to victims, expands court orders, allows wiretapping, and waives crime victim compensation applications.
- **Law Enforcement Training**: Provides training for law enforcement on human trafficking of minors.
- **Multidisciplinary Teams**: Creation of multidisciplinary teams to work with human trafficking cases.
- **Public Awareness**: Ongoing monitoring, public awareness campaigns, and mandatory data reporting.

## Summary of Trainings
Connecticut requires various professionals to undergo specific trainings:
- Child Protective Services staff: Annual trainings on human trafficking.
- Foster parents: Training on identifying and responding to signs of human trafficking.
- Service providers: Specific trainings related to child trafficking and labor trafficking.
- Schools: Mandatory trainings for teachers and staff on human trafficking prevention.
- Law enforcement (POST Certified): Comprehensive trainings including mandatory annual training.
- Emergency medical services (EMS): Trainings on identifying human trafficking victims in emergency situations.
- Medical providers: Training on recognizing signs of human trafficking and providing appropriate care.
- Hotels, motels, lodging businesses: Annual trainings on human trafficking awareness.

## Summary of Signage
Hotels, motels, and lodging businesses must provide annual trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees.

## Summary of Reporting Requirements
Mandatory data reporting is required for all cases involving human trafficking.
