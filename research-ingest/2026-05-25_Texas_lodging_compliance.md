## Summary

The Texas Legislature passed House Bill 390 (87th Regular Session), which requires the Office of the Attorney General (OAG) to approve training for the hospitality industry, with a focus on preventing and responding to human trafficking. The bill applies to commercial lodging establishments with 10 or more rooms but encourages training for all lodging establishments in Texas.

### Key Requirements:

1. **Annual Training Requirement**: Employees of commercial lodging establishments must receive annual human trafficking training.
2. **Training Content**: The training must include guidance on recognizing the signs of human trafficking, reporting suspected incidents, and responding to victims.
3. **Certification**: Employers must provide a certificate of completion to each employee who completes the training, which must include the date of the course and the title of the course.
4. **Records**: Operators of commercial lodging establishments must retain records of completed training certificates for at least five years and make these records available upon request by the OAG or relevant law enforcement agencies.

### Training Providers:

The approved training videos are provided by various organizations, including:

- Office of the Attorney General
- Etactics

- Harbor Alliance, Inc.
- Unbound Now

- Extended Stay America

- ECPAT-USA
- Refugee Services of Texas
- PBJ Learning
- Compliance Learning Solutions

### Signage Requirements:

Training must also include information on the human trafficking signage required to be displayed in commercial lodging establishments.

### Reporting Mechanisms:

The training includes guidance on contacting appropriate entities for reporting human trafficking, such as:
- The National Human Trafficking Hotline (888-373-7888 or text 233733)
- Appropriate local law enforcement agencies
- A telephone number designated by the attorney general

### Enforcement:

Non-compliance with the training requirements can result in penalties, including fines and legal action.

## Conclusion

The new requirement for human trafficking training in commercial lodging establishments is aimed at preventing human trafficking within the hospitality industry. By providing guidance on recognizing signs of trafficking, reporting incidents, and responding to victims, the training aims to empower employees to take proactive measures against human trafficking. This comprehensive approach includes certification and record-keeping requirements to ensure ongoing compliance and accountability.
