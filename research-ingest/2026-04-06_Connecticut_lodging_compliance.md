## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at addressing human trafficking, particularly in the context of sexual exploitation. The state mandates that hotels, motels, and similar lodging provide annual trafficking training to all employees, and it requires public agencies and local law enforcement to document trafficking cases and referrals. Legislation includes provisions for victim services, mandatory data reporting, and oversight by a statewide body.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging; public agencies and local law enforcement.
- **Training required:** Annual trafficking training for employees at hotels, motels, and similar lodging.
- **Signage required:** Not explicitly mentioned but implied through awareness campaigns and educational materials.
- **Reporting required:** Mandatory data reporting on trafficking cases and referrals to the Department of Public Health.
- **Enforcement agency:** Department of Public Health (DPH), local law enforcement agencies, Office of Victim Services (OVS).
- **Penalties:** Class C felony for commercial sexual exploitation of a minor; class C felony for knowingly purchasing advertising space for a commercial sex act involving minors.
- **Effective date:** Various laws have been enacted from 2010 to 2017.

## Summary of Legislation
Connecticut's human trafficking legislation includes several key acts:
- **Public Act 17-32 (2017)**: Expands conditions under which human trafficking can be committed when the victim is a minor.
- **Public Act 16-71 (2016)**: Requires annual trafficking training for employees at hotels, motels, and similar lodging.
- **Public Act 15-195 (2015)**: Expands protections for victims of human trafficking by providing them with services equal to those provided to certain sexual assault victims.
- **Public Act 14-186 (2014)**: Creates multidisciplinary teams to work with human trafficking cases and provides training to law enforcement on human trafficking.
- **Public Act 13-166 (2013)**: Aligns with the federal Trafficking Victims Protection Act, making it a class C felony if a person knew or reasonably should have known that another was younger than eighteen at the time of commercial sexual exploitation.
- **Public Act 12-141 (2012)**: Defines commercial sexual exploitation of a minor as a class C felony.
- **Public Act 11-180 (2011)**: Requires reporting of suspected abuse or neglect to the Department of Children and Families upon arrest for prostitution.
- **Public Act 10-115 (2010)**: Provides a safe harbor for exploited children by setting higher ages of criminal responsibility and presuming coercion in certain cases.

These laws collectively aim to strengthen protections, increase awareness, and ensure appropriate responses to human trafficking in Connecticut.
