# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at addressing human trafficking, particularly for sex exploitation. The state requires various groups, including DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other professionals, to undergo annual basic training on human trafficking. Additionally, the state provides specific trainings tailored for different sectors such as school settings, law enforcement, emergency medical services (EMS), hotels and motels, and various youth-focused programs.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, other professionals.
- **Training required:** Annual basic training on human trafficking for all specified groups. Specialized trainings include Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement (POST Certified), Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Emergency Medical Services (EMS), and others.
- **Significant legislation:** Public Acts 17-32, 16-71, 15-195, 14-186, 13-166, 12-141, 11-180, and 10-115 address human trafficking, expanding protections for victims and broadening conditions under which the crime is committed.

## Enforcement and Compliance
- **Data Reporting:** Ongoing monitoring of efforts to combat trafficking.
- **Public Awareness:** Legislation mandates public awareness and prevention initiatives.
- **Hotline Requirements:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide training and awareness campaigns annually and keep guest transactions and receipts for at least six months.

## Legal Protections
- **Forfeiture Properties:** Allows the Office of Victim Services to waive limitations on crime victim compensation applications for minors who are victims of human trafficking.
- **Affirmative Defense for Minors:** Provides an affirmative defense for minors in trafficking cases.
- **Vacating Records:** Expands the conditions under which a court must order the erasure of juvenile records.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's efforts to combat human trafficking are well-documented through robust legislation and ongoing training programs tailored for various sectors. The state is committed to providing comprehensive support and protections for victims, while also raising awareness and preventing trafficking incidents.
