### Overview

The California legislature has enacted several laws related to human trafficking, including requirements for hotels and motels to provide training and post notices. This analysis covers key points from multiple sources, ensuring an accurate understanding of the law.

### Key Legislation

#### SB 970 (2018)
- **Requirement**: By January 1, 2020, hotel and motel employers must provide at least 20 minutes of training to employees who interact with potential trafficking victims.
- **Exemption**: Employers who provided training on or before January 1, 2019, are not required to repeat the training.

### Training Requirements

#### Content of Training
The training must cover:
- Identifying signs and indicators of human trafficking
- The legal framework surrounding human trafficking
- Procedures for responding to suspected cases of human trafficking

#### Methods of Delivery
Training can be conducted through classroom instruction or other effective interactive methods.

### Posting Requirements

#### Model Notice
Hotel and motel owners are required to post a model notice in multiple languages:
- English (required)
- Spanish (required)
- Additional language(s) if the business is located in specific counties as mandated by federal law (Voting Rights Act).

The notice must provide:
- Telephone hotline numbers for seeking help or reporting unlawful activity
- A specified number that victims can text for services and support

### Penalties for Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with the posting requirements may result in fines, depending on the severity of non-compliance.

### Enforcement

#### California Attorney General's Office (AGO)
The AGO enforces human trafficking laws and provides resources for victims. They offer a model notice that businesses can use to comply with legal requirements.

#### National Human Trafficking Hotline
Victims can call or text for help:
- Call: 1-888-373-7888
- Text: Be Free (233-733)

### Key Points from Sources

#### Source 2 - California Coalition to Abolish Slavery and Trafficking (CAST)
- CAST provides contact information for additional resources, such as local hotlines and support services.

#### Source 3 - State of California Office of the Attorney General
- The Office offers additional resources and guidelines for compliance, including a model notice available on their website in multiple languages.

### Conclusion

The requirements for hotel and motel employees to receive training on human trafficking awareness are stringent and aim to protect vulnerable individuals. Compliance with these laws is essential not only to adhere to legal obligations but also to provide necessary support to potential victims of trafficking.
