The Act outlined in SB1422, effective January 1, 2026, establishes requirements for lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops in the state of Illinois regarding human trafficking recognition training for their employees. Here are the key points:

1. **Definitions**:
   - "Employee" includes anyone working in a reception area, performing housekeeping duties, helping customers move possessions, or transporting customers by vehicle.
   - "Employer" refers to any person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
   - "Human trafficking" is defined as depriving or violating personal liberty with the intent to obtain forced labor or services.

2. **Training Requirements**:
   - Starting June 1, 2020, employers must provide their employees with training in recognizing human trafficking and reporting observed incidents to appropriate authorities.
   - Training must be completed within 6 months of employment and every 2 years thereafter if still employed by the employer.
   - The training program should include:
     - A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children
     - Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking
     - Differences between labor and sex trafficking relevant to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops
     - Guidance on employee roles in reporting and responding to human trafficking incidents

3. **Curriculum Development**:
   - The Department of Human Services is required to develop a curriculum for approved human trafficking training programs.
   - This curriculum must include the same elements as described above for employers who do not administer their own training program.

4. **Penalties**:
   - A unit of local government or law enforcement agency can monitor and enforce compliance with this Act.
   - If a violation is discovered, the employer receives a reasonable notice of noncompliance, giving them 30 days to correct the issue before facing a civil penalty.
   - If the violation remains uncorrected after 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer. Violation of this Act constitutes a business offense, with fines not exceeding $1,500 per offense.

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and preparedness among employees in various service-oriented businesses to identify and respond appropriately to instances of human trafficking.
