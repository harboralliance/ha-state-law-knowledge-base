# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut provides various training programs for providers, foster parents, and communities on human trafficking. The state has enacted several public acts to combat human trafficking, including sex trafficking, with laws focusing on prevention, victim services, and penalties.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry**: Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging; emergency medical services; education.
- **Training required**: Annual basic training for all Child Protective Services staff; advanced training instead of the basic one; specialized trainings for foster parents, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry, other professionals, youth, and communities.
- **Signage required**: Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees annually.
- **Reporting required**: State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.; hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must keep guest transactions and receipts for at least 6 months.
- **Enforcement and Penalties**: Specific laws like the 2017 Public Act 17-32 (An Act Concerning Human Trafficking) mandate mandatory data reporting and require victims to receive services akin to those provided for sexual assault victims.

## Enforcement and Penalties
Connecticut has enacted multiple public acts aimed at combating human trafficking, including sex trafficking. These laws ensure that victims receive the necessary support, investigations are conducted by state attorneys and local law enforcement, and training is provided to various stakeholders. The state also mandates data reporting and requires hotels and motels to keep guest transactions and receipts for 6 months.

## Conclusion
Connecticut's approach to combating human trafficking involves comprehensive legislation, mandatory trainings, and stringent documentation requirements. These measures are designed to protect victims and ensure that the justice system can effectively address these crimes.
