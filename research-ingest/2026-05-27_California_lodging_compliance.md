## Summary

### Legal Requirements and Training for Hotel/Motel Employees Regarding Human Trafficking

#### **California State Laws:**
1. **SB 970 (Human Trafficking Prevention Act):** Effective January 1, 2020.
   - Requires hotel and motel employers to provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training and education regarding human trafficking awareness to each employee who is likely to interact or come into contact with victims of human trafficking. This includes employees employed as of July 1, 2019, and new employees within six months.
   - Employers are not required to provide additional training if they have already provided it by January 1, 2019.

#### **Notice Requirement:**
1. **Senate Bill 1193 (Human Trafficking Prevention Act):** Effective April 1, 2013.
   - Requires specified businesses, including hotels and motels, to post a notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking about telephone hotline numbers to seek help or report unlawful activity.

### Training Content
The required training must cover:
- Overview of human trafficking.
- Identification of potential signs of human trafficking.
- What to do if suspected human trafficking is observed.
- Contact information for reporting suspected trafficking activities.

### Documentation and Reporting
Employees are expected to document any observations of potential human trafficking and report such incidents to the appropriate authorities.

### Training Methods
Employers can deliver the training through various methods, including:
- In-person sessions.
- Online modules.
- Interactive workshops.

### Non-compliance Penalties
Failure to comply with the training requirement may result in penalties, which could include fines or other legal consequences.

### Model Notice and Language Requirements
The mandated notice is available on the California Department of Justice (DOJ) website. Each business must post the notice in English and Spanish. For businesses located in specific counties that require language assistance due to federal Voting Rights Act provisions, a third language posting may be necessary.

## Legal Compliance Resources
- **California Department of Justice:** Provides guidance, resources, and model notices for compliance.
- **National Human Trafficking Resource Center:** Offers hotline numbers and additional support resources.
- **State Bar of California:** Resources for legal professionals to ensure compliance with the law.

---

These requirements are designed to increase awareness among hotel and motel employees about human trafficking, enhance their ability to identify potential incidents, and facilitate reporting such occurrences. Compliance with these laws not only protects victims but also upholds public safety and ensures that businesses operate within legal boundaries.
