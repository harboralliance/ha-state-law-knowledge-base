### Summary of SB1422 - Human Trafficking Recognition Training for Employees in Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops

#### Purpose:
SB1422 aims to enhance awareness and action against human trafficking within the hospitality industry by mandating specific training for employees working at lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.

#### Key Provisions:

**Section 5: Definitions**
- **Department:** Department of Human Services.
- **Employee:** A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer:** A person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** The deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty for forced labor or services, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.

**Section 10: Human Trafficking Recognition Training**
- **Effective Date:** June 1, 2020
- Requirement: Employers must provide employees with training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols.
- Training Duration: At least 20 minutes.
- Training Frequency: Within 6 months of employment and every 2 years thereafter for those still employed.

**Section 15: Human Trafficking Recognition Training Curriculum**
- **Employer Option:** Employers can use their own program or a third-party program as long as it includes:
  - Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
  - Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
  - Difference between labor and sex trafficking relevant to lodging, restaurants, and truck stops.
  - Role of employees in reporting and responding to human trafficking instances.
- **Department Curriculum:** The Department is required to develop a standardized curriculum to be used by establishments without their own program. This includes the same elements as the employer option.

**Section 20: Penalties**
- **Government Enforcement:** Local governments or law enforcement agencies may monitor and enforce compliance with the Act.
- **Notice of Noncompliance:** Upon discovery, employers are given a reasonable notice to rectify violations; if not corrected within 30 days, they face potential civil penalties.
- **Civil Action:** The Attorney General or State’s Attorney can bring a civil action against employers who violate the Act.
- **Penalty:** Violation results in a business offense with fines up to $1,500 per offense.

**Section 99: Effective Date**
- The Act takes effect January 1, 2026.

### Significance:
SB1422 is crucial for preventing human trafficking within the hospitality industry. By mandating employee training, it empowers workers to recognize potential signs of trafficking and take appropriate actions to report and respond effectively. This legislation aims to protect vulnerable individuals in these sectors and contribute to overall public safety.
