## Summary
Connecticut has a robust set of legislation and training programs aimed at combating human trafficking, both for sex and labor. The state requires annual training for various groups including DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and other professionals. Legislation includes expanding the crime of human trafficking to include minors under 18 years old, providing services to victims, and establishing multidisciplinary teams to handle these cases.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Hotels, motels, inns, lodging businesses; medical providers; hospitality industry; law enforcement; DCF staff.
- **Training required:** Annual training for DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospital and medical staff, hotels, motels, and hospitality industry professionals.
- **Signage required:** Hotels, motels, inns, similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees annually.
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and cases referred for prosecution.
- **Records retention required:** Guest transactions and receipts must be kept for at least six months.
- **Legislation required:** Expanding the crime of human trafficking to include minors under 18 years old, providing services to victims, establishing multidisciplinary teams.

## Enforcement
- **Mandatory data reporting:** Ongoing monitoring of efforts to combat trafficking.
- **Statewide oversight and monitoring body:** Establishment of a statewide oversight and monitoring body.
