The legislation, commonly known as SB1422 or the Illinois Human Trafficking Prevention Act, aims to enhance awareness and response protocols for recognizing and addressing human trafficking within various establishments in Illinois. Here's a breakdown of the key provisions:

1. **Training Requirement**: Starting from June 1, 2020, employers must provide training on identifying signs of human trafficking and procedures for reporting such incidents to authorities.

2. **Employee Training Frequency**: Employees must complete initial training within six months of beginning their employment in roles involving public interactions and every two years thereafter if still employed by the same employer.

3. **Training Duration**: The training sessions must be at least 20 minutes long.

4. **Content of Training**:
   - Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
   - Identification of individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
   - Differences between labor and sex trafficking, particularly as it relates to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
   - Role of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

5. **Departmental Curriculum**: The Department shall develop a standardized curriculum that employers can use if they choose not to administer their own training program.

6. **Penalties for Non-Compliance**: Violations can lead to civil penalties, with a fine of up to $1,500 per offense if not corrected within 30 days after notice by the local government or law enforcement agency.

7. **Effective Date**: The Act takes effect January 1, 2026.

This legislation is part of broader efforts across the United States to combat human trafficking and protect vulnerable populations from exploitation. It requires specific training for workers in industries with high public interaction rates, such as lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops, which are seen as prime locations for human trafficking activities.
