### Overview

The question at hand is whether employers of motels and hotels are required to provide human trafficking awareness training to their employees. This requirement stems from a specific California law, SB 970, which was enacted in 2018.

### Analysis

#### Law Requirements

**SB 970:**
- **Effective Date:** January 1, 2020
- **Requirement:** Employers of hotels and motels must provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training regarding human trafficking awareness.
- **Target Employees:** Employees likely to interact with victims of human trafficking.

**SB 970 (External Source):**
- The law requires hotel and motel employers to provide specific training by January 1, 2020. It specifies that this training must be given to employees who are likely to come into contact with human trafficking victims.
- Employees already trained on or before January 1, 2019, do not need to receive additional training.

**California Attorney General's Office:**
- The Office of the Attorney General supports and enforces SB 970. They provide a model notice that businesses must post to inform the public about human trafficking resources.
- For hotels, motels, and bed and breakfast inns, these establishments are mandated to post the human trafficking hotline numbers on their premises.

#### Training Content

The training content is not explicitly detailed by the law but should include information on identifying signs of human trafficking, understanding the victims' experiences, and knowing how to report suspicious activities. Employers must ensure that the training is interactive and engaging to effectively communicate these critical points.

### Conclusion

Yes, employers of motels and hotels are required to provide human trafficking awareness training to their employees who are likely to interact with victims of human trafficking. This requirement is mandated by California's SB 970, which took effect on January 1, 2020. Employers must ensure that the training is comprehensive and interactive to effectively educate their staff on this critical issue.
