### Summary of HB 715

HB 715, also known as the "Human Trafficking Prevention Act," is a bill designed to address and prevent human trafficking within lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. The primary objectives include:

1. **Employee Training**: Starting June 1, 2020, employers (lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops) must provide their employees with training in the recognition of human trafficking and protocols for reporting such incidents. This training must be completed within six months of employment and every two years thereafter if the employee remains employed by the entity.

2. **Training Requirements**: The training must cover:
   - A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
   - Guidance on identifying individuals who are most at risk for human trafficking.
   - Differences between human trafficking for labor and for sex, as relevant to the workplace.
   - Roles of employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

3. **Curriculum Development**: The Department of Labor is tasked with developing a standardized curriculum for human trafficking recognition training. This curriculum must be made available to establishments that do not develop their own program.

4. **Penalties**: Violations of the Act can result in civil penalties ranging from $1,500 per offense by the Attorney General or State's Attorney if the violation is not corrected within 30 days of receiving notice.

5. **Effective Date**: The law takes effect on January 1, 2026.

### Key Terms and Definitions

- **Employee**: A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer**: A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking**: The deprivation or violation of personal liberty for forced labor or services, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.
- **Lodging Establishment**: An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the North American Industry Classification System code 721110.
- **Restaurant**: Any business primarily engaged in the sale of ready-to-eat food comprising at least 51% of total sales, excluding liquor sales.
- **Truck Stop**: An establishment providing services to the trucking industry.

### Implementation and Compliance

The bill aims to enhance awareness and preparedness among employees through mandatory training. This is crucial as early detection and reporting can significantly impact the prevention and resolution of human trafficking incidents. By ensuring that employers provide regular training, the state seeks to equip its workforce with the necessary skills to identify potential signs of trafficking and take appropriate action.

### Conclusion

HB 715 represents a proactive step in combating human trafficking within specific industries. Through mandatory employee training and a standardized curriculum, the bill aims to strengthen the ability of establishments to recognize and respond effectively to instances of human trafficking, ultimately contributing to the broader goal of preventing such exploitation.
