**Summary of Human Trafficking Recognition Training Mandate for Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops in Illinois**

The new Illinois law (P.A. 102-324) mandates human trafficking recognition training for employees at lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. Here are the key points:

### Definitions:
- **Department:** Department of Human Services
- **Employee:** Person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop with recurring public interactions.
- **Employer:** Person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** Deprivation or violation of personal liberty with intent to obtain forced labor or services.

### Training Requirements:
1. **Training Implementation:**
   - Beginning June 1, 2020, employers must provide training in human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols.
   - Employees must complete the training within 6 months of starting their job and every 2 years thereafter if still employed by the employer.

2. **Duration:** The training should be at least 20 minutes long.

### Curriculum:
- **Employer Responsibility:**
  - May use their own program or a third-party program, provided it includes:
    1. Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation.
    2. Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for trafficking.
    3. Difference between labor and sex-based trafficking as it relates to the establishment.
    4. Guidance on employee roles in reporting and responding to trafficking instances.

- **Department Responsibility:**
  - Develops a mandated curriculum if an employer does not administer their own program.
  - The curriculum must include the same elements as above, plus consultation with the U.S. Department of Justice (if applicable).
  - Publishes this curriculum by July 1, 2020.

### Penalties:
- **Local Government or Law Enforcement Agency:**
  - Can monitor compliance and provide a reasonable notice of noncompliance.
  
- **Violations:**
  - If the violation is not corrected within 30 days, an employer can be subject to a civil penalty of up to $1,500 per offense.

### Effective Date:
- The law takes effect on January 1, 2026.

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and response capabilities within these establishments to prevent and address human trafficking, ensuring the safety and well-being of all individuals involved.
