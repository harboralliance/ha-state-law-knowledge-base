### Human Trafficking Recognition Training Requirements in Illinois

#### Introduction
This legislation mandates that certain employees receive training on human trafficking recognition and protocols for reporting such incidents to the appropriate authorities. The law applies to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops with recurring interactions with the public.

#### Definitions
- **Employee:** A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
- **Employer:** A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking:** Deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell for commercial sex, or exploit in obscene matter.
- **Lodging Establishment:** An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under the North American Industry Classification System.
- **Restaurant:** A business primarily engaged in ready-to-eat food sales.
- **Truck Stop:** An establishment providing services to the trucking industry.

#### Training Requirements
Employees must receive training within 6 months of beginning employment and every 2 years thereafter if still employed. The training must be at least 20 minutes long and cover:
1. Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
2. Identification of individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
3. Differences between labor and sex trafficking related to lodging, restaurants, or truck stops.
4. Role of employees in reporting and responding to human trafficking.

#### Curriculum
Employers can use their own program or a third-party one meeting the minimum requirements outlined above. The Department of Labor must develop a model curriculum to be used by establishments not administering their own program. This curriculum must also cover all required topics.

#### Penalties
Local government units or law enforcement agencies may monitor and enforce compliance with this act. Upon discovery of a violation, they must issue a reasonable notice of noncompliance. If the violation is not corrected within 30 days, an employer can be fined up to $1,500 per offense.

#### Effective Date
This act takes effect on January 1, 2026.

### Conclusion
The legislation aims to enhance awareness and preparedness among employees in the hospitality industry regarding human trafficking. By requiring regular training, employers are better equipped to recognize signs of trafficking and take appropriate actions to ensure a safe and ethical workplace environment.
