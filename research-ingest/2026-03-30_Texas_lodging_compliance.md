The Texas Attorney General has enacted a new law, House Bill 390, which requires commercial lodging establishments with ten or more rooms to provide annual human trafficking training for their employees. The purpose of this law is to increase awareness and reporting of human trafficking in the hospitality industry.

To comply with this law, establishments must ensure that their employees receive training on identifying and responding to human trafficking. This includes recognizing red flags such as physical injuries, lack of control over personal documents, or working excessive hours. Employees should also be provided with contact information for appropriate entities to report human trafficking, including the National Human Trafficking Hotline.

In addition to providing annual training, establishments must also display human trafficking signage in their lodging establishments. This signage should inform guests about the risks of human trafficking and provide contact information for reporting suspected cases.

The law also protects employees who make good faith reports of suspected acts of human trafficking from retaliation or discrimination.

To help establishments comply with this law, the Attorney General has approved several training videos that are available on their website. These videos cover topics such as identifying and responding to human trafficking in the hospitality industry, recognizing the signs of human trafficking, and reporting suspected cases.

Businesses Ending Slavery and Trafficking also offers a training program specifically for commercial lodging establishments.

It is important for establishments to comply with this law in order to help prevent human trafficking and protect their employees and guests.
