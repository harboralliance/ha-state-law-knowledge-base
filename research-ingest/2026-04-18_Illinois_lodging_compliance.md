The provided text describes a new law in Illinois regarding human trafficking training for employees working at lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. This law will take effect on January 1, 2026.

Key Points:

1. Training Requirement:
   - Employers must provide training in human trafficking recognition to all employees who have recurring public interactions.
   - The training must cover identifying individuals at risk for trafficking, the difference between labor and sex trafficking relevant to these establishments, and employee roles in reporting and responding to trafficking incidents.

2. Existing Compliance Options:
   - Employers can use their own training program or a third-party program as long as it includes all required elements.
   
3. Department's Role:
   - The Department of Human Services is tasked with developing a curriculum for approved human trafficking training programs for establishments that don't develop their own.

4. Penalties:
   - Violations will result in reasonable notice to the employer.
   - If not corrected within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney can bring a civil action against the employer for up to $1,500 per offense.

This law aims to enhance employee awareness and response capabilities in identifying and addressing human trafficking incidents within these specific industries.
