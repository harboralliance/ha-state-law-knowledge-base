The provided text outlines the Illinois Human Trafficking Prevention Act, which was enacted in 2019 with the aim to prevent human trafficking and provide resources for employees working in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.

### Key Points:

1. **Definitions:**
   - **Department:** The Department of Human Services.
   - **Employee:** A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public.
   - **Employer:** A person or entity that operates a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
   - **Human Trafficking:** Deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell for commercial sex, or exploit in obscene matter.

2. **Training Requirement:**
   - **Effective June 1, 2020:** Employers must provide training on human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols.
   - **Frequency:** Employees must complete the training within 6 months of starting their role and every 2 years thereafter if still employed.
   - **Duration:** Training should be at least 20 minutes in duration.

3. **Training Curriculum:**
   - Employers can use their own or a third-party program that includes:
     - Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
     - Guidance on identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
     - Difference between labor and sex trafficking as it relates to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
     - Role of employees in reporting and responding to human trafficking instances.

4. **Penalties:**
   - A unit of local government or law enforcement agency may monitor compliance with the Act.
   - Upon discovery of a violation, an employer is given 30 days to rectify it; if not corrected, the Attorney General or State's Attorney can bring a civil action, with fines not exceeding $1,500 per offense.

5. **Effective Date:**
   - The act takes effect on January 1, 2026.

### Summary:
The Illinois Human Trafficking Prevention Act aims to enhance awareness and reporting of human trafficking among employees in the hospitality industry. It requires periodic training on recognizing and responding to potential cases of human trafficking within their employment settings. Failure to comply with these requirements can lead to penalties including fines from the state's legal authorities.
