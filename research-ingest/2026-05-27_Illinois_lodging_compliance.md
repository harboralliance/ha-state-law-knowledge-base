### Bill: **Human Trafficking Recognition Training for Employees in Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops**

#### **Title:** 
An Act establishing requirements for human trafficking recognition training for employees in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.

#### **Number:** 
SB 102 (2026)

---

### **Definitions:**
- **Department**: The Department of Human Services.
- **Employee**: A person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public, including those working in reception areas, performing housekeeping duties, helping customers move their possessions, or transporting customers by vehicle.
- **Employer**: A person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- **Human Trafficking**: The deprivation or violation of personal liberty of another with intent to obtain forced labor or services, procure or sell the individual for commercial sex, or exploit the individual in obscene matter. It includes any substantial and sustained restriction of another's liberty through fraud, deceit, coercion, violence, duress, menace, or threat of unlawful injury.
- **Lodging Establishment**: An establishment classified as a hotel or motel under code 721110 or as a casino hotel under code 721120 in the 2017 North American Industry Classification System.
- **Restaurant**: Any business primarily engaged in the sale of ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption comprising at least 51% of total sales, excluding the sale of liquor.
- **Truck Stop**: An establishment intended to provide services to the trucking industry, including selling fuel and food, providing showers, offering repair services, and ample room for long-haul truck drivers to park and rest.

---

### **Human Trafficking Recognition Training (Sec. 10):**
- Beginning June 1, 2020, employers in lodging establishments, restaurants, or truck stops must provide training on recognizing human trafficking and protocols for reporting it.
- Employees must complete this training within 6 months of beginning their role and every 2 years thereafter if still employed.
- Training must be at least 20 minutes long.

---

### **Human Trafficking Recognition Training Curriculum (Sec. 15):**
- Employers may use their own human trafficking training program or one provided by a third party, as long as it includes:
  - A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
  - Guidance on identifying individuals most at risk for human trafficking.
  - Differences between human trafficking for labor and sex within the context of lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
  - Guidance on employees' roles in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

- The Department must develop a curriculum for an approved program that includes all the above requirements. This curriculum must be published no later than July 1, 2020.

---

### **Penalties (Sec. 20):**
- Local government units or law enforcement agencies can monitor and enforce compliance with this Act.
- Upon discovery of a violation, these entities must provide reasonable notice to the employer. If the issue is not corrected within 30 days, they may file a civil action against the employer.
- Employers found in violation are guilty of a business offense and can be fined up to $1,500 per offense.

---

### **Effective Date (Sec. 99):**
- This Act takes effect on January 1, 2026.

---

### **Purpose:**
The purpose of this bill is to ensure that employees in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops receive training on recognizing human trafficking and understanding their roles in reporting such incidents. This initiative aims to enhance safety measures and help prevent the exploitation of individuals in these sectors.
