## Summary
Connecticut has enacted comprehensive legislation and provides training programs to combat human trafficking. The state requires annual basic training for Child Protective Services staff, with advanced training options available. Specific laws address commercial sexual exploitation of minors, human trafficking, and the protection of victims.

## Extracted Requirements
- Applicable industry: Child protective services, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospitality industry, medical staff.
- Training required: Annual basic training for Child Protective Services staff, advanced training options available. Specific training programs include Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement, etc.
- Signage required: Hotels, motels, and lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns annually.
- Reporting required: State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and prosecution cases. Mandatory data reporting.
- Enforcement agency: Office of Victim Services (OVS), Department of Children and Families (DCF).
- Penalties: Commercial sexual exploitation of a minor is a class C felony. Expanding the crime of human trafficking by broadening the conditions under which the crime is committed when the victim is a minor.
- Effective date: The laws mentioned above vary in implementation dates, with some taking effect as early as 2010.

## Summary of Legislation
Connecticut's anti-human trafficking legislation includes several key acts such as:
1. **Public Act 17-32 (2017)** and **Public Act 16-71 (2016)**: These laws address human trafficking, with specific provisions regarding the age at which a person can be guilty of prostitution and requirements for documentation.
2. **Public Act 15-195 (2015)**: This act expands protections for victims of human trafficking by broadening conditions under which crimes are committed when the victim is a minor and providing services to DCF.
3. **Public Act 14-186 (2014)**: This law requires DCF to provide training to law enforcement on human trafficking of minors and sets up multidisciplinary teams to work with human trafficking cases.
4. **Public Act 13-166 (2013)**: Aligns with the federal Trafficking Victims Protection Act (TVPA) and includes provisions for forfeiture properties, affirmative defense for minors, vacating records, public awareness, and prevention efforts.

## Training Programs
Connecticut offers various training programs to combat human trafficking, including:
- **Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut**: Provides essential knowledge on identifying and responding to child trafficking.
- **Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement**: Targets law enforcement officers with the tools they need to effectively identify, investigate, and prosecute human trafficking cases.

These trainings ensure that those working within various sectors have the necessary skills and awareness to recognize and address human trafficking issues.
