This bill amends the Human Trafficking Prevention Act to require certain employers, including lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops, to provide their employees with training in the recognition of human trafficking and protocols for reporting observed human trafficking to the appropriate authority. The training must be completed within six months of beginning employment in such a role and every two years thereafter if still employed by the employer.

The bill also establishes that an approved human trafficking training recognition program developed by the Department of Human Services shall include, at a minimum:

- A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children
- Guidance on how to identify individuals who are most at risk for human trafficking 
- The difference between human trafficking for purposes of labor and for purposes of sex as it relates to lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops
- Guidance on the role of lodging establishment, restaurant, and truck stop employees in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking

If an employer chooses not to administer its own human trafficking recognition program, they may use a third party program that meets these minimum requirements.

The bill establishes penalties for employers who fail to comply with the training requirement or who engage in human trafficking. These penalties include reasonable notice of noncompliance and potential fines up to $1,500 per offense if verified by the unit of local government or law enforcement agency regulating the employer.

Overall, this bill aims to increase awareness and education about human trafficking among employees in certain industries and provide them with tools to help identify and report instances of trafficking.
