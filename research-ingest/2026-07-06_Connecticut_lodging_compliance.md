## Summary
Connecticut has implemented various training programs and legislation to address human trafficking, including sex trafficking. The state requires all Child Protective Services staff to have annual basic training on human trafficking. Additionally, the state mandates that hotels, motels, and lodging businesses provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns annually.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging; child protection services
- **Training required:** Annual basic training for Child Protective Services staff; annual trafficking training for hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging
- **Signage required:** Not explicitly mentioned in the sources provided
- **Reporting required:** State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, trafficking cases referred for prosecution, etc.
- **Enforcement agency:** DCF (Department of Children and Families); local law enforcement; Office of Victim Services
- **Penalties:** Violations may result in fines or imprisonment depending on the severity of the offense as specified in the public acts
- **Effective date:** Varies by act; some laws effective since 2017, others since earlier years

## Conclusion
Connecticut's efforts to combat human trafficking include comprehensive training programs and stringent legislation. These measures aim to protect vulnerable populations and ensure the safety of victims and survivors.
