# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Sources
- https://portal.ct.gov/dcf/knowledge-base/articles/supports-for-children-and-families/hart---sex-trafficking/trainings-for-providers
- https://portal.ct.gov/dcf/knowledge-base/articles/supports-for-children-and-families/hart---sex-trafficking/human-and-sex-trafficking-legislation

## Summary
Connecticut has comprehensive legislation and training programs aimed at preventing and responding to human trafficking, including sex trafficking. Legislation such as the 2017 Public Act 17-32 requires mandatory human trafficking training for hotel staff and mandates the retention of guest transactions for six months. Training programs are available for various service providers, foster parents, schools, law enforcement, emergency medical services, and healthcare professionals.

## Extracted Requirements
- Applicable industry: Hotels, motels, inns, similar lodging; schools; law enforcement; emergency medical services; healthcare providers.
- Training required: Mandatory human trafficking training for hotel staff (every year); annual basic training for all Child Protective Services staff; advanced training instead of basic training for some CPSC staff.
- Signage required: Hotels, motels, inns must post awareness campaigns and trainings annually.
- Reporting required: State attorneys and local law enforcement must document training, investigations of missing children, trafficking referrals, and cases referred for prosecution. Hotels, motels must keep guest transactions and receipts for six months.
- Enforcement: The Department of Public Health (DPH) provides services to human trafficking victims similar to those provided to sexual assault victims under existing law.

## Effective Measures
1. **Comprehensive Legislation**: Multiple public acts have been enacted since 2013, expanding protections for victims and broadening the conditions under which human trafficking can be committed.
2. **Mandatory Training**: Hotels are required to provide annual training and awareness campaigns to all employees.
3. **Public Awareness**: Legislation mandates ongoing monitoring of efforts to combat trafficking and public awareness campaigns.

## Potential Areas for Improvement
1. **Enhanced Penalties**: Further enhancing penalties for human traffickers, including stricter fines and longer jail terms.
2. **Community Engagement**: Increasing community engagement and partnerships with local organizations to address the issue more comprehensively.
3. **Data Reporting**: Improving data reporting mechanisms to better track and analyze trafficking cases.

## Conclusion
Connecticut has robust laws and training programs in place to combat human trafficking, but there is always room for improvement to enhance effectiveness and provide better support to victims.
