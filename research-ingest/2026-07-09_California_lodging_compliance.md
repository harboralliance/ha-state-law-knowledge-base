Based on the information provided in the sources, here are key points about human trafficking laws and requirements for businesses in California:

- The California Human Trafficking Victim Protection Act requires certain businesses to post a notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking. 

- These businesses must post the notice in English and Spanish. For businesses located in specific counties with language assistance obligations under federal law, an additional third language is required.

- Hotels, motels, and bed & breakfasts are among the specified types of businesses that must post the notice starting April 1, 2013.

- The notice requires providing information on telephone hotline numbers for help and reporting trafficking. 

- Businesses have posting requirements, language requirements, and penalties for failure to comply with the notice obligations.

- Additional requirements were added later through SB 225 (requiring a text number) and AB 260 (adding hotels and motels).

So in summary, California requires certain businesses, including hotels, motels, and bed & breakfasts, to post notices about human trafficking victim assistance hotlines. The law specifies posting requirements by language and location, with penalties for non-compliance. This aims to inform the public and victims of resources available for support and reporting trafficking activities.
