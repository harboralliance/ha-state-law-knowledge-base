# State Research Capture

## State
Connecticut

## Summary
Connecticut has established comprehensive legislation and training programs to combat human trafficking, particularly focusing on sex trafficking. The state requires training for various groups such as DCF staff, foster parents, service providers, schools, law enforcement, hospitals and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals, and the general community. Legislation includes provisions for reporting requirements, mandatory data reporting, and penalties for offenders.

## Extracted Requirements
- **Applicable industry:** All service providers, DCF staff, foster parents, hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging businesses, schools, law enforcement, hospitals and medical staff, hospitality industry professionals.
- **Training required:** Introduction to Child Trafficking in Connecticut, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Law Enforcement (POST Certified), Emergency Medical Services (EMS) training, Child Trafficking Training for Medical Providers/Emergency Medicine, Introduction to Human Trafficking in Connecticut for Hotels, Motels, and Lodging, Child Trafficking of Boys, Trafficking of LGBQIA individuals.
- **Significant legislation:**
  - 2017 Public Act 17-32 (An Act Concerning Human Trafficking)
  - 2016 Public Act 16-71 (An Act Concerning Human Trafficking)
  - 2015 Public Act 15-195 (An Act Strengthening Protections for Victims of Human Trafficking)
  - 2014 Public Act 14-186 (An Act Concerning the Department of Children and Families and the Protection of Children)
  - 2013 Public Act 13-166 (An Act Concerning Sexual Exploitation and Trafficking in Persons)
  - 2012 Public Act 12-141 (An Act Concerning Commercial Sexual Exploitation of a Minor)
  - 2011 Public Act 11-180 (An Act Concerning Notification by the Deparment of Children and Families When a Youth is Arrested for Prostitution and Out-of-State Placements of Children and Youth)
  - 2010 Public Act 10-115 (An Act Providing a Safe Harbor for Exploited Children)

## Enforcement and Reporting
- **Mandatory reporting:** Hotels, motels, inns, and similar lodging must provide trafficking training and awareness campaigns to all employees annually.
- **Data reporting:** Mandatory data reporting to state agencies.
- **Penalties:** Penalties for offenders are not explicitly detailed in the sources provided.

## Significance
Connecticut's approach to human trafficking is robust, with a focus on comprehensive training and legislative measures. This ensures that various stakeholders are equipped to identify and respond to instances of human trafficking within their respective sectors.
