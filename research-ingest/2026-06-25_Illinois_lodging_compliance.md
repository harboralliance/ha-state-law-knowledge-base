The given text outlines a new legislation in Illinois regarding human trafficking recognition training for employees working in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops. Here's a summary of the key points:

1. **Definitions**:
   - "Department": Department of Human Services.
   - "Employee": Person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who interacts with the public regularly.
   - "Employer": Operator of a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
   - "Human trafficking": Deprivation or violation of personal liberty for forced labor, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.

2. **Training Requirement**:
   - Employers must provide training in human trafficking recognition and reporting protocols to their employees.
   - Training must be completed within 6 months after employment and every 2 years thereafter if still employed.
   - The training should be at least 20 minutes long.

3. **Curriculum**:
   - Employers can use their own or a third-party program that includes:
     1. Definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
     2. Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
     3. Difference between labor and sex trafficking in the context of lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops.
     4. Role of employees in reporting and responding to human trafficking instances.

   - The Department of Human Services is responsible for developing a standardized curriculum that all establishments must use if they don't have their own program.

4. **Penalties**:
   - Local government units or law enforcement agencies can monitor and enforce compliance.
   - If a violation isn't corrected within 30 days, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer.
   - Violation is considered a business offense with fines up to $1,500 per offense.

5. **Effective Date**:
   - The Act takes effect on January 1, 2026.

This legislation aims to enhance awareness and response capabilities among employees in potentially vulnerable industries regarding human trafficking, thereby contributing to the prevention of such exploitation.
