### Analysis and Synthesis

#### Overview:
The task involves synthesizing information from three different sources to provide a comprehensive understanding of human trafficking awareness training requirements for employees of motels and hotels, particularly in California. The synthesis will include the legal requirements, the enforcement mechanism, and available resources.

#### Key Points:

1. **Legal Requirement**:
   - **California State Law**: Under Senate Bill 970 (SB 970), hotel and motel employers are required to provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training regarding human trafficking awareness to employees who are likely to interact with victims of human trafficking.
   - **Effective Date**: The requirement becomes applicable by January 1, 2020.

2. **Exemptions**:
   - Employers who have provided the required training to an employee on or before January 1, 2019, are exempt from providing additional training.

3. **Enforcement and Penalties**:
   - The California Department of Justice is responsible for enforcing the compliance with this law.
   - There are penalties for non-compliance, including potential fines and legal actions against non-compliant employers.

4. **Support Resources**:
   - **National Human Trafficking Resource Center**: Provides a 24-hour, toll-free hotline (1-888-373-7888) to access help and services.
   - **California Coalition to Abolish Slavery and Trafficking (CAST)**: Offers additional resources and support.

5. **Model Notice**:
   - The state provides a model notice that must be posted by specified businesses, including hotels and motels.
   - The notice includes information on how victims can seek help and report unlawful activity.
   - Businesses are required to post the notice in English and Spanish, with additional languages required for specific counties.

#### Analysis:

1. **Requirement Analysis**:
   - The primary requirement under SB 970 is for hotel and motel employers to provide human trafficking awareness training to certain employees.
   - This training must cover essential information related to identifying and responding to human trafficking cases.
   - Employers are not required to retrain employees who received the initial training before January 1, 2019.

2. **Enforcement Mechanism**:
   - The California Department of Justice is tasked with enforcing compliance with SB 970.
   - Non-compliance could result in financial penalties and legal actions against employers.

3. **Support Infrastructure**:
   - Various organizations are available to provide support and resources for individuals experiencing human trafficking, including victims.
   - The National Human Trafficking Resource Center offers immediate assistance via a dedicated hotline.
   - California-specific organizations like CAST also provide additional support and resources.

4. **Model Notice Implementation**:
   - Specified businesses must post a model notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking about available hotlines.
   - The mandated notices are available in multiple languages to accommodate linguistic diversity across different counties.

#### Synthesis:

The requirement for hotel and motel employers to provide human trafficking awareness training is a critical step towards protecting vulnerable individuals from exploitation. As of January 1, 2020, these employers must ensure that certain employees receive the necessary training to identify and respond appropriately to cases of human trafficking.
To facilitate compliance with this law, various resources are available:
- The California Department of Justice for enforcement.
- National and state-specific hotlines and organizations providing immediate assistance and support.
- Multilingual model notices to ensure broad accessibility across different counties.

The implementation of SB 970 is an essential part of the broader effort to combat human trafficking in California. By ensuring that hotel and motel employees are equipped with the necessary knowledge, these businesses can play a crucial role in protecting victims and preventing further exploitation. The supportive infrastructure provided by various organizations underscores the commitment to addressing this pressing issue.
