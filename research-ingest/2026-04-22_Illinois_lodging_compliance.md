**Title: Amended Act Concerning Human Trafficking Recognition Training for Employees in Lodging Establishments, Restaurants, and Truck Stops**

**Act No.: 102-324**

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026

### **Section 5. Definitions**
In this Act:
- "Department" means the Department of Human Services.
- "Employee" means a person employed by a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop who has recurring interactions with the public, including those working in reception areas, performing housekeeping duties, helping customers move possessions, or transporting customers by vehicle.
- "Employer" means a person or entity operating a lodging establishment, restaurant, or truck stop.
- "Human trafficking" means the deprivation or violation of another's personal liberty for forced labor, commercial sex, or exploitation in obscene matter.
- "Lodging Establishment" means an establishment classified as a hotel or motel under code 721110 and a casino hotel under code 721120.
- "Restaurant" means any business primarily engaged in the sale of ready-to-eat food for immediate consumption, comprising at least 51% of total sales, excluding liquor sales.
- "Truck Stop" means an establishment intended to provide services to the trucking industry.

### **Section 10. Human Trafficking Recognition Training**
Beginning June 1, 2020:
- An employer (including lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops) shall provide its employees with training in human trafficking recognition and protocols for reporting observed human trafficking to the appropriate authority.
- Employees must complete the training within 6 months of beginning employment in such a role and every 2 years thereafter if still employed by the entity. The training shall be at least 20 minutes in duration.

### **Section 15. Human Trafficking Recognition Training Curriculum**
(a) An employer or lodging establishment may use its own human trafficking training program or that of a third party, as long as it includes:
- A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
- Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
- Differences between labor and sex trafficking, especially in relation to the industry.
- Guidance on employees' roles in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

(b) The Department shall develop a recognized curriculum for use by lodging establishments, restaurants, or truck stops not administering their own program. This curriculum must include:
- A definition of human trafficking and commercial exploitation of children.
- Guidance on identifying individuals at risk for human trafficking.
- Differences between labor and sex trafficking, especially in relation to the industry.
- Guidance on employees' roles in reporting and responding to instances of human trafficking.

The Department may consult with the U.S. Department of Justice regarding the recognition training program developed under this subsection.

### **Section 20. Penalties**
(a) A unit of local government or law enforcement agency may monitor and enforce compliance with this Act during its regulatory duties. Upon discovering a violation, they shall provide notice to the employer informing them that failure to rectify within 30 days will result in a civil penalty.
(b) If the violation is not remedied within the 30-day period, the Attorney General or State's Attorney may bring a civil action against the employer. An employer violating this Act is subject to fines of up to $1,500 for each offense.

### **Section 99. Effective Date**
This Act takes effect January 1, 2026.

---

**Summary:**

The Amended Act mandates that employees in lodging establishments, restaurants, and truck stops receive human trafficking recognition training within six months of beginning their role and every two years thereafter. Employers must comply with a set curriculum or develop their own, ensuring they understand the identification of risk factors, reporting obligations, and their role in responding to instances of human trafficking. The Department is tasked with developing a recognized curriculum and may consult with federal agencies for guidance.

Penalties are outlined for non-compliance, including civil penalties up to $1,500 per offense if the violation remains unresolved after 30 days of notice.
