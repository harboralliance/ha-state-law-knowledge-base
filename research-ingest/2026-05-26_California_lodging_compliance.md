## Summary of Requirements for Hotels and Motels in California Regarding Human Trafficking Training and Notices

### Overview:
In response to Senate Bill 970 (SB 970), effective January 1, 2020, hotel and motel employers in California are required to provide human trafficking awareness training to their employees. The training must cover the following key points:

1. **Awareness Training**: Employees must be trained on recognizing signs of human trafficking and the importance of reporting suspected cases.
2. **Required Training Content**:
   - Recognition of signs of human trafficking
   - Information about resources available for victims of human trafficking
   - The steps to take if an employee suspects or witnesses potential trafficking.

### Key Points from SB 970:

- **Training Requirement**: 
  - By January 1, 2020, hotel and motel employers must provide at least 20 minutes of classroom or other effective interactive training on human trafficking awareness.
  - The training must be provided to all employees who are likely to interact with or come into contact with victims of human trafficking.

- **Timing**:
  - Employees hired after July 1, 2019, must receive the training within six months of their employment.
  - Employers who have already provided training on or before January 1, 2020, are not required to provide additional training.

### Posting Requirements:

- **Posting Notice**: 
  - Hotels, motels, and bed and breakfast inns are required to post a notice informing the public and victims of human trafficking about the hotline numbers to seek help or report unlawful activity.

- **Language Requirements**:
  - The mandated notice must be posted in English and Spanish.
  - For businesses located in specific counties that are subject to federal language assistance provisions, an additional third language is required (e.g., Arabic, Cambodian, etc.).

### Penalties for Non-compliance:

Failure to comply with the posting requirements or to provide the necessary training can result in penalties. Each mandated business must post the notice in English and Spanish, with additional languages required in specific counties.

For more detailed information on human trafficking model notices and compliance requirements, refer to the [California Attorney General’s website](https://oag.ca.gov/human-trafficking/model-notice).

### Conclusion:
Compliance with SB 970 is crucial for hotel and motel employers in California. Providing adequate training and posting the required notice will help ensure that employees are aware of their roles in combating human trafficking and providing support to victims.
